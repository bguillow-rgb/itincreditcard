Credit Cards for DACA Recipients With an ITIN (2026)

By Editorial Staff · Secured & Starter Cards · Published August 13, 2026

Quick Answer

DACA recipients can get a U.S. credit card using either an ITIN or, if their EAD-based SSN is active, that SSN. Several major issuers including Capital One, Bank of America, and credit unions accept ITIN applications. Secured cards are the easiest first step, and unsecured options exist once you have a few months of history.

DACA recipients face a credit card question that search engines rarely answer well: do you apply with your ITIN, your EAD-linked SSN, or something else? Both paths work. Which one makes sense depends on your current DACA status, whether your Employment Authorization Document is active, and how much U.S. credit history you’ve had time to build. This guide covers both routes in plain terms.

Do DACA recipients qualify to apply for a credit card at all?

Yes, unambiguously. No federal law bars DACA recipients from holding a credit card. If you live in the U.S. and don’t have a Social Security number, you can apply for a credit card using an Individual Taxpayer Identification Number. DACA recipients with an active EAD can go one step further: that EAD makes you eligible to apply for an SSN at your local Social Security Administration office, which opens up a wider range of card products.

The practical result is two separate application paths. If your DACA is current and your SSN is active, use it. If your EAD has expired, your DACA renewal is pending, or you’ve never obtained an SSN, your ITIN works fine with the issuers covered here. You are not locked out either way. See the complete issuer list for a full breakdown of which banks explicitly accept ITINs.

Which card issuers actually accept an ITIN from DACA applicants?

A question we hear often: which major banks won’t quietly reject an ITIN application?

Bank of America accepts ITINs for credit card applications. Having a checking or savings account with them already improves your approval odds. Capital One is broadly immigrant-friendly and accepts ITIN applications for several cards, including the Capital One Platinum Secured Card. Citi accepts ITINs too, particularly if you already have a banking relationship there; the Citi Secured Mastercard is a solid option for ITIN holders starting to build credit.

Beyond the big banks, several fintech issuers have built their underwriting around thin-file and ITIN applicants. OpenSky is one of the few secured cards that openly approves applicants without an SSN. An ITIN works, there’s no credit check during the application, and that makes it accessible even if your U.S. credit history is thin or nonexistent.

For DACA recipients who want an unsecured card with no deposit, Zolve is an unsecured U.S. credit card with no annual fee that doesn’t require an SSN. You can apply with your passport or ITIN, and uniquely, you can apply and receive a virtual card before you’ve even arrived or settled in the U.S.

For the full bank-by-bank breakdown of application steps, see the bank-by-bank application guide.

One practical note: financial institutions collect identity data under Customer Identification Program rules established by Section 326 of the USA PATRIOT Act, which requires banks to verify the identity of anyone associated with an account. That’s why every issuer asks for government-issued ID even when they accept an ITIN. Bring your passport if your state ID doesn’t display your ITIN or visa status.

Should I apply with my ITIN or wait to get an SSN first?

Readers frequently ask: is it worth waiting for an SSN, or should I apply now with my ITIN?

The honest answer depends on your situation. If your DACA is current and your EAD is active, applying for an SSN at the Social Security Administration takes only a few weeks, and that SSN will immediately expand the number of cards you can qualify for. Most issuers require an SSN, so getting one is generally the faster path to more options.

If your DACA renewal is pending or your EAD has recently lapsed, waiting could mean months without a card and months without credit history accumulating. In that case, applying now with your ITIN is the smarter move. A secured card opened today starts reporting to the bureaus immediately. If you’re building a U.S. credit profile from scratch, the right first card can put you on a twelve-month path from zero history to a score in the mid-600s.

One step many Dreamers miss: once you do obtain an SSN after building history under an ITIN, contact each bureau to link the two numbers so your payment history carries over. The ITIN to SSN credit history transfer guide walks through exactly how to do that.

What is the easiest credit card to get with an ITIN as a DACA recipient?

For most DACA applicants starting from zero U.S. credit history, a no-credit-check secured card is the clearest path. OpenSky openly approves applicants with an ITIN, requires no credit check, lets you set your own credit limit with a refundable security deposit, and reports account activity to all three major credit bureaus every month with no hidden fees.

If you want to skip the deposit entirely, the Current Build Card requires no annual fee, no SSN, and no hard credit pull. It works from your Current spending account balance, making approvals effectively guaranteed. It’s best for ITIN holders and newcomers who want a true no-debt, no-risk credit builder.

For DACA recipients with at least a few months of existing U.S. credit history, the unsecured card options for ITIN holders page covers cards that get you off a deposit requirement. According to issuer data compiled by our editorial team, most secured ITIN cardholders are eligible to request an upgrade to an unsecured product after 12-24 months of on-time payments and low utilization.

Can I be denied because of my DACA or immigration status?

This is a sensitive question that deserves a direct answer. Issuers legally evaluate credit risk based on income, credit history, and debt, not immigration status as such. That said, the 2026 executive order directing regulators to treat immigration status as a factor in financial risk evaluation could affect credit card applications, particularly for ITIN-based applicants who may face increased scrutiny at some institutions.

In practice, DACA recipients with an active EAD and verifiable income are treated similarly to other thin-file applicants by most major issuers. Credit unions and community banks serving immigrant communities have been slower to change underwriting policies. See the 2026 executive order impact article for a full legal and practical breakdown.

If you are denied, you have options. Capital One’s pre-qualification tool lets you check your odds without affecting your credit, and the reconsideration line guide for ITIN holders explains how to call and make a human case for your application after an automated denial.

Can I add a family member with an ITIN as an authorized user on my card?

A question we hear often: many DACA cardholders want to help a spouse or parent build credit without putting them through a full application.

Some issuers, including American Express and Capital One, accept an ITIN instead of an SSN for authorized users. ITINs are issued by the IRS to people who aren’t eligible for a Social Security number, including noncitizen spouses.

Most issuers require the authorized user’s full legal name and date of birth. Many also ask for their SSN or ITIN, though some only require a name. Once added, most major issuers including Chase, American Express, Bank of America, and Capital One report authorized user activity to the bureaus. Smaller banks and credit unions may not, so confirm with your issuer before adding anyone.

The authorized user guide for ITIN holders covers the reporting nuances issuer by issuer, including which issuers send full payment history versus just account status to the bureaus.

How quickly can I build a usable credit score using an ITIN card?

Keep your reported balance under 10% of your credit limit, and after 6 months of clean history you’ll have a real FICO score. FICO requires 6 months of activity and at least one tradeline that has been open for 6 months.

From there, progress is mostly a function of consistency. Expect 40-80 point improvements within 12 months of steady, responsible card use. The credit-building guide for ITIN cardholders lays out a month-by-month action plan, including when to request a credit limit increase and when to apply for a second card.

Frequently asked questions

Can a DACA recipient apply for a credit card with an ITIN? Yes. Multiple issuers, including Capital One, Bank of America, and OpenSky, accept an ITIN in place of an SSN on credit card applications. DACA recipients with an active EAD can also obtain an SSN and use that instead, which opens up additional card options.

Do I need an SSN or can I use my ITIN to apply for a credit card as a DACA recipient? You can use either. If your DACA status is current and you have an EAD, you qualify for an SSN and many more cards become available. If your EAD has lapsed or you haven’t obtained an SSN yet, an ITIN still works with ITIN-friendly issuers like OpenSky, Capital One, and Bank of America.

What documents do DACA recipients typically need to apply for a credit card? You’ll typically need your ITIN or SSN, a valid photo ID (passport or state ID), proof of U.S. address (utility bill or bank statement), and proof of income (pay stubs, bank statements, or a prior-year tax return). Some issuers may also ask for your EAD card, though it’s not universally required for ITIN-based applications.

Will a credit card denial hurt my credit if I apply with an ITIN? A hard inquiry from a denial can lower your credit score by a few points temporarily, typically 5 points or less, and it stays on your report for two years. To cut down on unnecessary inquiries, use a pre-qualification tool before submitting a formal application. Capital One and several other ITIN-friendly issuers offer pre-qualification with no impact on your score.

Can I add a family member with an ITIN as an authorized user on my card? Yes. Issuers like Capital One and Bank of America accept an ITIN for authorized users. Adding a trusted family member lets them begin building a U.S. credit history while you stay the primary account holder responsible for the balance. Always confirm with your issuer that they report authorized user activity to all three bureaus before adding someone.
