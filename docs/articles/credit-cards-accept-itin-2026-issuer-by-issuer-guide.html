Credit Cards That Accept an ITIN (2026 Guide)

By Editorial Staff · Secured & Starter Cards · Published September 28, 2026

Advertiser disclosure:ITIN Credit Card is an independent resource. Some links on this page are from partners who compensate us, which may affect which products we feature and where. This does not change our editorial guidance and is never a cost to you. See our full disclosure.

Quick Answer

At least 10 issuers accept an ITIN for credit card applications in 2026, including Capital One, Citi, Chase, Wells Fargo, Bank of America, American Express, OpenSky, Self, Zolve, and Firstcard. Approval requirements, deposit minimums, and application channels vary significantly by issuer. Discover requires an SSN and does not accept an ITIN.

More ITIN holders get denied not because the card rejected their ITIN, but because they applied to the wrong issuer entirely.

Most “credit cards without SSN” lists are just copies of copies, and some of the most-recommended products don’t actually take an ITIN application at all. This guide exists to fix that. We checked every major issuer’s own documentation and live application flow, then built a ranked comparison so you can apply with confidence instead of guessing.

How we compiled this: every row in our tables was verified against the issuer’s own public documentation, application requirements page, and (where accessible) the live application flow. Approval requirements, deposit figures, and APR ranges were cross-checked against issuer sites and multiple independent review sources. Last verified: September 28, 2026.

Why does it matter which issuer I pick? Can’t I just apply anywhere?

A question we hear often: This is the most important strategic question any ITIN holder can ask before opening a browser tab.

To get a credit card without a Social Security number you need to verify your identity another way, typically with an Individual Taxpayer Identification Number. Issuers can also weigh income, credit history, employment status, and whether you already hold a checking or savings account with them. That last point is where most people go wrong.

ITIN acceptance and approval are two different things. Acceptance means the issuer takes the application, nothing more. Each issuer’s normal eligibility criteria (income, history, deposit) still apply, and policies change without notice.

The practical consequence: if you have zero U.S. credit history, applying to Wells Fargo or American Express will almost certainly result in a denial, even though both technically accept an ITIN. You need to match your application to your current credit profile. A brand-new ITIN holder with no U.S. history should start with Capital One Platinum Secured, OpenSky, Citi Secured, or a fintech like Zolve. After 12 to 18 months of on-time payments, rewards cards from Chase, Amex, and Wells Fargo become realistic targets.

One more thing: each credit card application creates a hard inquiry on your report. And issuers text a one-time code during the application, so a VoIP number (Google Voice, Skype) will fail that check. Use a real U.S. mobile number, or you risk an automatic rejection that has nothing to do with your creditworthiness.

What is the single easiest credit card to get with an ITIN and no U.S. history?

This one comes up a lot: Two cards consistently top the list for new ITIN holders with zero U.S. credit history: the Capital One Platinum Secured and the OpenSky Secured Visa. They take different approaches, and the right one depends on whether you mind paying an annual fee.

Capital One Platinum Secured is widely considered the most accessible mainstream option. Unlike secured cards that require your deposit to match your credit limit dollar-for-dollar, Capital One may approve you for a $200 credit line with a deposit of just $49, $99, or $200, depending on your creditworthiness. No annual fee, and Capital One reports secured accounts to all three major credit bureaus. Use their soft-check preapproval tool to gauge your odds before triggering a hard inquiry.

OpenSky Secured Visa takes a different angle: no credit check required. You make a refundable security deposit of $200 to $3,000, and that deposit sets your starting credit limit. Activity reports to all three bureaus. The tradeoff is a $35 annual fee and a 3% foreign transaction fee. You need a valid U.S. address and either an SSN or ITIN to apply. International students with an ITIN may qualify.

If avoiding an annual fee is the priority and you can fund a $200 deposit, Capital One is the better pick. If you want the highest possible certainty of approval regardless of cost, OpenSky’s no-credit-check path is hard to beat.

Issuer-by-issuer breakdown: what each bank actually requires

Capital One: the most ITIN-friendly major bank

Capital One accepts ITIN applications for several cards, including the Platinum Secured, and their prequalification tool lets you check your odds without affecting your credit.

Key terms on the Platinum Secured: $0 annual fee, a $49, $99, or $200 minimum refundable deposit, and a 28.99% variable APR. If approved, you have 35 days after approval to make the minimum deposit (Capital One states this deadline in its April 2, 2026 guidance). Capital One reviews secured accounts for credit limit increases and upgrade to unsecured status after responsible use, typically around the six-month mark.

Citi Secured Mastercard: no-fee with a caveat on graduation

The live application’s identity field is labeled “Social Security Number or ITIN (9-digit number required)” and Citi’s secured-card page lists “name, address and SSN or ITIN” among requirements. That is as explicit as any major bank gets.

Your security deposit ($200 to $2,500) goes into a collateral holding account for 18 months and earns no interest during that time. The APR is 26.74% variable, and there is no annual fee and no rewards. It is a solid, low-cost tradeline.

One critical caveat for ITIN holders: customers are reviewed for graduation starting at 9 months, then on a 12-month basis. To graduate, customers must pass credit graduation criteria and have a valid SSN on file. Without an SSN, Citi may keep you on the secured product indefinitely. Factor that in before applying.

Chase Freedom Rise: unsecured, but relationship-dependent

The Freedom Rise application page lists “Social Security Number (SSN) and/or Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN)” among requirements, making it one of the very few unsecured starter cards an ITIN holder can target without any deposit.

Chase does not require prior credit history to qualify. That said, having at least $250 in a Chase checking account within three days of submitting your application may improve your chances of approval. The card earns 1.5% cash back on purchases and carries no annual fee. You can potentially get a credit limit increase in as little as six months, and after 12 months Chase automatically evaluates you for an upgrade to the Freedom Unlimited.

The honest catch: the APR runs roughly 18.24% to 27.74% variable, and there is a 3% foreign transaction fee. Pay in full every month and the APR is irrelevant. Avoid using it abroad unless you have no alternative.

Bank of America: branch-only for ITIN holders

Bank of America’s online application stops ITIN holders with a message that reads, essentially, “If you have an ITIN, visit a financial center to continue.” This is not a rumor or a workaround. It is what BofA’s own application flow says.

The in-branch experience can vary. Going in person will work, but may require some persistence and speaking with more than one advisor before you find someone who can process the application.

The secured card takes a refundable deposit of $200 to $5,000 that sets your credit line, carries no annual fee, and reports monthly to all three bureaus. A possible deposit return or upgrade can happen after roughly 12 months. If you already have a BofA checking account, that relationship strengthens your application considerably.

Wells Fargo: ITIN accepted online, but good credit required

Wells Fargo accepts an ITIN in place of a Social Security number, and unlike Bank of America, you can apply online or in person. The practical limit for most ITIN holders: most Wells Fargo cards target applicants with a credit score of 670 or higher (700 and up recommended), which makes Wells Fargo a realistic option only after you have 12 to 24 months of U.S. credit history. Having a Wells Fargo bank account helps, though it is not required.

American Express: ITIN accepted, established U.S. file effectively required

American Express accepts an ITIN in its online application’s SSN field, and you can also apply by phone. But the reality is more nuanced. A U.S. credit file is still effectively required for approval. Most Amex cards require a FICO score of 670 or higher; premium cards like the Platinum and Gold typically want 700 or above.

There is one path designed specifically for immigrants: American Express accepts ITIN applications through direct application and Global Transfer paths. Direct ITIN approval is harder than with Capital One, but if you have non-U.S. Amex history, Global Transfer can be one of the fastest routes to an American Express card.

What about fintech options like Zolve, Self, and Firstcard?

Readers frequently ask: whether fintech cards are “real” credit cards or just prepaid workarounds. The answer depends on the product.

Zolve is the strongest fintech option for work visa holders and international students. You can apply with an SSN, ITIN, or a passport, and approval does not require a U.S. credit check. With an employer offer letter or pay stub, the approval limit can reach $3,000, higher than any other card accessible to new arrivals without U.S. credit. The ongoing APR is 19.50% to 39.50% variable, so Zolve is strictly a pay-in-full card. It reports to all three bureaus, so you start building history immediately.

Self Visa Credit Card works differently from anything else on this list. As of January 2025, you can fund a $100 minimum security deposit directly with a debit card or bank account and get the card right away, no loan required and no hard credit check. If you prefer the traditional path, you can open a Self Credit Builder Account first, make at least three on-time payments and reach $100 or more in savings progress, then use that balance to secure the Visa. The loan-first route builds an installment tradeline alongside the revolving card, which improves your credit mix faster than a card-only approach. The card carries a $0 annual fee for the first year, $25 after that, and a 27.49% variable APR.

Firstcard is the lowest-friction option for someone who does not yet have an ITIN. It pairs a secured credit-builder card with a checking account, runs no credit check, uses passport-based onboarding, and reports to all three bureaus. The whole process happens in the app. The cost is a subscription fee of roughly $72 to $144 per year depending on the plan, which is higher than most traditional secured card annual fees. Factor that in when comparing.

Which issuers do NOT accept an ITIN? (The “do not apply” list)

A question we hear often: Several widely recommended cards do not accept an ITIN at all, and applying to them wastes a hard inquiry.

Discover is the biggest one to avoid. Discover requires a Social Security number for all of its cards, no exceptions. This matters because the Discover it Secured appears on nearly every beginner card roundup. ITIN holders should remove it from consideration entirely.

HSBC is another issuer that moved from “unconfirmed” to SSN-required status in mid-2026. According to a re-verification completed August 30, 2026, HSBC was reclassified as SSN-required.

Chime’s Credit Builder, previously a popular fintech option, is also no longer available. Tilt (the WebBank-issued credit builder) replaced Petal, which no longer takes new applicants. The fintech landscape changes faster than the big-bank landscape, so always confirm before applying.

What do I actually need to have ready before I apply?

You can confirm your ITIN status and gather your documents in a single afternoon. Here is the practical checklist.

Get your documents ready: ITIN letter, foreign passport, U.S. mailing address, and a U.S. bank account for the deposit. For in-branch applications at Bank of America or Chase, bring physical copies of everything. For online applications, have digital scans ready.

Use a real U.S. mobile number. Issuers text a one-time verification code during the application, and a VoIP number (Google Voice, Skype) will fail that check. This is one of the most commonly overlooked rejection reasons and has nothing to do with your creditworthiness.

Having a checking or savings account at the same institution where you are applying dramatically improves your chances. This is especially true at Chase (Freedom Rise), Bank of America, and Wells Fargo, all of which reward existing banking relationships.

One more data point worth keeping in mind: the average APR on U.S. credit cards across all accounts was 21.00% in Q1 2026, according to the Federal Reserve G.19. The APRs on ITIN-accessible starter cards typically run higher (27% to 30%), so paying your full balance each month is not optional advice. It is the whole point of using these cards responsibly.

How quickly will I have a scoreable credit file after getting my first card?

Readers frequently ask: exactly when they will see an actual credit score appear after opening their first ITIN card.

You will typically have a scoreable credit file after six months of account activity. From there, consistent on-time payments and low utilization can get you to a score of 650 to 700 within 12 to 18 months.

That timeline matters because it maps directly to when more options open up. After 12 months of on-time payments, your score will typically be in the 680 to 720 range, and you can apply for rewards cards from Chase, Amex, and Wells Fargo. The key discipline is keeping your utilization low. See our article on credit utilization with an ITIN card for the exact percentages to target each month.

For ITIN holders who want to start building history before a score exists, the approach that works fastest is: open one secured card, use it for small recurring purchases (subscriptions, gas, groceries), pay in full every month, and do not apply for a second card until month seven or eight. At that point you will have both a score and a track record to present to the next issuer.

Does having a bank account with the issuer actually help approval?

Yes, consistently. The effect is clearest at three issuers.

At Chase, having at least $250 in a Chase checking account within three days of applying for the Freedom Rise improves your approval odds. Chase does not publish the exact magnitude of the boost, but it is frequently cited as the difference between an approval and a denial for thin-file applicants.

At Bank of America, an existing checking or savings account improves your chances considerably. This matters especially for ITIN holders because the branch-only application path gives the representative more discretion than an automated online system.

At Wells Fargo, the effect is subtler. A banking relationship is not required, but it can tip a borderline application toward approval for ITIN holders already above the 670 score threshold.

If you are starting from zero and want to use this strategy, open a basic checking account with Chase or Capital One first, let it sit for 30 to 60 days, then apply for the credit card. This also gives you a verified bank account to fund a secured deposit if needed.

What happens if my application is denied? (And when should I try again?)

Getting denied is not the end. It is information.

Lenders are legally required to send an adverse action letter within 30 days explaining the specific reason for denial. Common reasons for ITIN applicants include “unable to verify identity” (fix: apply in branch), “insufficient credit history” (fix: try a credit-builder product first to accumulate six months of history), or “address could not be verified” (fix: bring a lease agreement or utility bill).

Wait 30 to 60 days before reapplying after a denial. Each application creates a hard inquiry, and multiple inquiries in a short window signal financial stress to lenders. Apply for one card, use it for six months, then consider a second one.

If your denial reason was identity verification, the solution is almost always to apply in person at a branch rather than online. For Capital One and Citi, call the reconsideration line with additional documentation. The bottom line: our editorial recommendation by profile

There is no single best ITIN credit card. There is a best card for each stage of your credit journey.

If you have zero U.S. credit history: start with the Capital One Platinum Secured. The deposit starts as low as $49, there is no annual fee, and Capital One’s preapproval tool lets you check your odds without a hard inquiry. If Capital One does not preapprove you, try Citi Secured next. Both are fully online applications that accept an ITIN.

If you want guaranteed approval with no credit check: OpenSky Secured is your card. You set the credit limit with your refundable deposit, OpenSky reports to all three bureaus every month, and there are no hidden fees. The $35 annual fee is the price of certainty.

If you are a work visa holder or student who just arrived: Zolve is built for exactly your profile. You can apply with an SSN, ITIN, or a passport, and the approval process does not require a U.S. credit history. Just watch the APR range and pay in full.

If you already have 12 months of U.S. credit history: target the Chase Freedom Rise for a no-deposit unsecured card with 1.5% cash back, or look at Wells Fargo’s portfolio if your score has reached 670+. At that stage, rewards cards become viable and you no longer need a security deposit sitting idle.

Whatever card you choose, the single behavior that matters most is also the simplest: pay your statement balance in full by the due date every month. That one habit, maintained consistently, is what turns an ITIN credit card into a genuine financial foundation in the United States. Common questions

Can I get a credit card with just an ITIN and no U.S. credit history?

Yes. Capital One Platinum Secured, OpenSky Secured, and fintech options like Firstcard and Zolve all approve applicants with an ITIN and no prior U.S. credit history. Secured cards are the most reliable starting point because your deposit reduces the issuer's risk.

Does Discover accept an ITIN for credit card applications?

No. Discover requires a Social Security number for all its credit card products. ITIN holders cannot apply for the Discover it Secured or any other Discover card. Apply to Capital One or Citi instead.

Can I apply for a Bank of America credit card with an ITIN online?

No. Bank of America's online application stops ITIN holders and directs them to visit a financial center in person. You must apply at a branch. Having an existing Bank of America checking or savings account strengthens your application significantly.

Does Capital One accept an ITIN for credit card applications?

Yes. Capital One accepts an ITIN for both the Platinum Secured and Quicksilver Secured cards. You can check your approval odds using Capital One's preapproval tool without a hard inquiry, entering your ITIN in place of an SSN.

What credit score do I need to get a Chase Freedom Rise with an ITIN?

Chase Freedom Rise is designed for people with limited or no credit history, so no minimum credit score is required. Your approval odds improve significantly if you maintain at least $250 in a Chase checking account within three days of applying.

Does American Express accept an ITIN?

Yes. American Express accepts an ITIN in its online application's SSN field. However, Amex cards effectively require an established U.S. credit file for approval. ITIN holders with no U.S. history should start with a secured card and return to Amex after 12 to 18 months of reporting.

Which ITIN credit card has the lowest deposit requirement?

The Capital One Platinum Secured has the lowest traditional deposit, starting at just $49 for a $200 credit line for qualified applicants. Zolve and Firstcard require no traditional security deposit, though Zolve links your credit limit to your Zolve account balance.

Does the Citi Secured Mastercard accept an ITIN?

Yes. Citi's live application labels the identity field 'Social Security Number or ITIN' and Citi's own documentation lists an SSN or ITIN among application requirements. Note that Citi's graduation to an unsecured card requires a valid SSN on file, so ITIN holders may not be able to graduate automatically.

Can Wells Fargo approve a credit card application with an ITIN?

Yes. Wells Fargo accepts an ITIN in place of an SSN and allows online or in-branch applications. However, most Wells Fargo credit cards target applicants with a credit score of 670 or higher, making them better suited to ITIN holders who already have some U.S. credit history.

What documents do I need to apply for a credit card with an ITIN?

Most issuers require your ITIN letter, a valid government-issued photo ID (foreign passport is widely accepted), proof of a U.S. physical address, and proof of income. For in-branch applications at Bank of America, bring all documents physically. Also use a real U.S. mobile number, not a VoIP number, since issuers text verification codes during the application.
