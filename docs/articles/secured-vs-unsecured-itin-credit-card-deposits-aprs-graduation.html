Secured vs. Unsecured ITIN Credit Cards: Deposits & APRs

Quick Answer

ITIN holders can get both secured and unsecured credit cards in 2026. Secured cards require a $49-$300 refundable deposit and carry APRs of 23%-30%. Unsecured options like Chase Freedom Rise and Zolve skip the deposit entirely. Graduation from secured to unsecured typically takes 6-18 months of on-time payments.

Secured vs. unsecured: what does the difference actually mean for an ITIN holder?

A question we hear often: why does it matter whether a card is secured or unsecured if both build credit the same way?

The practical difference is not about how credit reporting works. Both card types report to the same three bureaus every month, and a payment made on a secured card counts exactly the same as one made on an unsecured card toward your FICO score. The real difference is about risk and access.

A secured card requires you to pre-fund a collateral deposit before you can spend. That deposit reduces the issuer’s exposure, which is why they will approve applicants who have no U.S. credit file at all. An unsecured card extends credit without any collateral, which means the issuer takes on more risk and therefore requires at least some evidence that you manage money responsibly, whether that is a bank relationship, a foreign credit history, or a verifiable income stream.

For most ITIN holders starting from zero, the secured route is the more realistic first step. The goal is to use that secured card for 6-18 months, then move into unsecured territory where higher limits, better rewards, and lower fees become available.

What deposit ranges should I expect from ITIN-accepting secured cards?

Deposit requirements vary more than most guides admit. Capital One Platinum Secured has an unusual partially-secured structure: you may qualify for a $200 credit limit with just a $49, $99, or $200 deposit, depending on your creditworthiness. That makes it the most accessible entry point when cash is tight.

At the other end, the Citi Secured Mastercard requires a deposit of $200 to $2,500, held in a collateral account for 18 months with no interest earned on it. That longer lock-up period is worth factoring in when you are deciding where to park your money. Citi’s upside is that you can size your deposit and therefore your credit limit up to $2,500, which matters for keeping your utilization ratio low.

OpenSky splits its lineup into two tiers. The standard OpenSky Secured Visa charges a $35 annual fee and requires a $200 minimum deposit. The Plus variant trades the annual fee for a higher minimum: the OpenSky Plus Secured Visa has a $0 annual fee and a $300 minimum security deposit.

Key takeaway: if you need to preserve cash, Capital One’s $49 entry deposit is the lowest in the ITIN-accepting market. If you want to avoid paying an annual fee long-term, OpenSky Plus or Citi are better choices.

How do the APRs compare across secured cards that accept an ITIN?

This one comes up a lot: secured cards are supposed to help you avoid debt, so does the APR really matter?

It matters more than most people think. The average APR on a secured credit card in early 2026 is 26.13%, several percentage points above the average APR on a rewards card at 23.66%. The right move is to pay your full balance every month and never let interest accumulate, but knowing the APR landscape helps you choose wisely if you ever slip.

That said, carrying any balance on a secured card is structurally counterproductive: you are paying interest on what is effectively your own money sitting in a collateral account.

If you have any credit history at all (including an ITIN with a few months of bureau reporting), Capital One’s $0-fee option with its higher APR may cost you less in total if you always pay in full.

Which secured cards for ITIN holders will actually graduate me to unsecured?

Readers frequently ask: is graduation guaranteed, or do some issuers keep you in secured status indefinitely?

Graduation is never guaranteed, but the timelines and mechanics differ significantly by issuer, and those differences matter for your long-term credit-building plan.

Capital One starts automatically reviewing your account after six months of responsible use. With continued on-time payments, you may get your deposit back and receive an upgrade to an unsecured version of the Quicksilver card. There is no set timeline, but consistent behavior typically leads to graduation within 12-18 months.

Discover (which generally requires an SSN rather than an ITIN, so this is relevant mainly if you later obtain one) runs a similar schedule, beginning automatic reviews at 7 months.

Citi takes a longer path. Citi reviews your account within 18 months to determine whether you are eligible for a deposit return. Critically, Citi requires a valid SSN on file before it will graduate its Secured Mastercard. ITIN-only holders can build credit with it but may not graduate until they later get an SSN. Many comparison guides leave this out.

OpenSky offers graduation by invitation. After roughly six months of on-time payments, eligible cardholders are invited to upgrade to the unsecured OpenSky Visa Gold. Upgrades are invite-only rather than open application, but the path is real: your deposit is returned when you graduate, and there is no fresh hard credit pull.

Wells Fargo Secured deserves a mention as a caution: as of 2026, the Wells Fargo Secured Credit Card has been discontinued and is no longer available to new applicants. If you see it listed elsewhere, skip it.

What unsecured credit cards accept an ITIN with little or no U.S. credit history?

The unsecured options for ITIN holders are fewer but real, and they are expanding in 2026.

Chase Freedom Rise is the strongest major-bank unsecured card for ITIN holders starting out. Applicants generally need to be 18 or older with a U.S. mailing address and an SSN or ITIN. Unlike secured cards, it requires no refundable security deposit. Your approval odds improve considerably when you already bank with Chase: having a Chase checking or savings account with a balance of at least $250 makes a real difference. The card carries a 18.24%-27.74% variable APR with no introductory rate on purchases or balance transfers.

Zolve is the leading fintech option for unsecured ITIN access. You can apply with an SSN, ITIN, or passport, and get approved without a credit check. Zolve’s main differentiator is offering an unsecured-style card even without an SSN or U.S. history, which is genuinely uncommon. The APR range is wide: approximately 19.50%-39.50% variable, with your exact rate set at approval. Zolve also offers a path to a higher limit over time: starting four months after opening your account, you may qualify to upgrade to the unsecured Zolve Credit Card, at which point your deposit is returned and your credit limit can go up to $5,000.

Capital One Platinum (unsecured) is worth considering once you have a thin but existing credit file. It is built for limited-credit profiles and accepts ITINs, making it a natural graduation target for Capital One Secured cardholders.

A question we hear often: should I start with secured and graduate, or skip straight to unsecured if I can qualify?

If you can qualify for an unsecured card from day one, skip the deposit and go unsecured. The credit-building mechanics are identical, you keep your cash liquid, and you avoid tracking a graduation timeline. The only reason to start secured is if unsecured approval is out of reach with your current profile. Of the top 10 credit card issuers, seven accept an ITIN, including Bank of America and Capital One. That is a wide enough playing field that you should check pre-qualification tools before assuming a secured card is your only option. Our guide to credit card pre-qualification with an ITIN walks through the exact soft-pull tools each issuer offers.

What actually triggers graduation, and how do I make it happen faster?

Graduation timelines are stated in months, but they are really driven by behavior. Every major issuer reviewing ITIN-holder accounts looks at the same core signals: payment history (did you pay on time, every time?), utilization (how much of your limit did you use?), and overall account tenure.

Using your secured card for small purchases and paying the full balance every month will move your score within 6 months. Keeping utilization below 30% of your credit limit, and ideally below 10%, accelerates the score improvement that triggers graduation reviews. Capital One’s own guidance states that responsible use, meaning on-time payments, can earn your deposit back and lead to an upgrade to an unsecured Platinum card.

Beyond the basics, two structural moves speed up the process:

  1. Increase your deposit (where allowed) to lower utilization. If you put $200 down and spend $150 a month, you are running 75% utilization. Increasing the deposit to $500 on the same spend drops utilization to 30%. Citi allows deposits between $200 and $2,500 in $100 increments. Capital One also lets you deposit more than the minimum, up to a maximum of $1,000, to raise your initial credit line.

  2. Add a second tradeline for credit mix. FICO scoring rewards borrowers who carry both installment and revolving accounts, and most ITIN holders start with neither. Self pairs a Credit Builder Account with a Visa card secured by the payments you have already made, and you can graduate to the Self Visa after three on-time payments and at least $100 saved. A second reporting account does not have to be another credit card.

One warning: applications create hard inquiries unless the issuer specifically uses a soft pull, so apply deliberately and space requests at least 90 days apart. Stacking multiple applications in a short window will suppress the score gains you are working to build.

Does it matter to credit bureaus whether my card is secured or unsecured?

Readers frequently ask: will bureaus treat my secured card as “less legitimate” than an unsecured card?

No. Both secured and unsecured cards report to all three credit bureaus, so your payment history contributes equally to your credit score regardless of which type you hold. Credit reporting agencies see the account, the limit, the balance, and the payment history. The “secured” flag is visible in your file, but FICO scoring does not penalize you for it.

What matters to lenders reviewing your profile is the length of account history, the consistency of on-time payments, and the utilization ratio. A 12-month secured card with zero late payments and 10% utilization is a stronger profile than a 4-month unsecured card with one missed payment.

One nuance: some lenders performing manual underwriting for larger products (mortgages, auto loans) may look at what types of credit accounts you hold. A secured-only file after 5+ years could raise questions in that context. The practical fix is the normal graduation path: move to unsecured at the 12-18 month mark so your file shows a mix of account types over time. Are there any hidden fees I should watch for on ITIN secured cards?

Watch out for cards that market themselves as ITIN-friendly but charge application fees, monthly maintenance fees, and a setup fee that eats your credit line before you even use the card. Also skip any offer that does not report to at least two of the three major bureaus. The whole point is building bureau-verified history.

The ITIN-accepting cards covered in this guide are all from reputable issuers, but it is worth running a quick check on any card before applying:

  • Annual fees: Capital One Platinum Secured and Citi Secured Mastercard charge $0. OpenSky Secured charges $35. OpenSky Plus charges $0.
  • Foreign transaction fees: Capital One charges none. OpenSky charges a 3% foreign transaction fee. Citi also charges a foreign transaction fee, which matters if you travel or send payments internationally.
  • No-interest grace period: All major issuers offer a standard grace period (typically 21-25 days). Paying in full before the due date means you owe zero interest, which makes the APR comparison largely academic for disciplined users.

What is the realistic credit score timeline once I start with a secured ITIN card?

This one comes up a lot: how long until I can qualify for a real rewards card?

Within a year of disciplined use, most ITIN applicants see a FICO score between 640 and 700, enough to qualify for a mainstream unsecured card or other credit products at a fair rate. That range is consistent with what issuers like Capital One and Chase target for their entry-level unsecured products.

The sequence most editorial teams recommend for ITIN holders starting from zero:

  1. Months 1-3: Open one secured card (Capital One Platinum Secured or Citi Secured, depending on how much deposit you can provide). Set autopay for the full statement balance.
  2. Months 3-6: Add a second tradeline for credit mix, such as the Self Credit Builder Account, which pairs a Credit Builder Account with a Visa card secured by the payments you have already made. You can graduate to the Self Visa after three on-time payments and at least $100 saved.
  3. Months 6-12: Capital One will begin automatic graduation reviews. Keep utilization below 10% in the two months before any anticipated graduation review.
  4. Months 12-18: Apply for Chase Freedom Rise or the unsecured Capital One Platinum. Once approved, keep the secured card open (do not close it) to preserve account age.
