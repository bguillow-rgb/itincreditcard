Starting your U.S. credit history from absolute zero is one of the most common financial challenges ITIN holders face.

Most traditional credit cards require a U.S. credit history, and if you just arrived from another country, you have none, even if you had excellent credit back home. Credit scores do not transfer between countries. The result is a circular problem: you cannot get credit because you have no history, and you cannot build history without a card.

This guide exists to break that loop. We compiled and verified the seven strongest starter cards available to ITIN holders in 2026, ranked by how quickly and reliably they build a real credit file from zero, with the concrete numbers (deposits, APRs, fees, upgrade timelines) that generic roundups leave out.

How we compiled this: We cross-referenced issuer application pages, third-party review data from WalletHub, CNBC Select, and FinanceBuzz, and issuer-published approval requirements, focusing exclusively on products that (1) explicitly accept an ITIN in place of an SSN, (2) report to all three major bureaus, and (3) are realistically attainable for someone with no prior U.S. credit file. Why does starting from zero matter so much for ITIN holders specifically?

Millions of U.S. residents file taxes with an ITIN rather than an SSN. Getting approved for a credit card with an ITIN used to feel nearly impossible, but the playing field has shifted. A handful of issuers now underwrite applicants using an ITIN, passport, or even visa documents, and a few newer products skip the credit check altogether.

The zero-history problem is especially acute because U.S. lenders cannot see your payment record from Mexico, India, Brazil, or anywhere else. Some lenders, including American Express, do partner with Nova Credit to import reports from countries like India, Canada, Mexico, Brazil, and the UK, which can help some newcomers bypass the zero-history hurdle. But not every applicant or country qualifies. For most ITIN holders, the fastest and most reliable route remains a domestic starter card that builds a fresh U.S. file from month one.

The timeline is shorter than most people expect. Once you open a secured credit card, it takes about 30-45 days for the account to be reported to the credit bureaus, and you can start building credit in as little as one to six months. That is a meaningful head start that compounds every month you wait.

What is the single best starter credit card for an ITIN holder with no U.S. credit history?

A question we hear often:

The Capital One Quicksilver Secured Cash Rewards Credit Card is our top-ranked pick for the majority of ITIN holders starting from zero, and it is not a close call. Here is why it earns that position over every other option on this list.

The Capital One Quicksilver Secured is one of the best credit cards for building credit because there is no annual fee, and cardholders earn meaningful rewards on purchases: 5% cash back on hotels and rental cars booked through Capital One Travel, and 1.5% cash back on all other purchases. You can get approved with an ITIN instead of an SSN, and it is available to people who have damaged or no credit.

The deposit minimum is $200, which becomes your starting credit limit. If you want a limit higher than $200, you can make an initial deposit of up to $3,000, depending on your creditworthiness. That flexibility matters because keeping your reported balance below 10-30% of your credit limit is one of the fastest ways to build your score. A $500 deposit gives you much more room to operate than a $200 one.

Every six months, Capital One automatically considers cardholders for a higher credit line with no additional deposit required. Once your credit has improved through responsible card use, you can upgrade to an unsecured Capital One Quicksilver Cash Rewards Credit Card and receive your security deposit back. That automated upgrade path is rare among starter cards and removes the guesswork of knowing when to apply for the next step. For anyone who wants to pre-check their approval odds without a hard inquiry, Capital One’s pre-qualification tool is an excellent first stop. See our guide on how to pre-qualify for a credit card with an ITIN for the step-by-step process.

What if I cannot pass a credit check? Is there a guaranteed-approval ITIN option?

Readers frequently ask:

Yes, and OpenSky is the clearest answer. The OpenSky Secured Credit Card is one of the few secured credit cards that openly approves applicants without an SSN and accepts an ITIN. There is no credit check during the application, which makes it accessible if your U.S. credit history is thin or nonexistent. You set the credit limit with your refundable security deposit, and OpenSky reports your account activity to all three major credit bureaus every month.

OpenSky reports that 2 out of 3 of its cardholders see an average increase of 47 points after six months. The original OpenSky Secured Visa carries a $35 annual fee, but the OpenSky Plus Secured Visa has no annual fee and requires a $300 minimum deposit instead. The original $35-fee Secured Visa has a 23.89% APR, the lowest of the three OpenSky card variants, making it the better choice if there is any chance you will carry a balance.

You need a valid U.S. address and either an SSN or an ITIN to apply. International students with an ITIN may qualify. The upgrade path is also real: after roughly six months of on-time payments, eligible cardholders are invited to upgrade to the unsecured OpenSky Visa Gold. Upgrades are by invitation rather than open application, but the deposit is returned when you graduate, and it does not require a fresh hard credit pull.

Is there a good unsecured ITIN starter card, so I do not have to tie up a deposit?

This one comes up a lot, and the honest answer is that unsecured options for true zero-history ITIN holders are narrow but real.

You can get the Chase Freedom Rise without an SSN, as long as you have an ITIN. Like most major issuers, Chase requires one of these forms of identification to apply for a credit card. The Freedom Rise is genuinely designed for new-to-credit customers: no minimum credit score is required, there is no annual fee, and it earns 1.5% cash back on all purchases.

The key to approval with no prior history is the Chase checking account strategy. Chase notes that applicants for the Freedom Rise could raise their approval odds by first opening a Chase checking or savings account with a minimum balance of $250. You can open that account and apply for the card on the same day. When applying online, select “Individual Tax ID number” under “Tax ID type” on the application form.

The tradeoff is that Chase does conduct a hard credit pull. If your file is completely empty, there is a real possibility of denial even with the checking account in place. In that scenario, start with the Capital One Quicksilver Secured or OpenSky for six months, then revisit the Freedom Rise once you have a thin but positive file.

What is the realistic credit score timeline starting from zero with an ITIN card?

This one comes up a lot:

The timeline is more predictable than most guides admit. Here is what the data shows.

Once you open a secured credit card, it takes about 30-45 days for the account to be reported to the credit bureaus, which is when your credit score actually starts building. VantageScore can produce a score after just one month of credit history, but most lenders use FICO, so the six-month threshold is what actually matters for getting approved for things.

Building a fair credit score (580-669) typically takes 4-6 months of on-time payments. A good score (670-739) usually takes 12-18 months of consistent behavior. Your results depend on whether you are starting from zero or have some existing credit history.

With consistent use of credit-builder tools and on-time payments, many newcomers reach a usable FICO score in three to six months. A strong score of 700 or higher typically takes 12-24 months. Running two or three tradelines at once can speed things up.

The single most powerful accelerator is keeping your reported balance low. Your issuer reports the balance on your statement closing date, not your payment due date. Paying your balance down before the closing date (not just before the due date) means the bureau sees a near-zero balance, which directly reduces your utilization rate and lifts your score faster.

What documents do I need to apply for a starter ITIN card?

All you need is a valid ITIN, a foreign passport, a U.S. mailing address, and a U.S. bank account to fund any security deposit. Some issuers also ask for proof of income, especially if you are under 21.

Practically, gather these before you start any application:

  • Your ITIN assignment letter from the IRS (the CP565 notice)
  • A valid foreign passport or government-issued photo ID
  • A U.S. physical mailing address (not a P.O. box, as most issuers reject those)
  • A U.S. bank or credit union account to fund the deposit via ACH transfer
  • Proof of income (pay stubs, bank statements, or a letter from your employer)

For online applications, use soft-check pre-qualification tools, like Capital One’s, to gauge approval odds without a hard inquiry. This matters especially if your file is empty: a hard inquiry on a completely blank file costs you a few points before you have even started accumulating positive history. How do I know if a card is actually building my credit, not just sitting in my wallet?

Readers frequently ask:

Three things confirm a card is actively building your U.S. credit file.

First, check that the card reports to all three bureaus (Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion). A card that reports only to one bureau is building one-third of the credit profile that lenders actually check.

Second, verify your file exists by pulling your free report at AnnualCreditReport.com after 60 days. If the account appears, the bureau has opened a file for you under your ITIN.

Third, check for a FICO score at around the six-month mark using a free score tool (Chase Credit Journey is available even to non-cardholders; Capital One CreditWise is another free option). Building a new history could take up to six months, and for a FICO score specifically, it takes a minimum of six months to see growth. If your score is not yet appearing at month six, it usually means the account is not being reported correctly, the ITIN on file does not match the bureau record, or the card only reports to one bureau. Call the issuer to confirm the ITIN on your account matches your IRS assignment letter exactly.

Should I open one card or two at the same time when starting from zero?

Our editorial stance: open one card first. Use it responsibly for six months. Then add a second card if your score warrants it.

Expect 6-12 months of consistent activity before your score is strong enough for more options. Trying to shortcut this process results in denials that can actually lower your score.

The one exception where two accounts from day one makes sense is the Self Visa path: pairing the Self Credit Builder Account (an installment loan) with the Self Visa (a revolving credit card) immediately gives you two tradeline types. The loan-first route builds an installment tradeline alongside the card, which boosts your credit mix faster than a card-only approach. Since both products are under the same issuer, there is only one hard pull and one relationship to manage.

Once you have 6-12 months of clean history and a score in the fair-to-good range, look at unsecured options. Our guides on unsecured credit cards for ITIN holders and how to upgrade from a secured to an unsecured card with an ITIN cover that next step in detail.

What common mistakes hurt ITIN holders in the first 12 months?

This one comes up a lot, and the mistakes are consistent.

Applying to the wrong card first. Some major cards that technically accept ITINs still steer ITIN-only applicants to in-branch verification, which creates delays and friction. The cards ranked above are verified to process ITIN applications smoothly.

Carrying a high balance relative to the credit limit. A $200 secured card with a $190 balance is running 95% utilization, which actively damages a new score. Keep reported balances below 10-30% of your limit. If your limit is $200, try to keep the statement balance at $20-$60.

Closing the secured card after graduating. Do not close your secured card after it is upgraded. An older account helps your credit score. Keep it open with occasional small purchases to show active use. The length of your oldest account is a scoring factor that only grows more valuable over time.

Missing a single payment. Payment history drives 35% of a FICO score. One missed payment in month three can undo months of positive history. Set up autopay for at least the minimum balance on day one.

Not checking for ITIN-matching errors. Your ITIN must be recorded identically on your credit card account and with all three bureaus. A single digit transposition is enough to create a split file, where your payments build history on a ghost record no lender can find. If a pre-qualification or score check returns an error, call the issuer to confirm the ITIN on file.
