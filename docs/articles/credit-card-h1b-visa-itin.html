Credit Cards for H-1B Visa Holders With an ITIN (2026)

Quick Answer

H-1B visa holders can get a U.S. credit card using an ITIN instead of an SSN. Capital One, Bank of America, and several credit unions explicitly accept ITINs. Secured cards are the easiest entry point, and most H-1B holders can reach a scoreable credit file within 6 months of opening their first account.

H-1B visa holders are in a surprisingly strong position when applying for a U.S. credit card. You have verifiable employer-sponsored income, a legal work authorization that many lenders view favorably, and the ability to use an ITIN in place of an SSN at a growing list of major issuers. The challenge is knowing which cards actually accept an ITIN and what documents to bring. This guide answers every question our editorial team hears from H-1B holders working through their first U.S. credit card application.

Can I actually get a credit card on an H-1B visa if I only have an ITIN and no SSN yet?

Yes, and it is easier than many people think. H-1B visa holders are legally allowed to apply for credit cards in the United States. The complication is that most traditional banks want a U.S. credit history and an SSN, neither of which you may have when you first arrive. Cards built for immigrants and newcomers exist, though, and your H-1B status can actually work in your favor with lenders who treat it as a sign of income stability.

Bank of America, Capital One, Citibank, Wells Fargo, and many credit unions accept an ITIN in place of an SSN for credit card applications. That covers several of the country’s largest card issuers, so you are not stuck with niche products. The catch is that not every product at these banks accepts an ITIN, which is exactly why the bank-by-bank breakdown below matters.

What documents does an H-1B holder actually need to apply?

A question we hear often: visa holders frequently assume they need an SSN before they can even start an application. You do not. Here is what most ITIN-accepting issuers actually ask for:

An ITIN may be accepted in place of an SSN. Proof of income, pay stubs or an employment letter, is often required. You also need a U.S. mailing address to receive the card. Beyond those basics, bring your ITIN letter from the IRS and a valid foreign passport.

One often-overlooked requirement: issuers text a one-time verification code during the application, and a VoIP number (Google Voice, Skype) will fail this check. A real U.S. SIM is necessary.

Keep all four items ready before you begin any application:

  1. IRS ITIN letter (or ITIN number from your tax return)
  2. Valid foreign passport
  3. Proof of U.S. address (lease, utility bill, or employer letter)
  4. Pay stub or employment verification letter

For H-1B holders who already have a relationship with a major bank, your best odds come from starting with a secured card at a bank where you already hold a checking or savings account.

Does my H-1B status itself affect my approval odds?

Readers frequently ask: does having an H-1B visa help or count against you compared to other ITIN holders?

H-1B status signals employer-sponsored income and a formal legal work status. Both are positives in an underwriter’s eyes. Some newer fintech companies are building products specifically for immigrants that use alternative data, employment history and visa status, instead of requiring a U.S. credit file. That benefits H-1B holders directly, because your employment letter and visa documents are exactly what these systems can evaluate.

That said, visa status alone does not override a missing credit file. The issuer’s system still needs a tax ID to pull or build a credit file, which is why having an ITIN ready before you apply, not after, is the single most important step. Verifiable U.S. income also increases credit line size and approval odds, so attach an offer letter or recent pay stubs to any application that accepts supporting documents.

One more thing worth knowing: if you eventually receive an SSN, your ITIN-built credit history can follow you. Contact each issuer and the credit bureaus to merge your history once you have an SSN. Your positive tradelines transfer to the SSN-based file, preserving the credit age you built as an ITIN holder. Every month of responsible card use now still counts later. More detail on that transition is in our guide on what happens to your credit cards when you get an SSN after using an ITIN.

Should I apply for a secured or unsecured card first as an H-1B holder?

For most newly arrived H-1B holders with no U.S. credit history, a secured card is the lower-risk starting point. Secured cards require a deposit that becomes your credit limit. Most will approve applicants with no U.S. credit history, just an SSN or ITIN, which makes them the most accessible option for someone new to the U.S. credit system.

If you would rather not tie up $200-$500 in a deposit, unsecured options do exist. Capital One’s Platinum card and Zolve both accept ITINs without requiring a deposit, though they may apply stricter income verification. A full side-by-side analysis is in our secured vs. unsecured ITIN credit card comparison.

Once you have held a secured card responsibly for 12-24 months, most issuers will upgrade you and return your deposit automatically. Our guide on how to upgrade from a secured to unsecured card with an ITIN walks through the exact steps.

How do I apply: online or in a branch?

A question we hear often: the right channel depends on the issuer.

Online applications run through automated underwriting that may auto-decline any applicant who enters an ITIN instead of a nine-digit SSN, even at banks that officially accept ITINs. Get your documents ready before you start: ITIN letter, foreign passport, U.S. mailing address, and a U.S. bank account for the deposit. Pre-qualify where you can, using soft-check tools like Capital One’s, to gauge approval odds without a hard inquiry.

In-branch applications let a banker manually flag your file and route it to a human underwriter if the automated system rejects you. Banks like Bank of America, Chase, and Wells Fargo handle ITIN applications better when you walk in with a full document packet. Our detailed breakdown of this decision is in applying online vs. in-branch with an ITIN.

How fast can an H-1B holder build a real credit score from zero?

You will typically have a scoreable credit file after six months of account activity. From there, consistent on-time payments and low utilization can get you to a score of 650-700 within 12-18 months.

Scores typically appear within 60-90 days of your first reported activity. To move faster, keep your utilization well below 30% of your credit limit and pay your statement balance in full each month.

The biggest mistake is applying for too many cards too quickly. Each hard inquiry may shave a few points off your score, and approval odds drop with each recent application. Stick to one or two applications every three months while you are in the building phase.

For a full playbook on using your card strategically once you have it, see how to build credit fast with an ITIN credit card.

What if my application gets denied?

A denial is not permanent. The OpenSky Secured Credit Card is one of the few secured cards that openly approves applicants without an SSN using an ITIN, with no credit check during the application, which makes it a workable fallback if a major bank turns you down and you need to start building your file somewhere.

You can also call the issuer’s reconsideration line and ask a human to review your file. This works particularly well when your denial comes from a thin file rather than negative marks. Our full guide on using the credit card reconsideration line with an ITIN includes the exact scripts that work best.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can an H-1B visa holder get a credit card without an SSN? Yes. Several issuers, including Capital One, Bank of America, Citibank, and credit unions, accept an ITIN in place of an SSN on credit card applications. Secured cards and fintech cards like OpenSky and Zolve are the easiest to get approved for with only an ITIN.

Does my H-1B status help or hurt my credit card application? It can help. Some lenders view an H-1B as proof of stable, employer-sponsored income and legal work authorization, which is a positive underwriting signal. Pairing that with a U.S. bank account or a deposit improves your approval odds significantly.

What documents do I need to apply for a credit card as an H-1B holder with an ITIN? You typically need your ITIN letter from the IRS, a valid foreign passport, proof of U.S. address (lease or utility bill), and proof of income such as a pay stub or employment letter. Some issuers also require a real U.S. mobile number, not a VoIP number, for identity verification.

Should I apply online or in a branch as an H-1B visa holder? In-branch applications often have higher approval rates for ITIN holders because a banker can manually verify your documents and escalate your file if the online system auto-declines you. Online applications work well at issuers with clear ITIN-friendly policies, like Capital One.

How long until I get a usable credit score after opening my first card on an H-1B visa? You typically have a scoreable credit file after six months of reported account activity. Consistent on-time payments and keeping utilization below 30% can bring you to a score of 650-700 within 12-18 months, according to credit bureau reporting timelines.
