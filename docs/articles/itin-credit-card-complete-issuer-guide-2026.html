Which issuers accept an ITIN, and which still say no?

A question we hear often: “I thought most big banks accepted an ITIN, but I keep getting rejected. Which ones actually work?”

At least seven of the top ten U.S. credit card issuers accept an ITIN in place of an SSN, including Capital One, Chase, American Express, Bank of America, Wells Fargo, and Citi.

The details matter, though. Some issuers only accept ITINs for specific cards, some require an existing banking relationship, and a few route ITIN applicants through an in-branch process rather than a standard online form.

Discover is the largest major issuer that still requires a Social Security number. According to Bankrate’s issuer table, Barclays, Synchrony, and TD Bank (for most unsecured cards) also still require an SSN, though TD Bank accepts ITIN for select secured cards.

I have zero U.S. credit history. Where do I even start?

This one comes up a lot: applying for your very first card is the hardest step because virtually every score-based card requires a history you do not yet have.

For applicants with no U.S. credit file at all, two paths stand out.

The first is OpenSky. It is the most accessible option because it requires no credit check, only a $200 minimum refundable deposit and a valid ITIN. The approval rate across OpenSky’s three secured cards sits at 89%, according to the company. That figure is backed by issuer-reported data: based on Q1 2025, the OpenSky Secured Visa average approval rate is 86.14%. OpenSky also offers two fee variations: the original card at $35/year with a lower 23.89% APR, or the Plus Secured at $0/year with a $300 minimum deposit. OpenSky accepts money orders and other payment methods, which opens the door for applicants who are unbanked or working on establishing banking access.

The second is Capital One Platinum Secured. You can get approved with a deposit as low as $49, $99, or $200 depending on your creditworthiness, and your credit limit starts at $200 regardless of which deposit amount you pay. Capital One will begin automatically reviewing your account after six months to see if you qualify for a higher credit line with no additional deposit.

A third option worth knowing: Zolve Classic, an unsecured card that requires no deposit at all. Zolve uses employment-based and income-based underwriting rather than credit history, making it a reasonable starting point for immigrants relocating on a student or work visa who want to begin credit building before they arrive. Country eligibility is not universal; Zolve accepts applicants from select countries and may not approve all nationalities.

What APRs will I actually be paying, and how do they compare?

A question we hear often: “The fees look fine, but what about the interest rate? Are ITIN cards penalizing me because I’m new?”

APRs on ITIN-accessible cards range widely depending on whether the card is secured or unsecured, and whether it targets thin-file or established borrowers.

OpenSky cardholders see an average increase of 47 points after 6 months of use, which means most people move up the rate ladder faster than they expect. In the short term, though, rates matter if you ever carry a balance. Here is the realistic picture by card type:

Secured ITIN cards run 23%-29% APR. The OpenSky Secured Visa’s 23.89% variable APR is actually lower than average for a secured card. The Capital One Platinum Secured and Quicksilver Secured both sit at around 29.24%-29.99% variable. The Capital One Quicksilver Secured Card’s interest rate is well above the average for a secured credit card offer (21.95%), per WalletHub’s research, but carrying a balance on a secured card is a bad idea regardless of the rate.

Unsecured fintech ITIN cards run 29%-35% APR. The ongoing APR for the Tilt Motion Visa is 29.24%-34.24% variable. The Zolve Classic carries a 19.50%-39.50% variable APR, a wide range; your actual rate depends heavily on the income and employment profile you submit.

Established-credit unsecured ITIN cards run 19%-28% APR. Once you build enough history to qualify, the Wells Fargo Active Cash runs around 20.24% variable for well-qualified applicants, and the Chase Freedom Rise is broadly comparable. That rate compression is real and worth the 12-18 months of secured card work to get there.

The core rule across all these cards: pay the statement balance in full every month. Credit building works through payment history, not through carrying balances. Every card on this list charges enough interest that a running balance erases the value of any rewards earned.

Which ITIN cards graduate to unsecured, and how long does that take?

This is one of the most practically important questions for ITIN holders, because graduating to unsecured means getting your deposit back, freeing up that cash, and keeping your credit history intact without closing an account.

With responsible use, holders of the Capital One Quicksilver Secured may get their deposit returned as a statement credit, effectively turning their secured card into a regular unsecured one. Even before that, cardholders can be considered for an automatic credit line increase after at least six months.

Capital One does not publish a fixed upgrade timeline the way Discover does. Discover commits to a review at 7 months. In practice, most Capital One secured cardholders with on-time payments and low utilization see upgrade offers between 12-18 months.

For Tilt Motion: eligible cardmembers can be reviewed for a credit limit increase as early as 4 months. According to Tilt’s own data, 66.6% of customers approved through their website had been denied credit elsewhere in the past 6 months, and 80% of customers receive a limit increase in their first 6 months.

Bank of America and Citi typically require you to apply for a new unsecured card rather than converting the existing one. Plan your timeline so you are not leaving a deposit locked up for years when you could have it back.

What about unsecured ITIN cards with no deposit required?

This one comes up a lot: “I do not want to tie up $200. Is there any way to get a real credit card with an ITIN without putting down a deposit?”

Yes, under the right conditions. There are two realistic paths.

Path 1: Zolve Classic. The Zolve Classic Credit Card works well for international students and H-1B and L-1 visa holders. If you lack a Social Security number, you can qualify with alternative documentation like offer letters, pay slips, or other eligible documents. There is no security deposit or credit history requirement. The card has a $0 annual fee, a rewards rate of 1%-10% cash back, and a variable APR of 19.50%-39.50%. Zolve also advertises limits up to $15,000 based on income and employment data, according to verified data from WalletGrower.

Path 2: Tilt Motion Visa. You can pre-qualify in minutes with no impact to your credit score, no security deposit, and no credit history required. Tilt acquired the Petal portfolio of credit cards in 2024, and Tilt’s three new credit cards have replaced Petal’s three cards. As of May 2026, Petal 1 and Petal 2 are no longer accepting new applications; new applicants are offered Tilt cards instead, while existing Petal cardholders keep their accounts and benefits. The Tilt Motion accepts applicants with no or limited U.S. credit history using cash-flow underwriting.

One honest caveat: if you have no U.S. credit history, an unsecured card is very unlikely from any major bank issuer. Zolve and Tilt are the practical exceptions, and both carry APRs well above average. Neither is a card you want to carry a balance on. Use them as charge-and-pay-in-full instruments to build the file you need to qualify for better unsecured cards within 12-18 months.

How do Chase, Amex, and Wells Fargo handle ITIN applicants differently from Capital One?

Readers frequently ask: “I have been building credit for about a year now and want to move to a major bank card. Do Chase and Amex actually work with ITINs, or is that only on paper?”

They do work, but each major bank has its own quirks.

Chase: applicants generally need to be 18 or older with a U.S. mailing address and an SSN or ITIN to get a Chase credit card. The best personal Chase card for ITIN applicants is the Freedom Rise, which has a $0 annual fee and is open to applicants with no credit history. Opening or having a Chase personal checking or savings account with at least a $250 balance improves your approval odds. For Chase Freedom Unlimited, you generally need a score of 670 or higher. For premium cards, Chase Sapphire Preferred and Amex Gold typically require a score of 720 or higher.

American Express: Amex accepts ITINs across its consumer card lineup. You enter your ITIN in the SSN field, and the application’s own help text confirms this is accepted. The Blue Cash Everyday and Blue Cash Preferred are the most realistic ITIN targets for applicants with 670+ scores. The Amex Gold and Platinum sit in the 720+ tier.

Wells Fargo: the online application uses a combined “SSN/ITIN” field, making it one of the cleaner online application experiences for ITIN holders. The Active Cash card earns a flat 2% cash back on all purchases with no annual fee, making it one of the best straightforward rewards cards once your score clears 670. Note the 3% foreign transaction fee on most Wells Fargo cards if you travel internationally.

Bank of America: you must be a U.S. resident to apply. You may apply online or at a local branch if you have a Social Security number, but ITIN holders must apply in person only. Opening a Bank of America checking account first, even a basic one, meaningfully changes your approval odds for their credit cards.

For a deep dive into any single issuer, see our dedicated guides: Capital One with an ITIN, Chase with an ITIN, American Express with an ITIN, Citi with an ITIN, and Wells Fargo with an ITIN.

What documents do I need to apply for a credit card with an ITIN?

Before you sit down to apply, gather these items:

  1. Your ITIN. You will enter this in the tax identification or SSN field. Have your IRS CP565 notice (the ITIN assignment letter) or W-7 approval document available. If your ITIN has expired, renew it before applying. See our guide on what happens if your ITIN expires.
  2. A government-issued photo ID. A foreign passport is the most universally accepted. Some issuers (notably Amex and Bank of America) also accept a national ID card.
  3. A U.S. mailing address. A valid street address, not a P.O. box.
  4. Proof of income. Pay stubs, bank statements, or an employer offer letter. Secured cards are more flexible here; unsecured cards from major banks look more carefully at income.
  5. A U.S. bank account (for secured cards). Most secured cards fund the deposit via ACH from a checking account. OpenSky is the exception: it accepts money orders and other payment methods, which opens the door for applicants who are unbanked or working on establishing banking access.

For a full walkthrough of the application process, including how to enter your ITIN on each issuer’s form, see how to apply for a credit card with an ITIN.

Should I apply online or go to a branch with an ITIN?

A question we hear often: “I keep hearing I might need to go to a branch. Is that really true, or can I just apply online?”

For most ITIN-accepting issuers, you can apply fully online. Capital One, Chase, OpenSky, Zolve, Tilt, Citi, and Wells Fargo all have online application fields that explicitly accept an ITIN. Wells Fargo uses a combined “SSN/ITIN” field directly on the online application. Chase’s Freedom Rise application checklist explicitly reads “SSN and/or ITIN.”

The exception is Bank of America. ITIN holders must apply in person at a branch financial center; online applications only work with an SSN. This is not a barrier if you have a BofA branch nearby, but an in-person visit is required regardless.

For a detailed comparison of online versus branch application experiences across every major issuer, see our online vs. in-branch application guide.
