Why is it so hard to get a first credit card with an ITIN?

A question we hear often:

Most traditional credit cards require a U.S. credit history. If you just arrived from another country, you have none, even if you had excellent credit back home. Credit scores don’t transfer between countries. Your payment record in Mexico, India, or China means nothing to U.S. lenders.

The second barrier is the identifier itself. An ITIN is a nine-digit IRS tax number for people who file U.S. taxes but are not eligible for an SSN. Not every issuer accepts it, but several major ones do.

Put those two barriers together and you get a classic chicken-and-egg problem: you need a card to build credit, but most cards want credit history before they approve you. The practical solution is to start with a secured card at an ITIN-friendly issuer. With no U.S. credit history, a secured card gives you the best approval odds because the refundable deposit lowers the issuer’s risk. The deposit is not a fee; you get it back when you close or graduate the account.

One important note: credit bureaus track activity by ITIN, so a card that reports your payments builds a credit file the same way it would with an SSN. Your ITIN status does not slow the credit-building process.

What documents do I need before I apply?

Gather these before you open a single application tab:

  • Your ITIN: The IRS CP565 notice letter or any IRS correspondence showing your nine-digit number.
  • Government-issued photo ID: A foreign passport is the universal standard. A consular ID (matrícula consular) works at some issuers.
  • U.S. mailing address: A P.O. box is rarely accepted. Use a physical street address.
  • Proof of income: Pay stubs, a bank statement, or a letter from your employer showing your earnings.
  • U.S. bank account: Required to fund the security deposit via electronic transfer.
  • Real U.S. mobile number: Issuers text a one-time code during the application, and a VoIP number (Google Voice, Skype) will fail this check.

Your ITIN works as a financial passport: when a U.S. issuer accepts it for identity verification, you can open and grow a U.S. credit profile.

Which card is best if I want the lowest deposit possible?

This one comes up a lot:

The Capital One Platinum Secured is a secured credit card, meaning you put down a refundable cash deposit that becomes your credit limit. Unlike some secured cards that require your deposit to equal your credit limit dollar-for-dollar, Capital One has a twist: you may qualify for a $200 credit limit with just a $49, $99, or $200 deposit, depending on your creditworthiness. That lower deposit requirement makes it more accessible than most competitors.

Use Capital One’s preapproval page first. It causes no score harm, and Capital One says the Platinum Secured accepts ITIN applications. If the Platinum Secured appears in your result, its live terms show a $0 annual fee, a refundable $49, $99, or $200 minimum deposit, at least a $200 initial line, and a 28.99% variable APR.

After six months of on-time payments, Capital One automatically considers you for a higher credit line with no additional deposit needed. That automatic review is a real advantage over issuers that require you to call or apply separately.

The only real downside: the 28.99% APR is high. If you pay your balance in full every statement cycle, you never pay interest and the rate is irrelevant. If you carry a balance, it will cost you quickly. Pay in full. Every month.

OpenSky offers two relevant cards in 2026, and they differ in ways that matter.

The OpenSky Secured Credit Card is one of the few secured credit cards that openly approves applicants without an SSN. There is no credit check during application, which makes it accessible if your U.S. credit history is thin or nonexistent. You need a valid U.S. address and either a Social Security Number or an ITIN to apply.

What about the Citi Secured Mastercard for ITIN holders?

Readers frequently ask:

You can apply for the Citi Secured Mastercard online or at Citi branches. You will need to provide personal identifying information, including your name, address, and SSN or ITIN. Minimum requirements include being at least 18 years old and having a valid Social Security number or ITIN. Applicants must also put down a security deposit of $200 or more before the account can open. There is no minimum credit score requirement, as the Citi Secured is designed for beginners with little or no borrowing history.

The terms are straightforward: no annual fee, a security deposit of $200 to $2,500 held in a collateral account for 18 months (it earns no interest during that time), and a 26.74% variable APR.

There is one limitation worth knowing before you apply. With responsible use, you may become eligible to upgrade to the Citi Diamond Preferred Credit Card. Customers are reviewed for graduation starting at nine months, then on a 12-month basis after that. To graduate, customers must pass credit graduation criteria and have a valid SSN on file.

In plain terms: you can open the Citi Secured with an ITIN and build credit normally, but graduation to the unsecured Citi card requires an SSN. That’s not a dealbreaker for most ITIN holders who expect to get an SSN eventually, but it’s worth knowing upfront. If you never expect to get an SSN, go with OpenSky or Capital One instead.

Are there any ITIN starter cards with no security deposit at all?

Yes, though they work differently from traditional secured cards.

The Current Build Card has no annual fee, no SSN required, and no hard credit pull. It works from your Current spending account balance, so approvals are effectively guaranteed. It’s best for ITIN holders and newcomers who want a true no-debt, no-risk credit builder. You spend from your Current account like a debit card, and the activity gets reported as on-time credit usage to Experian, TransUnion, and Equifax. You also earn one point per dollar on dining and groceries while you build credit.

The tradeoff is that this product behaves like a debit card with credit reporting bolted on. It won’t teach you revolving-credit habits the way a traditional card does, so many users move on to the Self Visa once six months of history is on the books.

The Self Visa Credit Card takes a different approach. As of January 2025, you can fund a $100 minimum security deposit directly with a debit card or bank account and get the card right away, with no loan required and no hard credit check. If you prefer the traditional path, you can still open a Self Credit Builder Account first, make at least three on-time payments toward the loan, reach $100 or more in savings progress, then use that balance to secure the Visa. The loan-first route lets you build an installment tradeline alongside the card, which boosts your credit mix faster than a card-only approach.

That credit mix benefit is real. FICO scoring rewards borrowers who carry both installment and revolving accounts, and most ITIN holders start with neither. Self gives you both inside a single app.

How long will it take me to go from zero to a real credit score?

This is the question that matters most, and the data is encouraging.

The short answer: six months to your first FICO score, 12-18 months to good credit (670+), and three to five years to excellent credit (740+). Consistent on-time payments and low utilization can get you to a score of 650-700 within 12-18 months. The timeline is the same as for anyone starting with no credit history. Your ITIN status does not slow the process.

From zero to excellent credit (750+): plan for at least 24 months, realistically two to three years. Length of credit history is a major factor, and account age just takes time. There is no way to accelerate the calendar.

The biggest lever you control is payment timing. The CFPB says paying on time every time builds history, and paying off monthly can improve scores; carrying debt is unnecessary. Set autopay for at least the minimum payment as a safety net, then manually pay the full balance before the due date.

What mistakes do ITIN holders make when applying for their first card?

A question we hear often:

Mistake 1: Applying to multiple cards on the same day. Each full application generates a hard inquiry. The CFPB says to apply only for credit you need. Space applications at least three to six months apart.

Mistake 2: Choosing a card that does not report to all three bureaus. Some secured cards and store cards skip one or two bureaus entirely. If your payment history isn’t being reported, you’re not building credit. Always confirm before you apply.

Mistake 3: Skipping the pre-qualification step. Capital One and some other issuers offer soft-check pre-approval tools. Use them to gauge approval odds without a hard inquiry, and only submit the full hard-pull application after you see a positive pre-qualification result.

Mistake 4: Depositing the bare minimum. Your credit limit equals your deposit at most issuers. A $200 limit means even a $60 grocery run pushes your utilization to 30%. Deposit $300-$500 if you can afford it. That gives you more room to spend without hurting your utilization ratio.

Mistake 5: Closing the secured card after graduation. Don’t close your secured card once it’s upgraded. An older account helps your score. Let it convert automatically and keep the account open.

Does it matter which issuer I choose, or are all ITIN secured cards basically the same?

They are not the same, and the differences are material. Here is an issuer-by-issuer breakdown of the factors that vary most:

Capital One: Accepts ITIN online without needing a branch visit. Soft-check pre-approval available. Automatic credit-line review at six months. No annual fee. No rewards, but straightforward. Capital One is one of the better starter card options for ITIN holders because applicants can use an ITIN instead of an SSN and may qualify despite limited or no credit history.

OpenSky (Capital Bank): OpenSky is a secured credit card that does not require a credit check. To open the card, you make a refundable security deposit from $200 to $3,000, and that deposit sets your starting credit limit. Activity is reported to all three major credit bureaus. The standard card charges a $35 annual fee and a 3% foreign transaction fee. The Plus version has no annual fee but a higher APR.

Citi: Citi accepts ITIN for credit card applications, particularly if you already have a banking relationship with them. The Citi Secured Mastercard is a solid option for ITIN holders looking to build credit. Key limitation: graduation to an unsecured card requires an SSN on file.

Bank of America: Bank of America accepts ITIN for credit card applications. If you have a checking or savings account with them, your approval odds improve. Their secured card is a reasonable starting option for ITIN holders with no U.S. credit history. The BankAmericard Secured has no annual fee and requires a minimum security deposit of $200, with a maximum of $5,000, at a 25.99% variable APR for purchases and balance transfers. Existing banking customers have better odds here.

Self: Best for ITIN holders who want both a revolving card and an installment tradeline at the same time. The card carries no annual fee for the first year, then $25 after that, plus a 27.49% variable APR (as of January 1, 2026). Self has high approval rates and is a consistent option for thin-file applicants.

Firstcard: No SSN required to apply, reports to all three credit bureaus (Experian, Equifax, TransUnion), no credit history needed, and available to ITIN holders. It operates as a debit-linked card with credit reporting, so it’s the lowest-risk first step but the least like a traditional credit card.

What is the step-by-step process to apply for my first ITIN card?

Here is a concrete sequence to follow, not generic advice:

Step 1: Run the Capital One pre-qualification. Go to Capital One’s preapproval page and enter your ITIN where the form asks for SSN. Capital One says this eligibility check will not hurt your score. If the Platinum Secured appears, proceed to a full application.

Step 2: If Capital One does not pre-qualify you, apply to OpenSky Plus. With no annual fee and no credit check required, the OpenSky Plus card is an effective way to build credit. Because there is no credit check, approval depends mainly on your identity verification and your ability to fund the deposit.

Step 3: Fund the deposit within the required window. Capital One gives you 35 days after approval to fund by electronic transfer. Citi gives you 14 days to provide your bank account information, which will be used to fund your security deposit via electronic bank transfer. Don’t miss these deadlines or the approval expires.

Step 4: Use the card for one or two small recurring purchases monthly. Keep your balance below 10%-30% of your credit limit at all times. Set autopay for the full statement balance.

Step 5: Check your credit reports at six months. Pull your free reports at AnnualCreditReport.com and confirm that your on-time payments appear correctly under your ITIN on all three bureaus. Dispute any errors in writing immediately.

Step 6: After 12-18 months, consider applying for an unsecured card. By then, you will typically have a scoreable credit file. Consistent on-time payments and low utilization can get you to a score of 650-700 within 12-18 months, and that score opens the door to unsecured cards with rewards. You can explore options in our guide to unsecured credit cards for ITIN holders.

What if my application gets denied?

A denial is not the end. See our full walkthrough at credit card denied with ITIN: what to do for the exact steps. In brief:

First, request the adverse action letter. Federal law requires issuers to tell you why they denied you, typically within 30 days of the decision. Common reasons for ITIN holders include insufficient income, unverifiable identity, or a recent bankruptcy.

Second, consider calling the reconsideration line. Our guide on the credit card reconsideration line for ITIN holders explains how to make that call effectively and what information to have ready.

Third, if the rejection is identity-related, a branch visit often solves it. An ITIN-only applicant may not be able to finish the application fully online and can be routed to phone or branch verification. Bring your ITIN letter, passport, and proof of address in person.

Fourth, if all major issuers decline, fall back to Firstcard or OpenSky. Both have near-universal approval for ITIN holders with a valid identity document and a fundable deposit.

Can I use my foreign credit history to skip the secured-card stage?

This one comes up a lot:

Possibly, but only for a limited number of countries. American Express offers a Global Card Transfer program that allows applicants from certain countries (including Canada, the UK, India, Mexico, Brazil, and Australia) to transfer their Amex relationship to a U.S. card without starting from zero. Amex’s own guide lists an ITIN and a source of income among the usual requirements.

Nova Credit, a third-party service, currently supports credit history from India, Mexico, UK, Canada, Australia, Brazil, Dominican Republic, Kenya, Nigeria, Philippines, and South Korea. A handful of U.S. issuers (including American Express and some student loan lenders) accept Nova Credit reports. Chase, Capital One, and most traditional secured-card issuers do not use Nova Credit directly.

For most ITIN holders from countries outside that list, the foreign credit history route is not available. A domestic secured card remains the most reliable path. If you want to explore this option further, our guide on using foreign credit history for a U.S. credit card with an ITIN covers the program details and issuer list.
