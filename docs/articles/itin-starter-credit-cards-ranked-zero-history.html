You are starting at exactly the same point that millions of immigrants have started before you, and the path forward is well-documented. Why does starting from zero credit matter so much, and why is an ITIN card the fix?

A question we hear often:

Most traditional credit cards require a U.S. credit history. If you just arrived from another country, you have none, even if you had excellent credit back home. Credit scores do not transfer between countries. Your perfect payment record elsewhere is invisible to U.S. lenders.

The practical consequence is a chicken-and-egg problem: lenders want to see a credit history before they will extend credit, but you cannot build a history without a card that reports to the bureaus. The solution is a category of products designed for exactly this situation.

A handful of issuers now underwrite applicants using an ITIN, passport, or even visa documents, and a few newer products skip the credit check altogether. The ITIN is the key that opens this door. It is a nine-digit IRS tax number for people who file U.S. taxes but are not eligible for an SSN. When an issuer accepts it, they can verify your identity, open an account in your name, and begin reporting your payments to the three major credit bureaus: Experian, Equifax, and TransUnion.

Once a credit account is opened using your ITIN, it reports to the credit bureaus the same way as any other account. Payment history, utilization, and account age all factor into your score identically, whether you used an ITIN or SSN to open the account.

What is the quickest timeline from zero ITIN history to a real FICO score?

This one comes up a lot:

The timeline is more predictable than most people expect. It takes at least six months of account history to generate your first FICO Score, though VantageScore produces a score faster.

After you open your first ITIN card and make on-time payments, here is a realistic milestone map:

  • Months 1-3: Your account is reported to the bureaus. No score yet, but your file exists.
  • Month 6: Building a fair credit score (580-669) typically takes 4-6 months of on-time payments with an ITIN.
  • Months 12-18: A good score (670-739) usually takes 12-18 months of consistent behavior.
  • Month 24+: From zero to excellent credit (750 or above), plan for at least 24 months, and realistically 2-3 years.

Payment history is 35% of your FICO score. It is the single biggest factor. The cards in this guide all report monthly to all three bureaus, which means every on-time payment compounds.

Which card should I get first if I have an ITIN and literally no U.S. credit file?

Our top recommendation is the Capital One Platinum Secured for most applicants, with OpenSky Plus Secured as the backup for those who cannot pass a credit check or who have no U.S. bank account.

#1 Capital One Platinum Secured

Unlike some secured cards that require your deposit to match your credit limit dollar-for-dollar, Capital One has a twist: you may qualify for a $200 credit limit with just a $49, $99, or $200 deposit, depending on your creditworthiness. That lower deposit requirement makes it more accessible than most competitors.

The Capital One Platinum Secured is one of the better starter credit cards for applicants without an SSN because you can use an ITIN instead and may qualify despite limited or no credit history. Capital One also offers a soft-check pre-approval tool, so you can check your odds without a hard inquiry on your file.

After six months of on-time payments, Capital One automatically reviews your account for a credit limit increase with no additional deposit required. The card carries a $0 annual fee and no foreign transaction fees, which matters for ITIN holders who may still be sending payments or shopping internationally.

The honest drawback: the variable APR currently sits at 29.99%. Pay the full balance every month and that number is irrelevant. Carry a balance and it will cost you.

#2 OpenSky Plus Secured Visa (best no-credit-check option)

The OpenSky Secured Credit Card is one of the few secured cards that openly approves applicants without an SSN: an ITIN works. There is no credit check during the application, which makes it accessible if your U.S. credit history is thin or nonexistent.

There is no annual fee on the OpenSky Plus Secured Visa. The tradeoff is a higher minimum deposit. The OpenSky Plus requires an upfront security deposit with a minimum of $300, a bit higher than some other secured cards.

All three of OpenSky’s options feature an 89% approval rate and do not require a credit check. On average, OpenSky users improve their credit scores by 50 points in 6 months.

For ITIN holders with no U.S. credit history who are uncertain they would pass even a basic underwriting check, this is the most reliable first card available.

#3 OpenSky Secured Visa (fee card, lower deposit)

If you can only put down $200 and need the no-credit-check guarantee, the original OpenSky Secured Visa is available at a $200 minimum deposit. The cost is a $35 annual fee. If you tend to carry a balance and want the lowest APR, the original Secured Visa’s 23.89% rate is the cheapest of the three OpenSky options. You need a valid U.S. address and either an SSN or an ITIN to apply. International students with an ITIN may qualify.

#4 Citi Secured Mastercard

Citi’s Secured Mastercard is a no-annual-fee secured card that reports to the major bureaus and lets you choose a deposit from $200 to $2,500 in $100 increments. Citi’s own requirements list an SSN or ITIN among the identity fields, which makes it a viable ITIN route.

Two caveats for ITIN-only applicants. First, like Bank of America, an ITIN-only applicant may not be able to finish the application fully online and can be routed to phone or branch verification. Second, graduating to an unsecured card requires a valid SSN on file, so an ITIN-only holder can open it and build credit but may not graduate until they later obtain an SSN.

The upgrade timeline is also longer than Capital One’s. After 18 months of on-time payments, Citi may offer a product change to an unsecured card and return your security deposit. Unlike Capital One, Citi does not conduct automatic reviews: you should contact Citi to request a product change at the 18-month mark.

#5 Zolve Credit Card (best no-deposit option)

Zolve is an unsecured U.S. credit card with no annual fee that does not require an SSN. You can apply with your passport or ITIN. Uniquely, you can apply and get a virtual card before you even arrive in the U.S., which makes it the best fit on this list for the truly remote, abroad-first applicant.

Through Zolve, you can apply with an SSN, ITIN, or passport, and you can get approved without a credit check. Unlike a traditional unsecured credit card, the spending limit is initially tied to your Zolve checking account balance. It functions more like a charge card against your own funds at first, but it reports as a credit account to the bureaus.

The maximum limit is $15,000, though not all applicants will qualify. Zolve works particularly well for international students and new professionals who want a card that works immediately without a deposit sitting locked up.

#6 Firstcard Secured Credit Builder

No SSN required to apply, reports to all three credit bureaus, no credit history needed, available to ITIN holders. Firstcard was built specifically for people starting their U.S. credit journey. Unlike most cards that reject applicants without an SSN, Firstcard welcomes immigrants and international students from day one. It reports to all three bureaus, so your on-time payments actually count toward building your score.

#7 Self Visa Credit Card

The Self Visa Credit Card gives you two ways in. As of January 2025, you can fund a $100 minimum security deposit directly with a debit card or bank account and get the card right away, with no loan required and no hard credit check. If you prefer the traditional path, you can open a Self Credit Builder Account first, make at least three on-time payments toward the loan and reach $100 or more in savings progress, then use that balance to secure the Visa.

The loan-first route adds an installment tradeline alongside the card, which builds your credit mix faster than a card-only approach. The Self Visa carries a $25 annual fee and accepts ITIN applicants who already have a Self Credit Builder Account. It reports to all three bureaus and works best for ITIN applicants who want both an installment tradeline and a revolving card in one place.

Important 2026 update: the Discover it Secured is no longer available

This is a piece of current information that many older guides miss.

Discover has temporarily discontinued applications for the Discover it Secured Credit Card. Applications closed on June 2, 2026. Capital One, which acquired Discover in 2025, says it will relaunch the card later this year.

This matters because the Discover it Secured was for years the most recommended secured card for ITIN holders, thanks to its cash-back rewards and automatic 7-month upgrade review. That automatic review feature, which for years allowed it to stand out, is gone: the card will no longer automatically check at the seven-month mark whether a cardholder qualifies to move to an unsecured line and get their deposit back.

If you see any guide recommending the Discover it Secured as a live option, that guide has not been updated since June 2026. The Capital One Platinum Secured is the direct replacement for most applicants.

Does it matter which specific card I choose, or is any reporting card equally good?

This is one of the most underrated questions for ITIN holders starting from zero. Your behavior matters more than your card, but three card features can meaningfully accelerate or slow your progress.

  1. Three-bureau reporting. All three bureaus (Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion) matter for future auto, rental, and mortgage approvals. A card that only reports to one bureau slows you down because different lenders pull different bureaus. Every card in this guide reports to all three.

  2. Upgrade path transparency. Capital One’s automatic 6-month review and OpenSky’s invitation-based upgrade are both cleaner than Citi’s 18-month self-initiated request. The faster you graduate to an unsecured card, the sooner your deposit is returned and the sooner you can apply for rewards products. Our guide on how to upgrade from a secured to an unsecured card with an ITIN covers exactly how to handle that conversation.

  3. Annual fee vs. no fee. A $35 annual fee on a card you plan to hold for 12-18 months is $35-$52.50 total, which is modest. But if you are cost-sensitive, the OpenSky Plus ($300 deposit, no fee) and Capital One Platinum ($49 deposit, no fee) both cut that cost entirely.

The behaviors that drive score growth are consistent regardless of which card you choose. Pay your full balance before the due date every single month. Never carry a balance. This builds your score faster than anything else.

Can I use two cards at once to build credit faster?

Readers frequently ask:

Yes, and for some ITIN holders it is a smart move, but timing matters. The strategy that works is opening one card first, making 6 months of on-time payments to generate your first FICO score, and then adding a second card.

Managing credit cards and installment accounts at the same time accelerates the timeline. Pure credit-card-only users progress more slowly than those handling multiple account types. This is why Self’s loan-plus-card setup is genuinely useful: it adds an installment tradeline alongside a revolving card from day one.

If you go the single-card route first, here is a common sequence that ITIN holders use successfully:

  1. Month 0: Open Capital One Platinum Secured or OpenSky Plus
  2. Month 6: First FICO score generated, Capital One auto-review for limit increase
  3. Month 12: Apply for a second card (unsecured or rewards product once score is in the 650+ range)
  4. Month 18-24: Graduate primary secured card, recover deposit, apply for mainstream cards

For a detailed look at which unsecured cards you can realistically target after your secured card builds your file, see our guide to unsecured credit cards for ITIN holders.

What should I do if my ITIN card application gets denied?

With no U.S. credit history, a secured card gives you the best approval odds because the refundable deposit lowers the issuer’s risk. A denial on a secured card is uncommon but not impossible, and it almost always comes down to one of three things: a VoIP phone number, an incomplete address match, or a prior negative account at the same bank.

If you are denied, here is what to do:

  1. Do not immediately re-apply at another issuer. Each hard pull costs a few points from a file that does not yet have many points to spare.
  2. Call the reconsideration line. Issuers often reverse denials when applicants provide additional context like proof of income or a U.S. bank statement. Our full guide to calling the reconsideration line as an ITIN holder walks through the exact script.
  3. Move to OpenSky. If Capital One denied you, OpenSky’s no-credit-check process removes underwriting friction entirely. It is built for this situation.
  4. Check your application. Make sure your ITIN matches your name exactly as it appears on your IRS letter, and that your address is a real deliverable U.S. address, not a forwarding service.

For a full breakdown of why ITIN applications get denied and what to do, see our guide on what to do when a credit card is denied with an ITIN.
