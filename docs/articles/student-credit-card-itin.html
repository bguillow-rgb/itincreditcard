Student Credit Cards With an ITIN (2026 Guide)

Yes, international students and college students with an ITIN can apply for student credit cards without an SSN. Capital One Savor Student, Capital One Quicksilver Student, and Chase Freedom Rise all accept ITIN on their online applications and report to all three credit bureaus.

Getting a student credit card as an international student or newcomer without an SSN is very doable in 2026.

The Capital One Savor Student Cash Rewards Credit Card lets you apply with an ITIN instead of an SSN and carries no annual fee, making it a reasonable starting point for students building credit. Below, our editorial team answers the real questions we get about student credit cards for ITIN holders, so you can pick the right card and avoid common mistakes.

Which student credit cards actually accept an ITIN instead of an SSN?

A question we hear often: students assume that because most card applications have an “SSN required” field, there is no way in without one. That is not the case.

If you are a recent immigrant, an international student, or another noncitizen without a Social Security number, you may still be able to apply for a credit card using an Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN). For student-specific cards, three options are consistently verified in 2026:

Capital One and Chase both publish guidance confirming that an ITIN may be used by some applicants in place of an SSN.

One thing worth knowing: Capital One states that “card issuers decide which of their credit cards are available for ITIN applicants,” meaning a bank can accept your ITIN for one card and reject it for another. Always verify the specific card’s application before assuming your ITIN will be accepted.

Is there a difference between “student” cards and regular ITIN cards?

Student credit cards are designed for people aged 18-24 who are enrolled in a qualifying college or university. They tend to have lower income requirements, reduced credit history thresholds, and sometimes perks tied to good grades or on-campus spending. Regular ITIN cards (like the Capital One Platinum Secured) are open to anyone with an ITIN but are not tied to enrollment status.

The practical advantage for ITIN-holding students is that recent immigrants may qualify for cards built for people with little or no credit history, and student cards are one of the better fits in that category, alongside secured cards and some unsecured options. Student cards skip the deposit requirement entirely if you qualify, which saves you from tying up $200 or more in a security deposit.

For students who cannot yet qualify for an unsecured student card because they have zero U.S. credit history, the right path is usually a secured card first. See our guide to secured credit cards for a full comparison of deposit-based options that accept ITINs.

How do I actually apply for a student credit card using my ITIN?

This one comes up a lot: the online form asks for a “Social Security Number.” Where does the ITIN go?

The process is simpler than it looks. On both Capital One and Chase applications, the SSN field accepts an ITIN. For Capital One and Discover, entering your ITIN in the SSN field has worked for many applicants. You will also need:

  • Your ITIN letter (IRS Form CP565) or the nine-digit number itself
  • A foreign passport or government-issued ID
  • A U.S. mailing address
  • Proof of college enrollment (the application may ask for your school name)
  • Your annual income, including allowances, part-time wages, or stipends

If you end up applying for a secured card rather than an unsecured student card, you will also need a U.S. bank account to fund the deposit.

If you do not yet have an ITIN, apply using IRS Form W-7. Processing typically takes about seven weeks. Do not apply for any credit card until your ITIN is confirmed: issuers verify the number against IRS records, and a pending or absent ITIN will cause an instant rejection.

What rewards can I earn on a student card with an ITIN?

Readers frequently ask: “Are student cards just bare-bones credit builders, or do they actually earn rewards?”

They genuinely earn rewards. The Capital One Savor Student is the standout for most students because it focuses on categories where students actually spend money. WalletHub rated it 4.7/5 as of November 2026. On the flat-rate side, the Capital One Quicksilver Student card earns 1.5% cash back on every purchase, making it one of the more rewarding cards available to students.

WalletHub also reported in 2026 that seven of the 10 largest credit card companies will consider applicants who do not have an SSN, including Bank of America and Capital One. The ITIN student card market is real and growing.

For students who want to compare rewards options beyond student-only products, our cash back credit cards for ITIN holders guide covers both student and non-student options side by side.

What if I have no U.S. credit history at all? Can I still get a student card?

This is where many students hit a wall. Student cards are easier to get than premium travel cards, but they are not guaranteed with zero U.S. history. Here is a realistic path:

Option A: Apply directly. Capital One explicitly states it accepts ITIN applicants with limited credit history for its student cards. If you have any form of thin file, perhaps from being an authorized user on a parent’s U.S. card, the approval odds improve considerably. Check out our guide on how to become an authorized user with an ITIN if you want to build history that way first.

Option B: Start secured, then graduate. If you just got your ITIN and have no U.S. credit history, start with a secured card. A secured card requires a cash deposit (usually $200-$500) that becomes your credit limit. Use it for small purchases, pay the full balance every month, and your score will climb within 6 months.

You will typically have a scoreable credit file after six months of account activity. From there, consistent on-time payments and low utilization can get you to a score of 650-700 within 12-18 months. The timeline is the same as for anyone starting with no credit history. Your ITIN status does not slow the process down.

One warning: watch out for cards that market themselves as ITIN-friendly but charge application fees, monthly maintenance fees, and a setup fee that eats your credit line before you even use the card. Skip any offer that does not report to at least two of the three major bureaus, because the whole point is building bureau-verified history.

The trade-off is straightforward: student cards save you a deposit but require enrollment and slightly more creditworthiness. Secured cards are more universally accessible but tie up cash. If you are enrolled in a U.S. college or university and have even a thin credit file, try the student card route first. If that fails, pivot to a secured card and revisit the student card after six months of reported payments.

Should I apply for more than one student card at once?

No. Applications create hard inquiries unless the issuer specifically uses a soft pull, so apply deliberately and space requests at least 90 days apart. Applying for two or three student cards on the same day can lower your score before it has even had a chance to form, and multiple hard inquiries in a short window can flag you as a risk to issuers reviewing your file later.

A better approach: apply for one card, use it responsibly for six months, then consider a second card from a different issuer to diversify your credit mix. After six months, ask your first issuer for a credit-line increase, since a larger limit lowers utilization automatically. If you want to hold multiple cards eventually, our guide to having multiple credit cards with an ITIN covers timing and strategy.
