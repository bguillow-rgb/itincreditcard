Banks & Credit Unions That Accept ITIN for Credit Cards (2026)

By Editorial Team · Cards & Credit Building · Published August 18, 2026

Quick Answer

More than 10 banks and credit unions accept an ITIN for credit card applications in 2026, including Capital One, Chase, Bank of America, Citi, Wells Fargo, Discover, and several credit unions. Most allow online applications; Bank of America and some credit unions typically require an in-branch visit. A secured card is the strongest starting point.

”Which big bank should I start with if I have an ITIN and no U.S. credit history?”

A question we hear often: the instinct of many new ITIN holders is to walk into the nearest Chase or Bank of America branch. That is not always wrong, but it is not always the best first move either.

Chase, American Express, and Capital One all accept an ITIN on credit card applications. They sit at very different points on the approval spectrum, though, especially if you are starting from zero.

Capital One is the most accessible starting point among major banks. You can compare Capital One credit cards and check whether you are pre-approved before you apply, without any impact to your credit scores. That soft-pull pre-qualification tool is a genuine advantage: you find out whether approval is likely before a hard inquiry ever hits your file. The Platinum Secured carries no annual fee, Capital One reviews your account after six months for a possible unsecured upgrade, and ITIN is accepted.

Chase accepts ITIN in the SSN field on its online application. Lenders ask for a Social Security number or an Individual Taxpayer Identification Number to verify your identity, and Chase officially lists both as valid. The catch is that Chase’s most desirable cards, like the Sapphire Preferred, require an established U.S. credit history. For a true beginner, Capital One or a secured specialist card is a better entry point. Chase is the graduation destination.

Bank of America also accepts ITIN, but with a practical caveat. If you have a checking or savings account with them, your chances of approval improve considerably. Their secured card is a reasonable starting option for ITIN holders with no U.S. credit history. The main friction: large national banks like Bank of America are known to accept ITINs, but you almost always have to apply in person at a branch. An existing account relationship is a significant plus.

Editorial verdict: Start with Capital One if you want the path of least resistance online. Visit a Bank of America or Chase branch if you already have an account there and want a relationship with a specific big bank.

”Does Wells Fargo, Citi, or Discover actually accept ITIN? I keep getting conflicting answers.”

Readers frequently ask: this because outdated forum posts still claim these issuers require an SSN. The current reality is more ITIN-friendly.

Wells Fargo officially lists ITIN as acceptable. An Individual Taxpayer Identification Number can be used if you don’t have an SSN, and a physical U.S. address is required for credit card applications. Having a Wells Fargo bank account is not mandatory, but it can strengthen your application.

Citi accepts ITIN, particularly when you bring an existing banking relationship. The Citi Secured Mastercard is a reasonable option for ITIN holders who want to build credit.

Discover is one of the more welcoming mainstream issuers. Discover accepts ITIN holders for both unsecured and secured credit cards in 2026. Immigrants who do not qualify for a standard credit card may be eligible for a secured card or a student credit card, and Discover makes that path clear. The Discover it Secured consistently ranks as a top first card for ITIN holders: it has a clear upgrade path, reports to all three bureaus, and matches your first year of cash back rewards.

American Express also accepts ITIN. Cards that accept ITIN include the Chase Freedom Unlimited, Chase Sapphire Preferred, American Express Blue Cash Everyday, American Express Gold Card, and Capital One Savor. Keep in mind that Amex premium cards expect a healthy U.S. credit history. They are not entry-level products.

”What about fintech issuers and cards that don’t require a credit check?”

This one comes up a lot, especially from readers who have been denied at a major bank and need a guaranteed-approval path.

OpenSky is the standard-bearer for no-credit-check ITIN approval. The OpenSky Secured Credit Card is one of the few secured cards that openly approves applicants without an SSN, and an ITIN works. You need a valid U.S. address and either a Social Security Number or an ITIN to apply. OpenSky now offers three secured Visa tiers: the original ($35 annual fee, $200 minimum deposit, 23.89% APR), the Plus ($0 annual fee, $300 minimum deposit), and the Launch ($0 annual fee, $100 minimum deposit). After roughly six months of on-time payments, eligible cardholders are invited to upgrade to the unsecured OpenSky Visa Gold. Upgrades are by invitation rather than open application, but your deposit is returned when you graduate, and no fresh hard credit pull is required.

Zolve sits at the other end of the spectrum: an unsecured card with no deposit required. Zolve has no annual fee, does not require an SSN, and you can apply with your passport or ITIN. You can apply and get a virtual card before you even arrive in the U.S., which makes it the best option for international students or professionals relocating to the United States. You can also open a U.S. checking account entirely online with just your passport, SSN, or ITIN.

Tilt (issued by WebBank) also accepts ITIN and shows up regularly in 2026 roundups as a credit builder that replaced Petal, which no longer takes new applicants.

”Are credit unions worth pursuing for an ITIN credit card?”

For many ITIN holders, especially those in immigrant-heavy communities, a local credit union or CDFI (Community Development Financial Institution) is the single best path to a first credit card.

Many local credit unions and CDFIs accept ITIN for credit cards and tend to have more flexible requirements than big banks. That flexibility means an underwriter may look at your rental history, employment record, or bank account activity rather than purely relying on a FICO score you have not had time to build yet.

A handful of credit unions with confirmed ITIN acceptance: La Capitol FCU, Wings Financial, and First Tech FCU. La Capitol appears the most lenient, accepting ITIN holders and non-residents. Resource One Credit Union (R1CU) has specifically announced ITIN as an accepted form of identification and serves members in the Dallas, Texas area. Alliant Credit Union is considered the best online-only option for ITIN holders, with zero fees and the ability to apply entirely online.

The trade-off with credit unions is membership eligibility: you usually need to live, work, or worship in a defined service area, or join an affiliated organization. Call the credit union before visiting to confirm both ITIN acceptance and your membership eligibility. Policies can vary by branch.

”Why should I trust that ITIN holders are good credit customers? Won’t banks reject me as high-risk?”

A question we hear often, from readers who worry that having an ITIN instead of an SSN automatically flags them as a credit risk. The data says otherwise.

According to Experian’s research white paper on ITIN credit behavior, ITIN holders maintain a lower debt-to-income ratio (25%) than SSN consumers, and have fewer accounts with payments past due. SentiLink’s research confirms that ITIN applicants in auto lending missed fewer payments over the 12-month window compared to SSN applicants.

As reported by Experian’s 2026 white paper, the IRS has issued more than 27 million individual taxpayer identification numbers since 1996. That is a large, financially engaged population, and issuers that understand the data are increasingly competing for it. Fintechs have posted a 71% year-over-year increase in account originations, with ITIN-friendly products accounting for a growing share of that growth.

The practical takeaway: if an issuer is hesitant, it is usually because of a thin credit file, not the ITIN itself. The fastest way to address that is a secured card that reports to all three bureaus, held for at least 6-12 months before you apply for anything unsecured. See our guide on how to build credit fast with an ITIN credit card for the full strategy.

”What exact documents do I need to bring or have ready before I apply?”

This one comes up a lot, especially from readers who have been stuck at a branch because they did not bring the right paperwork.

The standard document set required across most issuers is consistent:

  1. Your ITIN letter (IRS notice CP565 or CP567). This is the physical letter the IRS mailed you when your ITIN was assigned. Collect it along with your passport or government ID, proof of U.S. address, and your initial deposit amount.

  2. A valid government-issued photo ID. A foreign passport is universally accepted. A consular ID (matrícula consular) is accepted at some issuers but not all.

  3. Proof of U.S. physical address. A utility bill, lease agreement, or bank statement works. Most banks require you to open an account in person, not online, if you are using an ITIN instead of an SSN.

  4. Proof of income. Pay stubs, an employment offer letter, or recent tax returns. Income documentation matters more for unsecured cards.

  5. A U.S. bank account (for secured card deposits). You need a valid ITIN, a foreign passport, a U.S. mailing address, and a U.S. bank account to fund any security deposit.

One practical tip: use a real U.S. mobile number. Issuers text a one-time verification code during the application, and a VoIP number (Google Voice, Skype) will fail this check.

For online applications, enter your ITIN in the Social Security Number field. Most applications include an ITIN option or an “I don’t have an SSN” choice. If the online form does not accept it, visit a branch and apply at the counter. The branch application process always allows ITIN.

”Does having a bank account at the same institution really matter that much?”

Yes, and this is one of the most underrated pieces of advice for ITIN holders. Having a checking or savings account at the same institution where you are applying for a credit card meaningfully improves your chances.

Here is why: when a bank has no U.S. credit history to pull for you, a deposit relationship is the next best evidence of financial responsibility. The bank can see your average balance, your income deposits, and your spending patterns. That internal data can substitute, at least partially, for the thin credit file that would otherwise prompt an automatic denial.

This is especially true at Bank of America and Citi, where branch staff have more discretion to approve an applicant with a solid deposit history even if the external credit file is sparse. An existing bank account with them is a significant advantage and can make the process considerably smoother.

The same logic applies to credit unions. If you are a member in good standing and your checking account shows regular deposits, the loan officer reviewing your credit card application has concrete evidence of your reliability. Community banks and credit unions are often your best allies: they offer more personalized review and are more willing to look at your whole financial situation rather than a single number.

”What is the recommended sequence for ITIN holders applying for cards over time?”

Rather than applying to several issuers at once (which triggers multiple hard inquiries and can hurt your approval odds), follow a staged approach.

Stage 1: Month 0-6 Apply for one secured card from a no-credit-check or pre-qualification issuer. OpenSky (no credit check) or Capital One Platinum Secured (soft pre-qual available) are the top two choices. Put one recurring charge on the card and pay the balance in full each month. That is how you get a scoreable credit file. You will typically have a score after six months of activity, and consistent on-time payments with low utilization can get you to a score of 650-700 within 12-18 months.

Stage 2: Month 6-18 Once your score reaches roughly 640-670, you become eligible for a broader set of unsecured cards. Discover, Capital One, and Wells Fargo are natural next steps. At this stage, revisit the secured vs. unsecured ITIN credit card comparison to decide whether to upgrade your existing card or open a new one.

Stage 3: Month 18 and beyond With 18+ months of clean history and a score above 700, mainstream rewards cards at Chase, American Express, and Citi become realistic targets. This is also when you can look at cash back credit cards for ITIN holders and travel credit cards that were not accessible at Stage 1.

”Do I need to worry about the 2026 regulatory environment affecting ITIN credit card applications?”

This is a fair concern in 2026. While the use of an ITIN was designated as a “risk factor” in a May 2026 executive order, Experian’s data points in the other direction: ITIN holders have a lower debt-to-income ratio and fewer accounts with payments past due compared to SSN holders.

For credit cards specifically, the regulatory pressure has been aimed more at mortgage lending than at revolving credit. Major card issuers including Capital One, Chase, Discover, and Wells Fargo have not announced any policy changes restricting ITIN credit card applications as of August 2026. Fintech issuers like Zolve and OpenSky have similarly continued accepting ITINs without interruption.

The most up-to-date analysis of how federal policy intersects with ITIN credit card access is in our dedicated article on the 2026 executive order and ITIN credit card applications.

8 things that can get your ITIN credit card application rejected (and how to fix them)

Even a strong applicant can trip over a preventable error. Here are the most common rejection triggers, compiled from reader feedback and issuer guidance:

  1. VoIP phone number. Many issuers text a verification code to your mobile number. A Google Voice or Skype number will fail. Use a real U.S. SIM.
  2. P.O. Box as your address. Federal regulations require a physical address, not a P.O. Box, for credit card applications.
  3. No existing U.S. bank account. Without a U.S. account, you cannot fund a secured card deposit, and many issuers cannot verify your identity.
  4. Applying at the wrong issuer first. Applying for an unsecured Chase Sapphire with zero U.S. credit history wastes a hard inquiry. Match the card tier to your credit stage.
  5. Not calling ahead for a branch visit. Before visiting, call the branch and tell them you want to open an account with an ITIN. This ensures you speak with someone who knows the process and can prepare the right paperwork. Not all branch staff are equally familiar with ITIN accounts.
  6. Applying for multiple cards at once. Each application triggers a hard pull. Space applications at least 3-6 months apart.
  7. Expired ITIN. ITINs expire if you have not filed a federal tax return for three consecutive years. An expired ITIN will cause an immediate application failure. Check our article on what happens to your credit card if your ITIN expires if you are unsure about your ITIN’s status.
  8. No income documentation. For any card that is not no-credit-check, underwriters need to see that you can repay. Even self-employment income counts if you can document it.
