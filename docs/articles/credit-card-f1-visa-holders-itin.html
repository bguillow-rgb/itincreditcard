Credit Cards for F-1 Visa Holders With an ITIN (2026)

By Research Desk · Issuers & Approval · Published September 11, 2026

Quick Answer

F-1 visa holders can get a U.S. credit card using an ITIN in place of an SSN. Secured cards from issuers like Capital One, Discover, and Bank of America accept ITINs. You will need your ITIN, passport, F-1 visa, I-20, a U.S. address, and proof of income if you are under 21.

F-1 students face a classic catch-22: you need U.S. credit history to qualify for a card, but you need a card to build credit history. An ITIN breaks that cycle.

Getting approved as a noncitizen is genuinely tricky, but it is not impossible. This guide covers which cards accept an ITIN, what documents to gather, and the one legal rule that trips up applicants under 21.

Can F-1 students actually get a credit card with just an ITIN?

Yes, and more issuers accept ITIN applications than most students realize.

Most credit cards require a Social Security number or an Individual Taxpayer Identification Number. An ITIN satisfies that requirement: it is the tax ID an issuer needs to verify your identity on a credit application. Banks that have confirmed ITIN acceptance include Capital One, Bank of America, Citi, and Wells Fargo, according to issuer documentation reviewed in 2026. You do not need to be a U.S. citizen or permanent resident. You need a valid tax ID, a U.S. address, and verifiable income.

What documents do I need to apply?

A question we hear often: students collect every possible document, then discover one item missing at the application screen.

Here is the complete checklist:

  • ITIN letter from the IRS (or the ITIN itself entered on the application form)
  • Valid passport (primary photo ID)
  • F-1 visa stamp or visa foil
  • I-20 form (Certificate of Eligibility for Nonimmigrant Student Status)
  • U.S. mailing address (your campus dormitory qualifies)
  • U.S. bank account for any security deposit and for verifying financial activity
  • Proof of income if you are under 21 (on-campus pay stub, research stipend letter, work-study award)

One practical note: use a real U.S. mobile number. Issuers send a one-time verification code during the application, and a VoIP number will fail that check.

What is the income rule for F-1 students under 21?

This one comes up a lot: applicants under 21 get denied and have no idea why.

Under the Credit CARD Act of 2009, you must have independent income: on-campus wages, work-study earnings, research assistant stipends, or CPT internship pay. Parental transfers do not count. This is federal law, not an individual issuer policy, so it applies regardless of which card you choose.

If you are under 21 with no qualifying income, two options are worth considering. First, wait until you secure on-campus employment, which most F-1 students are eligible for up to 20 hours per week. Second, become an authorized user on a trusted family member’s or friend’s U.S. credit card in the meantime. It is a legitimate way to begin a credit file before you apply on your own. See our guide on becoming an authorized user with an ITIN for the mechanics.

Should I apply with my ITIN now or wait for an SSN?

F-1 students with work authorization can apply for an SSN and qualify for most student credit cards. Students not authorized to work can apply for an ITIN, which is available to anyone in the U.S. with taxable income, including taxable scholarships.

The decision comes down to timing. If you get a card with an ITIN and later receive an SSN, you will need to request a transfer of your credit history from each bureau separately. That is extra administrative work, and if your SSN is just a few weeks away, waiting may be the cleaner path.

That said, every month without a card is a month of credit history you cannot get back. Opening a card in month one versus month twelve means eleven extra months of history, which has a real and lasting effect on your score. If your SSN is more than a month or two out, applying with your ITIN now is generally the better financial call.

Once you receive your SSN, the merge process is straightforward: contact Experian, Equifax, and TransUnion individually and request that your ITIN file be linked to your new SSN. Our article on what happens to your credit cards when you get an SSN after ITIN covers this step by step.

How do I pre-qualify without hurting my credit?

Readers frequently ask: is there a way to check approval odds before committing to a hard inquiry?

Pre-qualification uses a soft inquiry and does not affect your credit score. Capital One and Discover both offer pre-qualification tools. Using them before you formally apply is especially important if you have no U.S. credit history, because a rejected hard inquiry leaves a mark with nothing to show for it.

Bank of America also allows in-branch pre-qualification conversations for ITIN holders, particularly if you already hold a checking account with them. That existing relationship improves your approval odds considerably.

The broader pre-qualification process for ITIN holders is covered in our credit card pre-qualification with an ITIN guide.

What is the fastest way to build credit after I get my first F-1 card?

According to a 2026 Experian white paper, ITIN holders carry a lower debt-to-income ratio and fewer past-due accounts than SSN holders. That matters because it shows you are not a high-risk borrower. The habits below put you on the right side of that data from day one.

Most scoring models need at least one account to report for roughly six months before generating a score. To make that window count:

  • Pay the full statement balance every month. Carrying a balance costs interest and does nothing extra for your score compared to paying in full.
  • Keep spending below 30% of your credit limit. Below 10% is better during the credit-building phase.
  • Set up autopay so you never miss a due date.
  • Use the card for small, regular expenses like groceries or a streaming subscription so activity is consistent but easy to pay off.

The average APR on U.S. credit cards was 21.00% across all accounts in Q1 2026, per the Federal Reserve G.19. That number is why carrying a balance is so costly. Treat the card like a debit card: only charge what you can pay in full each month.

For a deeper look at habits and timelines, see how to build credit fast with an ITIN credit card and how to use an ITIN credit card responsibly.

What if my application is denied?

Being denied on a first try as an F-1 student is common. It is not a permanent setback.

Start by calling the issuer’s reconsideration line and asking them to review your application with additional documentation, such as income proof or enrollment verification. If that does not work, apply for a secured card. Because the deposit reduces the issuer’s risk, approval rates on secured products are much higher.

Do not submit multiple new applications quickly. Each one generates a hard inquiry, and a cluster of hard inquiries in a short window looks bad. Space applications at least 90-180 days apart. Federal law requires the issuer to send you an adverse action notice explaining why you were denied. Read it carefully and address the specific gap before reapplying. Frequently Asked Questions

Can I apply for a credit card on an F-1 visa with only an ITIN and no U.S. credit history? Yes. Secured cards like the Capital One Platinum Secured and the Discover it Secured accept ITIN applications and do not require an existing U.S. credit history. The security deposit reduces issuer risk, which is why approval rates are higher for these products.

What documents do I need to apply for a credit card as an F-1 student with an ITIN? You typically need your ITIN, a valid passport, your F-1 visa, your I-20 form, a U.S. mailing address, and proof of income (such as a stipend letter or on-campus pay stub) if you are under 21, as required by the Credit CARD Act.

Should I wait for an SSN or apply now with my ITIN? If you have taxable U.S. income or scholarships and already have your ITIN, applying now starts your credit history earlier. If you expect to receive an SSN within a few weeks, you may prefer to wait so you avoid requesting a credit-file merge later.

Will my F-1 status affect my credit card application? F-1 visa status alone does not disqualify you. Issuers care about identity verification (ITIN or SSN), income, and credit history. Providing your ITIN and a U.S. bank account relationship strengthens your application regardless of visa type.

How long does it take to build a credit score after getting my first card as an F-1 student? Most scoring models require at least one account to report for roughly six months before generating a score. With on-time payments and low utilization, many F-1 students see a usable score within four to six months of opening their first card.
