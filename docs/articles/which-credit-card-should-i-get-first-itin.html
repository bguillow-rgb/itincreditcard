Which Credit Card Should I Get First With an ITIN?

By Research Desk · Issuers & Approval · Published September 4, 2026

Advertiser disclosure:ITIN Credit Card is an independent resource. Some links on this page are from partners who compensate us, which may affect which products we feature and where. This does not change our editorial guidance and is never a cost to you. See our full disclosure.

Quick Answer

Your best first credit card with an ITIN is usually a secured card from a major bank (Capital One, Bank of America, Citi) or a no-deposit fintech card (Current Build, Firstcard). Both paths report to all three bureaus. Pick the one that fits your deposit budget and document situation, then use it for 12-18 months before upgrading.

Picking your first credit card with an ITIN is one of the most consequential financial decisions you will make as a newcomer to the U.S. The right choice gets your credit file started immediately. The wrong choice, or worse, waiting, means months of history you can never recover. This guide walks through exactly how to choose: not just which card tops a ranking, but which type fits your specific situation right now.

Why does my first ITIN credit card choice matter so much?

A question we hear often: “Does it really matter which card I pick first, or should I just grab whatever approves me?”

It matters more than most people realize. The clock on your credit history starts the moment you open your first account, and every month you wait is a month of history you can never get back. Your first card will likely be open the longest, so it will anchor the “length of credit history” factor on your profile for years.

Two practical reasons your choice matters beyond just getting approved. First, some cards report to only one credit bureau, which builds a thinner file than cards that report to all three. The cards that serve ITIN holders best report to Experian, Equifax, and TransUnion. Second, fees and deposit requirements vary widely, and a card with a high annual fee or a poor graduation track record can trap you in a starter product longer than necessary.

With consistent on-time payments and utilization under 30%, most ITIN cardholders reach a scoreable credit file within 3-6 months and a 700-plus score within 12-18 months. That timeline is realistic, but only if you start with a card that actually reports and fits your documents.

Should my first ITIN card be secured or unsecured?

The honest answer: it depends on how much U.S. financial history you already have.

Unsecured options like Zolve are the strongest choice for applicants who want a card with no deposit from day one, particularly immigrants relocating on a student or work visa who want to begin credit building before they arrive. But unsecured cards for ITIN holders usually require some verifiable income or U.S. bank account history. If you landed recently with neither, you may not clear their alternative underwriting models yet.

Secured cards require a refundable cash deposit, but approval is far more predictable. The Capital One Quicksilver Secured is the best all-around secured card for ITIN holders: the $200 minimum deposit is accessible, the 1.5% flat cash-back rate earns rewards while you build, and there is no annual fee. If you cannot put up $200 right now, the Current Build Card charges no deposit at all. Current opens a spending account, you deposit any amount, and it reports your balance activity to the bureaus as credit usage. Because the card never advances actual credit, there is no hard pull, and Current accepts an ITIN or a valid government ID during verification.

A practical rule: if you have been in the U.S. for fewer than three months and have no existing U.S. bank account, go secured first. After 6-12 months of clean payment history, you will have a file strong enough to qualify for unsecured cards that accept ITIN.

What if I have no deposit money right now?

Readers frequently ask: “I just arrived and my savings are tight. Can I still get a first card?”

Yes, and you have two real options. The Current Build Card requires no security deposit and has no annual fee. A brand-new ITIN holder with no U.S. history can have a reporting tradeline on their credit file within 30 days of approval. That is one of the fastest on-ramps available.

The second no-deposit path is Zolve, which is designed for immigrants on F-1, H-1B, and similar visas. A NerdWallet April 2026 review confirmed that Zolve accepts alternative identification, including offer letters and pay slips, in place of an SSN. If you do not yet have an ITIN but have an employer offer letter or student enrollment documents, Zolve may approve you before your ITIN even arrives.

If neither option works and you can scrape together $200, OpenSky is worth considering because it skips the credit check entirely. Most credit card applications trigger a hard inquiry that can temporarily lower your score by 5-15 points. OpenSky performs no credit check at all, meaning zero score impact when you apply.

Does it matter whether I apply online or in person?

For some issuers, yes. For Capital One and Discover, applying in person at a branch significantly improves approval odds for ITIN holders. The online system often cannot process ITIN numbers correctly. If you are going the bank route for your first card, plan for an in-branch visit. Bring your passport, ITIN letter, proof of U.S. address (utility bill, lease, or bank statement), and one or two months of bank statements.

Fintech cards work the opposite way. Firstcard, Zolve, and Current Build are designed to be applied for online or through a mobile app. Their systems handle immigrant documentation from the start, and Firstcard and Zolve typically approve within 24-48 hours. If visiting a branch is difficult, a fintech card is a practical first card even if it means a slightly smaller reward structure.

For more detail on this decision, see our guide on applying online vs. in branch with an ITIN.

How should I actually use my first ITIN card to build credit?

Once approved, the strategy matters as much as the card itself. Payment history accounts for 35% of your FICO score, followed by credit utilization at 30%, which means every on-time payment carries real weight, especially early on.

The practical playbook for your first 12-18 months:

Keep utilization under 30%. If your limit is $1,000, keep your balance under $300. Many experienced ITIN cardholders target under 10% for maximum impact.

Pay in full every month. Carrying a balance does not help your score and will cost you interest. Automatic full-payment settings eliminate the risk of a forgotten due date.

Do not apply for more cards in the first 6 months. Multiple applications trigger multiple inquiries and complicate things early on. Stick with one card for your first 12 months.

After 6-12 months of clean history, you can explore a credit limit increase or start looking at upgrading from secured to unsecured.

What documents do I need to apply for my first ITIN card?

A question we hear often: “I have my ITIN letter but I’m not sure what else they’ll ask for.”

The document checklist varies slightly by issuer, but the standard set for a first ITIN credit card application is:

  • Your ITIN (the IRS letter showing your 9-digit number)
  • Passport or government-issued photo ID
  • U.S. mailing address (utility bill, lease, or bank statement)
  • Proof of income or bank statements (required by most issuers; some fintechs skip this)

For student visa applicants, documents typically needed include your passport, F-1 visa, I-20 form, U.S. address, proof of income if required, and ITIN or SSN if required by that issuer.

The Credit CARD Act of 2009 requires applicants under 21 to show independent income or have a co-signer. Part-time jobs, stipends, and work-study all count.

FAQs: First credit card with an ITIN

Can I get my first credit card with just an ITIN and no U.S. credit history? Yes. Several issuers, including Capital One, Bank of America, Citi, OpenSky, Firstcard, and Current Build Card, accept an ITIN in place of an SSN and do not require any prior U.S. credit history for approval.

Is a secured or unsecured card better as my first ITIN credit card? A secured card is easier to get approved for when you have zero U.S. credit history. Unsecured ITIN cards like Petal 2 or Zolve are available but typically require some verifiable U.S. bank or income history. Start secured if you just arrived; consider unsecured after 6-12 months.

How much deposit do I need for my first secured credit card with an ITIN? Most secured cards require a minimum deposit of $200 to $300, which becomes your credit limit. Some options, like the Current Build Card, require no deposit at all. OpenSky lets you set your own limit between $200 and $3,000.

How long does it take to get a usable credit score after opening my first ITIN credit card? Most ITIN holders get a scoreable credit file within 3-6 months of opening their first card. A score of 700 or higher typically takes 12-18 months of consistent on-time payments and low credit utilization.

Does my first ITIN credit card need to report to all three bureaus? Yes, this matters a great deal. A card that only reports to one bureau builds a thinner file. Confirm before applying that your card reports to Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion. All the cards listed in this guide do.
