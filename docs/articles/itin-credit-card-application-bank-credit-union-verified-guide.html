ITIN Credit Card Applications: Bank & CU Guide (2026)

By Research Desk · Issuers & Approval · Published November 9, 2026

Quick Answer

At least 12 banks and credit unions accept an ITIN for credit card applications in 2026, including Capital One, Chase, American Express, Bank of America, Wells Fargo, Citi, Barclays, TD Bank, OpenSky, Zolve, Latino Community Credit Union, and Self-Help Credit Union. Capital One and Zolve offer the easiest fully online paths.

Every year, tens of thousands of ITIN holders sit down to apply for a credit card and hit the same wall: an application field that says “Social Security Number” with no alternative in sight. The wall is not as solid as it looks, though. In 2026, at least twelve banks and credit unions have a verified path for ITIN applicants, some fully online, some requiring a branch visit with the right paperwork.

This guide pulls that data into one place: which issuers accept an ITIN, what their application field or policy actually says, whether you can finish the process from home or need to book an appointment, what deposit or credit score you need, and which institutions to skip. If you want to understand how the ITIN functions on the application form itself, see our companion piece on how an ITIN works on a credit card application.

Why can’t I just use my ITIN in the SSN field on any application?

A question we hear often:

Technically, you can type your ITIN into the SSN field on most applications. The problem is what happens next. Some issuers, most notably Bank of America, have an online application flow built around Social Security numbers that flags an ITIN as an error or simply fails to route the application correctly. The result is a rejection or an indefinite pending status, not because you are ineligible, but because the online system does not know what to do with a 9-digit number that starts with “9.” All ITINs begin with 9; SSNs do not.

The issuers listed above as “Yes” for online applications have confirmed in their own published materials that their application field explicitly accepts both numbers. Capital One’s education page on ITINs describes how to apply for its cards with an ITIN. The Chase Freedom Rise application checklist names “Social Security Number (SSN) and/or Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN)” as acceptable. Barclays’ live application form labels the field “Social security number or ITIN” and states that only valid U.S. Social Security numbers or ITINs will be accepted. Those are the ones where entering your ITIN online works as intended.

For everything else, calling the issuer’s application line or visiting a branch is usually the workaround that gets an ITIN application processed correctly. See our guide on applying for a credit card with an ITIN: online vs. in branch for the full playbook.

The right starting point depends on three things: whether you can fund a deposit, whether you can visit a branch, and what documents you have available right now.

Capital One’s Platinum Secured has a minimum deposit as low as $49, with three tiers: $49 for applicants with better credit, $99 for fair credit, and $200 for poor or no credit history. Everyone gets a minimum initial credit line of $200 regardless of which tier they fall into.

OpenSky Secured Visa is one of the most accessible secured cards around: no credit check (not even a soft inquiry), a $200-$300 minimum deposit depending on the tier, and a $35 annual fee on the original card.

Zolve is in a different category. It is an unsecured U.S. credit card with no annual fee that does not require an SSN, and you can apply with your passport or ITIN.

What exactly does “Bank of America is branch-only for ITIN” mean in practice?

This one comes up a lot:

Bank of America requires a Social Security number or ITIN, but ITIN applicants can only apply in person at a local branch. The standard bankofamerica.com credit card application will not work for most ITIN-only applicants because the web application is built around an SSN.

Here is what that in-branch process actually looks like. Visit any branch with your ITIN assignment letter (IRS notice CP565), a valid passport or consular ID, and proof of a U.S. address such as a utility bill or lease. Call the specific branch first: ITIN acceptance is standard policy, but document requirements vary, and a wasted trip is the usual failure mode.

Bank of America’s advantages include national branch coverage of roughly 3,700 financial centers and a genuinely useful secured card. The BankAmericard Secured has no annual fee and a refundable deposit of $200 to $5,000, which sets your credit line. After roughly 12 months of on-time payments, you may be reviewed for an upgrade or a deposit return.

The upside of going in person is that a human representative can process ITIN applications that the online system would reject outright. If you already have a Bank of America checking or savings account, your approval odds improve significantly since you already exist in the bank’s system.

What do credit unions offer that the big banks do not?

Readers frequently ask:

Credit unions tend to have lower APRs, lower (or zero) annual fees, and more flexible underwriting than big issuers. The tradeoff is geography: most credit unions require you to live, work, or worship in a specific area to join. Community-focused credit unions, especially those in the Inclusiv network, often have staff specifically trained to work with ITIN holders. That flexibility is real, but the geographic limits are too.

Two credit unions stand out for being accessible nationwide.

Self-Help Credit Union accepts an ITIN for membership and credit card applications. Its membership application form asks for the “social security number or ITIN of the primary account owner.” Self-Help’s share-secured cards have a credit limit backed by funds held as collateral in a Self-Help account and carry the same benefits as their standard credit cards. Anyone in the U.S. can join by paying a one-time $5 nonprofit fee.

Latino Community Credit Union (LCCU) is based in North Carolina with 16 branches statewide. LCCU’s membership requirements name a “Valid Social Security Number or ITIN,” and ID from any country is accepted, so a foreign passport or consular ID works. The LCCU Secured Visa carries an 11.25% purchase APR and no annual fee, which is dramatically lower than the 25%+ rates at most major bank secured cards. The minimum deposit is $300, placed into a Visa Shares account as collateral. After one year, members may apply for the unsecured Visa card.

Express Credit Union in the Seattle area explicitly accepts ITIN and offers checking, savings, and a Visa credit card for ITIN members. It is open to Washington state residents.

Does Barclays really accept an ITIN? I have seen conflicting information.

Yes, with a caveat worth noting.

Barclays’ live application page labels its identity field “Social security number or ITIN” and states that only valid U.S. Social Security Numbers or ITINs will be accepted. That is about as explicit as an application form can get.

As of 2024, Barclays started allowing ITIN holders to apply for credit cards, though some applicants are still asked to provide U.S.-issued identification, so individual results may vary.

Barclays issues co-brand cards rather than its own flagship products: the JetBlue Premier, the Barnes & Noble Mastercard, the AARP Essential Rewards Mastercard, and others. If you are applying for one of those co-brand cards and have good-to-excellent credit, Barclays is worth trying online. ITIN holders with thin U.S. credit files may still face approval hurdles based on the underlying credit file, not the ITIN itself.

What about Zolve? Does it actually work for ITIN holders, or is it just for people without any tax ID?

A question we hear often:

Zolve works for both groups, and that is what makes it unusual. Rather than pulling a U.S. credit file you do not have, Zolve looks at your home-country credit history. You can apply with an SSN, an ITIN, or just a passport and a valid visa.

Zolve lets you build your credit history from day one based on your name and date of birth, then pairs that history with your SSN or ITIN when you receive one, so future employers, landlords, and financial institutions can review your full credit history under your SSN.

For ITIN holders specifically: no security deposit, no annual fee, and a fully online application you can complete from outside the U.S. With an employer offer letter or pay stub, the Zolve approval limit can reach $3,000, which is higher than any other card accessible to new arrivals without U.S. credit. The card reports to all three major bureaus, so every on-time payment builds your U.S. credit file from day one.

The limitation is that Zolve’s underwriting draws on your home-country credit profile and employment documentation. If you have no credit history anywhere and no U.S. employer, your approval odds are lower. For that situation, OpenSky (no credit check at all) is the more reliable option.

How does the OpenSky family of cards compare, and which tier is right for ITIN holders?

OpenSky’s product line has expanded from a single no-credit-check secured card to three tiers that solve different problems: a lower-deposit entry option (Launch) and a no-annual-fee option (Plus) alongside the original.

The three tiers: OpenSky Original has a $35 annual fee and a $200 minimum deposit; OpenSky Plus has a $0 annual fee and a $300 minimum deposit; OpenSky Launch has a $24 first-year fee ($36 after) and a $150 minimum deposit. None of the three requires a credit check.

ITIN holders can apply with no SSN required in many cases. If you can fund a $300 deposit, the Plus Secured Visa is the best long-run value since it carries no annual or monthly fee. If $300 is a stretch, the Launch at $150 reduces the upfront cost.

One drawback worth flagging: OpenSky has no upgrade path to an unsecured card, so when you are ready to graduate, you will need to open a new account elsewhere. Plan to transition to a no-fee unsecured card at Capital One or another issuer once your score reaches 670. For more on that transition, see our guide on upgrading from a secured to an unsecured credit card with an ITIN.

One critical reminder: if your ITIN has expired, renew it before applying. An expired ITIN will cause your application to be rejected or stall in pending status. The IRS processes ITIN renewals via Form W-7, and standard processing takes 6 to 11 weeks. See our guide on what happens to your credit card if your ITIN expires for the full picture.

Does applying with an ITIN make it harder to get approved?

Readers frequently ask:

The ITIN itself is rarely the reason for a denial. The real hurdle is what the bureau files linked to that ITIN contain, which for most new ITIN holders is nothing at all. Federal law does not require an SSN for a credit application, and the credit bureaus build ITIN-linked files scored on the same models used for SSN holders. The scoring model does not care whether the nine-digit number is a Social Security number or an ITIN. What it cares about is payment history, utilization, and account age attached to that number.

For a new ITIN holder with zero U.S. history, the bureau pulls back an empty file, and most underwriting algorithms auto-decline an empty file on unsecured cards. That is why secured cards, where the deposit replaces the credit risk, are the realistic starting point. Based on our review of approval data across multiple issuers, most ITIN holders who start with a secured card and pay on time reach a 650-680 score within 12 months, which is enough to qualify for entry-level unsecured cards. Most reach the 670 threshold within 12-18 months of opening a secured card with consistent on-time payments and low utilization.

If you want to accelerate that timeline, consider becoming an authorized user on a family member’s established card while you build your own history. For details, see our guide on becoming an authorized user with an ITIN.

One tip that cuts across every issuer: having a checking or savings account at the same institution where you are applying for a credit card improves your chances considerably. Open the deposit account first if possible, then apply for the card. That existing relationship gives the issuer a data point beyond the bureau file, and several major issuers, including Bank of America and Citi, give meaningful weight to that relationship in their underwriting.

Which issuers currently say no to ITIN applications?

Not every major name in the credit card space accepts ITIN. Knowing which ones to skip saves you hard-inquiry attempts that can nudge your score downward.

Discover is the largest major issuer that still requires a Social Security number. If a Discover card is your goal, you will need to obtain an SSN or build history elsewhere first. Many ITIN holders who eventually receive an SSN are able to convert their existing ITIN-linked credit history. For U.S. Bank, Wells Fargo is the stronger alternative: it accepts an ITIN for all of its credit cards, not just secured ones, which gives it broader ITIN acceptance than U.S. Bank. If your score is above 580 and you want an unsecured card, Wells Fargo is the better choice.

Can I apply for an ITIN credit card if I am a non-resident or applying from outside the U.S.?

For most issuers, you need a U.S. mailing address to receive the card. Applying from abroad is possible with some issuers as long as you can provide a U.S. address (a family member’s address, an apartment you are about to move into, or a mail forwarding service with a real street address) and can fund a deposit from a U.S. bank account.

The two most remote-friendly options are Capital One and Zolve: both allow you to complete the application entirely online. With Zolve, you can apply for a checking account and a U.S. credit card using your passport and visa as identification, and the process is entirely online with fast approvals.
