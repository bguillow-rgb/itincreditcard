How to Add an Authorized User to Your ITIN Credit Card

By Editorial Team · Cards & Credit Building · Published August 28, 2026

Quick Answer

Yes, ITIN holders who are primary cardholders can add authorized users to their credit card accounts. Most major issuers, including Chase and Capital One, allow it. The authorized user typically needs only a name and date of birth, though some issuers also ask for their SSN or ITIN.

If you hold a credit card with an ITIN and want to help a spouse, child, or trusted family member start building U.S. credit, you have more options than you might expect. Adding an authorized user is one of the most overlooked tools available to ITIN cardholders, and it works much the same way it does for SSN holders.

Can I really add someone to my account if my card is under an ITIN?

A question we hear often: ITIN holders sometimes assume their accounts carry restrictions that SSN accounts do not. In practice, once a card is open and in good standing, most issuers treat the primary cardholder’s tax ID type as irrelevant when it comes to adding users. What the bank cares about is whether the account is in good standing, not whether it was opened with a Social Security number or an ITIN.

An authorized user is someone permitted to make purchases on another person’s credit card account. They can get a card with their name on it and access to the primary cardholder’s credit line, but they are not responsible for making payments. That legal distinction is exactly why issuers do not need to run a credit check on the person being added.

The practical result: if your ITIN card is open, the normal authorized user rules apply. You can add a family member, a spouse, or any trusted person by contacting your issuer.

What information does my issuer need to add an authorized user?

This varies more than most cardholders expect, and the differences matter for ITIN households where the person being added may not have an SSN.

Chase typically requires only the authorized user’s name, birthday, address, and relationship to the primary cardholder. Barclays asks for name, birthday, address, relationship, and the citizenship status of the authorized user.

Discover, by contrast, requires the primary cardholder to provide the first and last name, address, date of birth, and Social Security number for all authorized users. That effectively means someone without an SSN cannot be added as a Discover authorized user. American Express similarly requires an SSN or ITIN, generally at card activation.

How do I actually add the authorized user to my account?

This one comes up a lot: the process itself is simpler than the research.

Contact your card issuer first. You can usually add an authorized user through your online account, mobile app, or by calling customer service. Look for account management or card member settings.

Once you find the right section, gather what your issuer needs. For Chase or Capital One, you will likely need only the person’s full legal name and date of birth. For American Express, also have the person’s ITIN or SSN ready. Submit the request, and a physical card will be mailed to the address on file for your account.

One practical note: the authorized user does not need to be physically present or even in the same country when you add them. Once the primary cardholder signs off, the authorized user gets a card in their name linked to the primary account. The authorized user will likely not receive a monthly statement. You remain the one responsible for paying the bill in full each month.

Will this help the authorized user build credit?

Readers frequently ask: this is usually the whole point of the exercise, so it deserves a thorough answer.

Authorized users can build credit. The account’s payment history, credit limit, and balance typically appear on both people’s credit reports. Every on-time payment you make as the primary cardholder gets recorded on the authorized user’s credit file, which is a real shortcut for someone starting from zero.

Most major issuers, including Chase, American Express, Bank of America, and Capital One, report authorized user activity to the credit bureaus. Smaller banks and credit unions may not. Confirm with your issuer before adding someone.

One important nuance: the credit benefit flows from the primary account to the authorized user, not the other way around. If you have a strong on-time payment history and low utilization, the authorized user inherits that positive history. If you carry a high balance or miss payments, that negative information also appears on the authorized user’s file. According to the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB), payment history is one of the most heavily weighted factors in scoring models, so the stakes run both ways.

The authorized user does not even need to actively use the card to benefit. Simply being listed on a healthy, well-managed account can help establish or thicken a credit file within one to two billing cycles.

Does adding an authorized user affect my credit score as the primary cardholder?

Adding a user does not affect the primary cardholder’s credit score. There is no hard inquiry, and adding a name to your account does not count as a new account for scoring purposes.

The indirect risk is utilization. Many issuers let you set a sub-limit for the authorized user’s card, capping how much they can charge in a billing cycle. Ask your issuer whether that option is available, and arrange spending limits that keep total combined charges from pushing up your utilization ratio.

A good rule of thumb: keep total combined spending below 30% of your credit limit at all times, and ideally below 10% if you want to maximize the positive credit scoring impact. If you are working to build credit alongside the authorized user, read our guide on how to build credit fast with an ITIN credit card for a broader strategy.

What if my authorized user doesn’t have a U.S. address or any ID at all?

This scenario is common in mixed-status households and among ITIN holders whose close family members still live abroad.

For issuers that do not require an SSN or ITIN, such as Chase, the authorized user does not need a U.S. address at the time of addition. The physical card will ship to your address on file, so you would need to forward it. Some issuers also offer virtual card numbers for authorized users through their apps, which can be useful before the physical card arrives.

Some issuers have minimum age requirements for authorized users and a cap on how many users you can add to a single account. Discover, for example, requires authorized users to be at least 15 years old. Confirm both the age floor and the maximum number of users allowed before adding multiple people.

If the authorized user also holds an ITIN and wants to understand their side of this relationship, including how the account history flows to their credit file, our guide on becoming an authorized user with an ITIN covers that perspective in detail.

Can I remove an authorized user later if things change?

Yes, and this is worth knowing before you add anyone. An authorized user can request removal by calling the card issuer directly. The primary cardholder can also initiate removal at any time.

Once removed, the account history that was flowing to the authorized user’s credit report stops updating. Depending on the bureau and the issuer’s reporting practices, the historical tradeline may remain on the authorized user’s report for up to seven to ten years, or it may be removed immediately. This is worth discussing with the authorized user before you add them, because a sudden removal of a positive tradeline can temporarily lower their score if it was their primary source of credit history.

Frequently asked questions

Can an ITIN holder add an authorized user to their credit card? Yes. Being the primary cardholder with an ITIN does not prevent you from adding an authorized user. The issuer’s authorized user rules apply the same way they do for SSN holders. Requirements vary by bank, but most only need the user’s name and date of birth.

Does the authorized user I add need an SSN or ITIN? It depends on the issuer. Chase and Capital One typically require only the authorized user’s name and date of birth, making it possible to add someone without an SSN or ITIN. American Express and Discover usually require an SSN or ITIN for identity verification.

Will adding an authorized user affect my credit score as an ITIN holder? Adding a user does not directly affect your credit score. However, if the authorized user spends heavily and raises the card’s utilization ratio, that higher utilization can indirectly lower your score. Keep combined spending well below the credit limit to protect your profile.

Will the authorized user’s account activity appear on my credit report? No, but your account history will appear on the authorized user’s credit report, which is the whole point of the arrangement. Your payment behavior, credit limit, and balance all get reported to the bureaus under the authorized user’s profile.

Can I add an authorized user who lives outside the United States? Some issuers allow it, but the card will be mailed to your U.S. address on file. Chase and Capital One do not require the authorized user to have a U.S. address at the time of addition. Check with your specific issuer before proceeding, as policies vary.
