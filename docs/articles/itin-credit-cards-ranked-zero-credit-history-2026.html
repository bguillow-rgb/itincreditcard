Why can’t I just apply for any credit card with my ITIN?

A question we hear often: An ITIN proves your U.S. tax identity, but it does not create a U.S. credit file. When a lender pulls your application, they see an empty report or no file at all. That is the core problem. Most rewards cards and unsecured cards require at least six to twelve months of U.S. credit history, and a thin or missing file triggers an automatic denial regardless of how strong your income is.

The workaround is a secured card. You post a refundable cash deposit, the issuer uses that as collateral instead of relying on credit history, then reports your payment behavior to the bureaus every month. After six to twelve months of on-time payments you typically have a scoreable file, and consistent on-time payments with low utilization can get you to 650-700 within 12-18 months.

One thing worth knowing: your ITIN does not slow the process. Once an account is opened using your ITIN, it reports to the bureaus the same way as any other account. Payment history, utilization, and account age all factor into your score identically, whether you used an ITIN or an SSN.

What is the single easiest credit card to get with an ITIN and zero U.S. credit?

If the goal is maximum approval certainty with minimum friction, two cards stand apart.

OpenSky Secured Visa: the no-credit-check standard

The OpenSky Secured is one of the few secured cards that openly approves applicants without an SSN. An ITIN works, there’s no credit check during the application, and no bank account is required at the time you apply. You make a refundable security deposit between $200 and $3,000, and that amount becomes your starting credit limit. Activity reports to all three bureaus.

The trade-off is a $35 annual fee and a 3% foreign transaction fee. Both are worth accepting if your only alternative is a denial.

Capital One Platinum Secured: the major-bank starting point

Capital One is one of the most ITIN-friendly major U.S. banks. They maintain a published resource on their own website titled “How to get a credit card with an ITIN,” and the Platinum Secured consistently appears on expert-reviewed lists as one of the most accessible ITIN-accepting cards at a major institution.

No annual fee, and eligible applicants can put down as little as $49 or $99 rather than a full $200 for a $200 starting line. That lower barrier matters when money is tight.

How does the Capital One Platinum Secured deposit system actually work?

This one comes up a lot: The deposit model here is genuinely different from most secured cards, and it’s one of the main reasons we rank it first for ITIN starters.

Most secured cards require your deposit to match your credit limit dollar for dollar. Capital One’s version has a twist: depending on your creditworthiness, you may qualify for a $200 credit limit with a deposit of only $49, $99, or $200. You have up to 80 days to build the deposit before activation, in electronic fund transfer increments of at least $20.

On the upgrade path: after activation, the credit limit is locked in until Capital One automatically considers you for a higher one. That can happen in as little as five months with a strong on-time payment record. You do not have to add to your security deposit when your credit line increases.

The main downside is that the card earns no rewards. If you can comfortably fund a $200 deposit and want cash back while building credit, the sibling Capital One Quicksilver Secured earns 1.5% cash back with the same $0 annual fee and a $200 flat minimum deposit. Both cards accept ITIN.

Is Chase Freedom Rise actually approvable with an ITIN and no credit history?

Readers frequently ask: Yes, but with an important asterisk.

According to WalletHub, the Chase Freedom Rise is one of the best credit cards available without an SSN requirement. It earns 1.5% cash back on all purchases, has no annual fee, and accepts ITIN applicants with limited or no U.S. credit history. Freedom Rise is an unsecured starter card that pays a flat 1.5% plus a capped 3% on dining for the first six months. The $25 statement credit for enrolling in autopay within the first three months is a small but useful nudge toward good habits.

The real catch is approval. In practical terms: open a Chase checking or savings account, let it season for a month or two, then apply. That deposit relationship signals financial stability and meaningfully improves your odds when your U.S. credit file is empty.

There is no 0% intro APR. The ongoing rate is 18.49%-27.99% variable, which means carrying a balance gets expensive fast. Pay the full statement balance every month and this is one of the best no-deposit ITIN options on the market.

What about the Citi Secured Mastercard for ITIN holders?

Citi’s official 2026 guide confirms that an ITIN can be supplied for its secured-card application, either online or at a branch.

The Citi Secured Mastercard is designed for people new to credit. It charges no annual fee, includes FICO score access, and reports monthly to all three major bureaus.

The deposit terms deserve a close read. Your security deposit of $200 to $2,500 goes into a collateral holding account for 18 months and earns no interest during that time. That lock-up period is longer than most competitors, and Citi does not advertise an automatic graduation review timeline the way Capital One does. If having your deposit tied up for a year and a half concerns you, Capital One’s six-month upgrade review is a meaningful advantage.

The card charges a 26.74% variable APR, a 3% foreign transaction fee, and a 5% balance transfer fee with a $5 minimum. For an ITIN holder who already banks with Citi or prefers the Mastercard network for international use, this is a solid option. For everyone else, Capital One or OpenSky are more flexible starting points.

What happened to Discover it Secured, and is it still an option?

A question we hear often: Previously one of the most-recommended ITIN starter cards, the Discover it Secured is no longer available to new applicants.

Discover stopped accepting new applications on June 2, 2026. Existing cardholders are unaffected and their accounts remain open. The rewards and the $0 annual fee still hold up for current members, but new applications are closed.

If you were planning on Discover it Secured because of its cash-back rewards and graduation path, the closest functional replacements for ITIN holders in 2026 are:

  • Capital One Platinum Secured (zero annual fee, six-month upgrade review)
  • Capital One Quicksilver Secured (1.5% cash back, zero annual fee, $200 deposit)
  • OpenSky Plus Secured Visa (zero annual fee, no credit check, $300 deposit)

How long will it realistically take to build credit from zero with my ITIN card?

Readers frequently ask: Building credit is a timeline question, and the honest answer has two distinct phases.

Phase 1: Becoming visible (months 1-6)

According to Experian, most scoring models require at least one account that has been open for six months and at least one account reported to the bureau within the past six months before a score can be generated. That is why the 3-6 month window is standard: it’s the minimum activity threshold to produce a calculable score.

Your first credit score typically appears in the 600-650 range after 3-6 months, then reaches 650-700 with consistent good habits between months 6-12. VantageScore may generate a number sooner, sometimes within a month or two, but most lenders use FICO scores for approval decisions.

Phase 2: Reaching a usable score (months 6-24)

Starting with zero credit history, you can establish credit in as little as six months. A good credit score of 700 or better usually requires timely payments for at least 18 months to two years.

With clean execution, good credit (670+) commonly arrives within about 12 months and 700+ within 12-24 months. Authorized-user history on an old, clean account compresses the timeline. Getting to 800+ takes years because account age can only be earned by waiting.

The practical implication: open your starter ITIN card now, use it for small recurring purchases, and pay the full statement balance every month. Don’t carry a balance. Don’t miss a payment. After 6-12 months you will have a score, and after 12-18 months you will likely qualify for unsecured cards with real rewards. See our guide on how to build credit fast with an ITIN credit card for the month-by-month playbook.

What documents do I need to apply for a secured ITIN credit card?

This one comes up a lot: The document list is shorter than most people expect.

You apply online with your ITIN, a foreign passport, a U.S. mailing address, and a U.S. bank account to fund any deposit. For OpenSky specifically, no bank account is required for the application itself, though you will need one to fund your deposit after approval.

Here is the standard document checklist across major ITIN-accepting issuers:

  1. ITIN number (IRS Form W-7 letter or card)
  2. Foreign passport (most widely accepted photo ID)
  3. U.S. mailing address (P.O. boxes are generally not accepted)
  4. U.S. bank or fintech account (to fund deposit, if secured)
  5. Proof of income (pay stubs, offer letter, bank statements; not always required but helps)

One application tip: most major issuers let you enter your ITIN in the SSN field on their online form, or provide an option to indicate you have an ITIN rather than an SSN. Most Chase, Amex, and Capital One applications include an option to enter an ITIN or select “I don’t have an SSN.” You can also apply in person at a branch.

For a complete walkthrough of the online vs. in-branch application decision, see our dedicated guide on applying for a credit card with an ITIN online vs. in branch.

What are the biggest mistakes ITIN holders make with their first credit card?

Understanding what breaks the credit-building process is as useful as knowing what speeds it up.

Carrying a balance month to month. Every card on this list has an APR above 20%. At 23-30% variable, a $200 balance that takes three months to pay off costs $15-20 in interest and does nothing positive for your credit history. Pay the full statement balance every cycle, not just the minimum.

Applying for multiple cards at once. Each application usually triggers a hard inquiry, which temporarily dents the thin credit file you are trying to build. Start with one card, let it season for 6-12 months, then consider adding a second. Our guide on having multiple credit cards with an ITIN covers the right timing.

Maxing out the credit limit. Your credit utilization ratio is how much of your available limit you are using. Most credit experts recommend staying under 30%, and under 10% for the fastest score gains. On a $200 limit, that means keeping your balance below $60 at the time the statement closes.

Closing the card too soon. The length of your credit history matters. Keep your first card open even after you graduate to a better one, as long as there is no annual fee eating into your budget.

I got denied everywhere. What is my absolute last resort?

A question we hear often: If you have been denied by Capital One, Citi, and Chase, and you cannot fund the $200 deposit required by OpenSky, there are a few remaining paths.

Current Build Card: The Current Build Card has a $0 annual fee, requires no minimum deposit, requires no credit check, earns 1 point per dollar on eligible categories, and reports to Experian, TransUnion, and Equifax. Most immigrant-friendly cards still require a Social Security Number or a hefty security deposit. Current sidesteps both. You open a free Current spending account, deposit any amount, and the card reports your balance activity to the bureaus as credit usage. Because the card never advances actual credit, there is no hard pull and no SSN requirement at sign-up. Current accepts ITIN or a valid government ID during verification.

Self Visa Credit Card: The Self Visa Credit Card charges a $25 annual fee (first year $0) and accepts ITIN applicants who already have a Self Credit Builder Account. It reports to all three bureaus and is best for ITIN applicants who want both an installment tradeline and a revolving card in one setup. You must make three on-time payments into the Credit Builder Account and have at least $100 saved before you can activate the card.

Kikoff Secured Credit Card: The Kikoff Secured Credit Card requires a low refundable deposit, uses no hard credit pull, and accepts ITIN during account opening. It is best for first-time borrowers who want a small line to practice responsible use.

None of these is a long-term card, but all three can give you the six months of bureau-reported activity you need to become scoreable. At that point, Capital One or Chase becomes accessible.

How do I choose between these cards? A decision framework

Use this simple flowchart logic before applying:

Step 1: Do you have at least $200 to lock up in a refundable deposit?

  • Yes: Go to Step 2
  • No: Start with the Current Build Card or Self Visa to build a thin file, then revisit in six months

Step 2: Do you have a Chase checking account?

  • Yes: Apply for Chase Freedom Rise first (no deposit, 1.5% cash back)
  • No: Go to Step 3

Step 3: Are you comfortable with a $35 annual fee in exchange for guaranteed approval?

  • Yes: OpenSky Secured Visa (no credit check, fast approval)
  • No: Go to Step 4

Step 4: Default pick is Capital One Platinum Secured ($49-$200 deposit, $0 annual fee, major-bank reporting, six-month upgrade review). Use Capital One’s pre-qualification tool to check eligibility before submitting a hard-pull application.
