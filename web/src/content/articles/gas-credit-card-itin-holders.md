ITIN holders can earn rewards at the gas station. The key is matching the right card to your current credit profile: a secured card if you are still building your U.S. file, or an unsecured rewards card once your score is established. This guide covers every option worth considering, what each card actually earns at the pump, and which issuers confirm they accept an ITIN.

## Can I actually get a gas rewards credit card with just an ITIN?

*A question we hear often:*

Yes, and the options are better than most people expect. Some credit card issuers let applicants use ITINs instead of SSNs. The challenge is not eligibility, it is knowing which cards combine meaningful gas rewards with confirmed ITIN acceptance.

The short answer: Capital One accepts ITINs across its full card lineup, including secured cards that earn flat cash back at every merchant, gas stations included. American Express also accepts an ITIN and has cards built around gas and grocery rewards, though those cards generally expect a U.S. credit score above 670. If you are starting from zero, the Capital One path is the more realistic first step. If you already have 12-18 months of U.S. credit history under your ITIN, the Amex options become genuinely reachable. See our [secured vs. unsecured comparison](/articles/secured-vs-unsecured-credit-card-itin-comparison) for more context on which card type fits your stage.

## Which card should I start with if I have no U.S. credit history yet?

*This one comes up a lot:*

The Capital One Quicksilver Secured is the strongest option for ITIN holders starting from scratch: 1.5% cash back on every purchase, no annual fee, and an automatic account review for upgrade around month six. That flat rate applies at gas stations the same as everywhere else, so you are earning while you build.

The mechanics work like any other secured card. You place a refundable deposit (minimum $200), and that deposit becomes your credit limit. Secured cards report to all three credit bureaus the same way unsecured cards do, so your score builds at the same rate either way. You are not being penalized for using a secured product; it is simply the fastest legitimate path to the unsecured gas rewards cards.

Capital One's Platinum Secured and Quicksilver Secured both have a path to return the deposit and convert to an unsecured account with responsible use, and the documented product language does not require an SSN for that upgrade step. That matters for ITIN holders who want confirmation they are not locked into a secured product permanently.

One important note: Capital One's pre-qualification tool lets you gauge your odds with a soft inquiry before committing. Worth doing, given that credit card applications get rejected around 22% of the time, according to the latest data from the Federal Reserve Bank of New York. Always pre-qualify before submitting a formal application.

## When can I upgrade to a card that earns 3% back at gas stations?

*Readers frequently ask:*

The Amex Blue Cash Everyday is the top destination for ITIN holders who want maximum gas rewards without paying an annual fee. American Express's application interface states: "If you do not have an SSN, you can enter your ITIN here instead."

In practice, an existing U.S. credit file is effectively required, so treat the Blue Cash Everyday as a possible second card rather than the default first card for a blank file.

The timeline most ITIN holders follow: start with the Quicksilver Secured, pay the balance in full every month (keeping utilization below 30% of your limit), and after 12-18 months you will typically have a score in the high 600s or low 700s. That is the range where Amex applications start converting. The Blue Cash Everyday and Blue Cash Preferred are the most realistic ITIN targets for applicants with 670+ scores.

If your score is in the fair range (580-669) and you want to skip the deposit, the Capital One QuicksilverOne unsecured card earns the same 1.5% flat rate with a $39 annual fee. That fee works out to roughly $3.25 per month, so you need to run at least $217 per month through the card to come out ahead versus a no-fee secured card. For most drivers who fill up weekly, that threshold is easy to clear.

## Why do most gas station branded cards not work for ITIN holders?

Most co-branded fuel retailer cards (Shell, BP, Chevron, and similar) are issued by banks like Synchrony or Comenity. Capital One, Chase, American Express, Bank of America, Wells Fargo, Citi, U.S. Bank (secured only), OpenSky, Zolve, Tilt, TD Bank, and Barclays all accept an ITIN on credit card applications, while Discover and Synchrony still require a Social Security number.

That means any gas station card issued through Synchrony is effectively off the table for ITIN holders. The practical workaround is a general-purpose network card (Visa, Mastercard, or Amex) that earns strong gas rewards, rather than a retailer-specific card that locks you to one fuel brand anyway. You end up with a more flexible card and broader acceptance.

## What documents do I need to apply for a gas rewards card with an ITIN?

*A question we hear often:*

The required documents are largely the same across issuers that accept ITINs. You will typically need a valid ITIN and your ITIN assignment letter (CP-565), plus a foreign passport for identity verification. For secured cards, you also need a U.S. bank account to fund the deposit.

A few practical notes that reduce friction:

- Enter your ITIN in the SSN field. Most online applications have a single tax ID field. An ITIN is formatted exactly like an SSN but always starts with the digit 9, so it drops right in.
- Bring your physical ITIN letter if applying in branch. The IRS letter is stronger proof than simply writing the number on a form.
- Use a residential street address, not a P.O. box. Issuers use address verification, and commercial mailbox addresses are a common rejection trigger.
- Check whether your ITIN is current. An expired ITIN is an easily overlooked reason for application errors. Renew with IRS Form W-7 before applying.

When that happens, your 1.5% gas cash back rate stays the same, the deposit comes back, and your credit limit typically increases. The card number may change, but the rewards program is continuous.

If you want to add a 3% gas card on top of your existing card rather than replacing it, that is also a viable approach. According to a 2026 Experian white paper, ITIN holders have a lower debt-to-income ratio and fewer accounts with payments past due compared to SSN holders. Issuers reviewing your file will see a healthy payment history. Running two cards, one for gas and one for everyday spending, is a common tactic for getting more out of your rewards.
