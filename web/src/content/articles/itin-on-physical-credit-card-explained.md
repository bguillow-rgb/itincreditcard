Your ITIN is used to open a credit card account, but it never shows up on the card itself. A lot of people assume the number is printed somewhere on the plastic (or virtual card), the way a Social Security number appears on an SSN card. That's not how it works. This guide explains exactly where your ITIN lives in the credit card process, what your card does show, and why the difference matters for your privacy and your applications.

## "Wait, so where is my ITIN on my actual credit card?"

*A question we hear often:* people expect to see their ITIN somewhere on the front or back of their new card, the same way a Social Security card displays an SSN. The answer is simple: it's not there at all.

An ITIN is a nine-digit number in the format 9XX-XX-XXXX, and the IRS confirms it is "a 9-digit number, beginning with the number 9, formatted like an SSN." That number is a tax identifier, not a payment credential. A physical or virtual credit card carries a completely different set of data:

| What appears on your card | What does NOT appear on your card |
|---|---|
| Your name | Your ITIN |
| 16-digit card account number | Your SSN (for anyone) |
| Expiration date (MM/YY) | Your date of birth |
| CVV / security code | Your address or income |
| Issuer logo and network logo | Tax identification of any kind |

None of the data on your card is connected to your ITIN. The card number is generated by the issuer's payment system and assigned to your account after approval. Your ITIN is collected during the application, verified internally, and stored in the issuer's secure records.

## "Then why does the application ask for it?"

*This one comes up a lot:* applicants sometimes wonder whether the ITIN field on a credit card application actually works, since the form often just says "Social Security Number."

When you apply for a credit card, some issuers allow people with ITINs to enter their ITIN in the SSN field; others don't. The reason issuers collect it at all is identity verification and credit-file lookup. An ITIN is a tax identification number, not an SSN, but many issuers accept it in the same field.

In practice, entering your ITIN in the SSN field is the right move. An ITIN follows the same nine-digit format as a Social Security number (XXX-XX-XXXX) and can be used in place of one on credit card applications, regardless of your immigration status.

Several major issuers now label the field explicitly. Citi's online application uses a single identity field labeled "Social Security Number or ITIN," and Barclays' application field reads "Social security number or ITIN." American Express takes a similar approach. Most Chase, Amex, and Capital One applications either include an option to select "I have an ITIN instead of an SSN" or simply let you type your ITIN directly into the SSN field.

## "What does my ITIN actually look like, so I know I'm entering it correctly?"

An Individual Taxpayer Identification Number has the same nine-digit structure as a Social Security number: three digits, a hyphen, two digits, a hyphen, four digits, written as 9XX-XX-XXXX. The first digit is always 9, which is the clearest sign you're looking at an ITIN rather than an SSN.

The fourth and fifth digits fall within specific ranges: 50 to 65, 70 to 88, 90 to 92, or 94 to 99, according to the American Immigration Council. If your number falls outside those middle-digit ranges, double-check your CP565 letter before submitting.

Enter your ITIN exactly as it appears on your CP565, in the format 9XX-XX-XXXX. Don't omit the hyphens when typing into an online form, and don't substitute zeroes or letters.

## "What is the CP565 letter and why do I need to keep it?"

*Readers frequently ask* what document they should bring to a branch if the banker wants to verify their ITIN, since there's no plastic card to show.

The IRS does not issue a physical ITIN card. You get a CP565 notice letter instead. The letter includes IRS letterhead, your name and mailing address, the notice number (CP565), and your nine-digit ITIN printed clearly in the body.

That letter is your primary proof of ITIN ownership. Keep it somewhere safe, and make a photocopy before any branch visit. If you've misplaced it, you can call the IRS at 1-800-829-1040 and request a replacement CP565.

One thing worth knowing: the CP565 letter itself does not expire, but the ITIN printed on it can become inactive. The letter still works as proof of your original assignment even after expiration and renewal.

## "Does my ITIN need to be active for my existing credit card to keep working?"

This is a genuinely important distinction. An expired ITIN does not automatically freeze or cancel a credit card account you already have open. The card's payment credentials (card number, CVV, expiration date) operate independently of the tax ID in the issuer's back-end records.

Where expiration does matter is in new applications and sometimes account reviews. An expired ITIN can stop you from filing your federal tax return, opening a new bank account, or applying for credit that requires an IRS-verified taxpayer ID. So if you plan to apply for a new card, request a credit limit increase, or add a product, renewing first is the smart move. See our guide on [what happens to your credit card when your ITIN expires](/articles/expired-itin-credit-card-what-happens) for the full picture.

Renewal is free. The IRS charges $0 for renewal via Form W-7 under Rev Proc 2015-35. In 2026, ITINs with middle digits 85, 86, and 87 expire on December 31. All ITINs with middle digits 70 through 84 are already expired. Check the middle two digits of your 9XX-XX-XXXX number to see where you stand.

After renewal, the IRS does not reissue a different number; it just refreshes the active status. Any credit accounts, bank accounts, or financial records tied to your ITIN don't need to be updated.

## "So my card looks the same as everyone else's?"

Exactly. Once you're approved, nothing on the physical card or in the account interface marks you as an ITIN holder. Experian, TransUnion, and Equifax can all create and maintain credit files using an ITIN. When you open a credit account with an ITIN and make payments, the lender reports that activity to the bureaus the same way they would for someone with an SSN.

Every on-time payment, every month you keep your balance low, every year you keep the account open: it all builds the same kind of U.S. credit history that any SSN holder builds. The ITIN gets you in the door. What you do after that is what actually determines where you end up. ## Frequently asked questions

**Is my ITIN printed on my physical credit card?**
No. Your physical credit card shows only your name, 16-digit card number, expiration date, and CVV security code. The ITIN is collected during the application process and stored in the issuer's records; it never appears on the card itself.

**Where do I enter my ITIN on a credit card application?**
You enter your ITIN in the field labeled "Social Security Number" or "SSN/ITIN." It follows the same nine-digit format as an SSN (9XX-XX-XXXX), so it fits in the same box. Some issuers like Citi and Barclays explicitly label the field "Social Security Number or ITIN."

**Can someone see my ITIN by looking at my credit card?**
No. There is no way to determine your ITIN from your physical card. The card carries only payment-network data (card number, name, expiration, CVV). Your ITIN is held securely in the issuer's internal records.

**What document proves I have an ITIN if the number isn't on my card?**
Your IRS CP565 notice letter is the official proof of your ITIN. It is a full-page IRS letter that includes your name, address, and nine-digit ITIN. The IRS does not issue a plastic ITIN card; the CP565 is what you present to banks or issuers when proof is requested.

**Does an expired ITIN affect my existing credit card?**
An expired ITIN generally does not cancel or freeze an existing credit card. However, if you apply for a new card or a credit limit increase, the issuer may verify your ITIN with the IRS. Renewing via Form W-7 before it expires keeps everything running smoothly.
