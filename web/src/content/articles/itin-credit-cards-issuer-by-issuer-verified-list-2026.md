## What do I actually need to apply for a credit card with an ITIN?

*A question we hear often:* the documents list varies slightly by issuer, but the core stack is consistent across all of them.

Issuers that accept an ITIN typically ask for your ITIN assignment letter from the IRS or a recent tax return showing the ITIN, plus a valid passport, consular ID (such as a Mexican matricula), or a state-issued ID. Beyond those two items, you will also need a U.S. mailing address (a utility bill or lease works as proof) and a U.S. bank account if you are funding a secured deposit.

Use a real U.S. mobile number. Issuers text a one-time verification code during the application, and a VoIP number such as Google Voice will fail this check.

One practical tip that makes a real difference: having a checking or savings account at the same institution where you are applying dramatically improves your chances. This matters most at Chase, Bank of America, and Wells Fargo, where a pre-existing banking relationship signals stability to the underwriter.

## Which issuer is the easiest starting point if I have no U.S. credit history at all?

*This one comes up a lot:* for applicants starting from zero, the issuer landscape splits cleanly into two camps: those that run a credit check (most major banks) and those that do not.

Secured cards are easier to qualify for because they require a refundable deposit and typically carry lower credit limits than unsecured cards. Within the secured category, Capital One Platinum Secured stands out for ITIN holders because the entire application is online, the minimum deposit can be as low as $49, and Capital One reports to the major credit bureaus every month. After you make your first six payments on time, they may increase your credit limit with no additional deposit required.

For applicants who have been denied elsewhere or simply want the highest possible approval odds, OpenSky Secured is one of the few secured cards that openly accepts an ITIN with no credit check at all. The tradeoff is a $35 annual fee on the standard version, though the OpenSky Plus Secured Visa drops that fee entirely.

Editorial verdict: if you can fund a $200 deposit and want to start with a major issuer, Capital One Platinum Secured is the clearest path. If you have been denied before or prefer no credit check, OpenSky Plus (requires a $300 deposit, no annual fee) is the best no-friction alternative.

## Capital One: the ITIN-friendliest major bank

Capital One has a number of credit cards available for ITIN applicants, including the Platinum Secured, which allows an initial security deposit of $49, $99, or $200 to open an account with a credit line of at least $200.

Capital One's advantage over every other major bank is the fully online application. No branch visit required. You can use Capital One's soft-check pre-approval tool first to check eligibility without triggering a hard inquiry, then decide whether to submit a full application with your ITIN.

Two cards are the main ITIN starting points at Capital One.

Capital One Platinum Secured is the foundational credit-builder. A $49, $99, or $200 minimum deposit opens your account with an initial credit line of at least $200, and you can push that line higher by depositing more than the minimum, up to $1,000. By using your card responsibly and making on-time payments, you may be considered for a credit line increase in as little as six months. The card earns no rewards, but it costs nothing in annual fees and carries no foreign transaction fees. Once your profile strengthens, Capital One may automatically upgrade you to an unsecured Platinum card and return your deposit.

Capital One Quicksilver Secured is for ITIN holders who want to earn while they build. The credit limit equals your deposit, with a $200 minimum. It builds credit, earns rewards, accepts an ITIN, and requires a security deposit you can eventually get back if you maintain on-time payments and either close the card or get upgraded to the regular unsecured Quicksilver.

For established ITIN holders with a credit score around 650+, Capital One Savor is a realistic unsecured target. It is more flexible than Chase or Amex and sometimes approves scores in the 650-670 range.

## OpenSky: the no-credit-check secured card

*Readers frequently ask:* whether there is a credit card that approves ITIN holders regardless of credit history. OpenSky is the most consistent answer.

The OpenSky Secured Visa is a secured card with no credit check. You make a refundable security deposit between $200 and $3,000, and that amount sets your starting credit limit. Activity reports to all three major credit bureaus. The card charges a $35 annual fee and a 3% foreign transaction fee.

The no-annual-fee alternative is the OpenSky Plus Secured Visa. It requires a $300 minimum deposit (up to $3,000) and carries an ongoing variable APR of 28.24%. If you prefer the lower APR and can absorb the annual fee, the original $35-fee Secured Visa has a 23.89% APR, which is the cheapest of the three OpenSky options.

On the upgrade path: after roughly six months of on-time payments, eligible cardholders get an invitation to upgrade to the unsecured OpenSky Visa Gold. Upgrades are by invitation rather than open application, your deposit is returned when you graduate, and it does not require a fresh hard credit pull. According to OpenSky's own data, users improve their credit scores by an average of 50 points in 6 months.

## Zolve: an unsecured card with no deposit and no SSN required

*This one comes up a lot:* can I get an unsecured card with an ITIN and no U.S. credit history? Zolve is currently the clearest answer for employed newcomers.

You can apply with a Social Security Number, an ITIN, or a passport, and approval does not require a credit check. Zolve sets credit limits based on employment and income rather than U.S. credit history, and some users report receiving limits between $1,000 and $15,000. There is no annual fee, and the card reports to all three major U.S. credit bureaus, so every on-time payment builds your file.

The honest caveat is the APR. The card carries a variable rate in the range of about 19.50% to 39.50%, depending on the issuer's assessment of your creditworthiness. That is a wide and potentially high range. It only matters if you carry a balance. If you pay the statement balance in full every month, the APR is irrelevant, and the card is genuinely one of the best no-deposit options for ITIN holders. You can also apply and receive a virtual card before you even arrive in the U.S., which makes it the best fit for applicants who are still abroad.

## Self Visa and Kikoff: credit-builder cards for ITIN holders

*A question we hear often:* what if I want to build both an installment and a revolving credit history at the same time? Self addresses that directly.

The Self Visa Credit Card has a $25 annual fee after the first year and accepts ITIN applicants who already have a Self Credit Builder Account. It reports to all three credit bureaus. As of January 2025, you can also fund a $100 minimum security deposit directly with a debit card or bank account and get the card right away without the loan. Or you can still open a Self Credit Builder Account first, make at least three on-time payments, reach $100 or more in savings progress, and use that balance to secure the Visa.

The strategic benefit: FICO scoring rewards borrowers who carry both installment and revolving accounts, and most ITIN holders start with neither. Self gives you both inside a single app.

For applicants who want the absolute lowest barrier to entry, the Kikoff Secured Credit Card has a low refundable deposit, no hard pull, and accepts an ITIN during account opening. It is best for first-time borrowers who want a small line to practice responsible use. All three of these cards (Current Build, Self Visa, and Kikoff) report to Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion.

## Chase: how ITIN holders access a cards portfolio worth building toward

Chase is not a starting point for most ITIN holders, but it is the most valuable destination after 12-18 months of credit building. Its Ultimate Rewards program is among the most flexible in U.S. credit.

Chase Freedom Unlimited applicants need to be 18 or older with a U.S. mailing address and an SSN or ITIN. The credit score bar is real: you generally need 700 or better to have a shot. Chase also applies the 5/24 rule. If you have opened five or more credit cards in the last 24 months, Chase may deny your application, and this rule applies to most of their cards.

A practical tip that helps ITIN holders specifically: applicants for the Chase Freedom Rise could raise their approval odds by first opening a Chase checking or savings account with a minimum balance of $250. The same logic applies broadly. Opening a Chase bank account first, funding it, and demonstrating responsible behavior before applying meaningfully improves your odds as an ITIN holder with a shorter U.S. credit file.

For rewards context: the Chase Freedom Unlimited earns 5% back on travel booked through Chase, 3% back at restaurants including eligible delivery, 3% back on drugstores, and 1.5% cash back on everything else. You generally need a score of 670 or higher.

## Bank of America and Citi: ITIN accepted, but often branch-required

Both Bank of America and Citi formally list an ITIN as an accepted identifier, but neither has a fully friction-free online flow for ITIN-only applicants.

Bank of America: a Social Security number or ITIN is required to be considered for Bank of America credit cards. That said, ITIN applicants typically need to visit a Bank of America financial center to finish the process, since the standard online application often does not complete successfully without an SSN. The product range is wide once you get through the door. The secured card takes a refundable deposit of $200 to $5,000, which sets your credit line, charges no annual fee, and reports monthly. A deposit return or upgrade is possible after roughly 12 months. For established ITIN holders, the Customized Cash Rewards card earns rewards across six selectable categories with no annual fee, and applicants generally need to be 18 or older with a U.S. mailing address and an SSN or ITIN.

Citi Secured Mastercard: it is a no-annual-fee secured card that reports to the major bureaus and lets you choose a deposit from $200 to $2,500 in $100 increments. Citi's own requirements list an SSN or ITIN among the identity fields. The critical limitation: Citi requires a valid SSN on file before it will graduate its Secured Mastercard to an unsecured account. An ITIN-only holder can build credit with the card but cannot graduate until they later obtain an SSN. If you anticipate getting an SSN within a few years, this is not a dealbreaker. If you want a clean upgrade path on an ITIN alone, choose Capital One or OpenSky instead.

## What happened to the Discover it Secured? Can ITIN holders still get it?

This is an important update for 2026. As of June 2, Discover is no longer accepting applications for the Discover it Secured Credit Card. Capital One, which acquired Discover in 2025, says it will relaunch the card later this year, but no firm date has been announced as of the publication of this article.

For existing cardholders, nothing changes: your account continues to work, payments still report to all three bureaus, and the rewards structure is unchanged. For new applicants who were counting on Discover, the best current alternatives are Capital One Platinum Secured (same issuer, similar philosophy, fully online) and OpenSky Plus (no annual fee, no credit check).

## 8 mistakes ITIN holders make when applying for credit cards

Even with the right card in mind, common errors cause unnecessary denials. Here is what to avoid:

1. Applying at the wrong tier. Applying for Chase Freedom Unlimited with no U.S. credit history produces a denial and a hard inquiry. Start with a secured card and work up.
2. Using a VoIP phone number. Issuers text a one-time verification code during the application, and a VoIP number will fail this check. A real U.S. SIM is required.
3. Skipping the soft-check pre-approval. Use soft-check tools like Capital One's to gauge approval odds without triggering a hard inquiry.
4. Applying without an existing bank relationship. Having an account at the same institution where you are applying dramatically improves your chances, and many banks that accept ITINs for bank accounts also accept them for credit cards.
5. Carrying a balance. APRs on secured cards average 26%+. Paying in full every month eliminates interest entirely.
6. Closing the secured card too soon. Closing an account shortens your average credit age. Aim to upgrade rather than close wherever the issuer offers it.
7. Missing the Citi graduation limitation. If you open the Citi Secured Mastercard expecting a straightforward upgrade path on an ITIN alone, you will be disappointed. Know this before you apply.
8. Letting your ITIN expire. If your ITIN is not used on a federal tax return for three consecutive years, it expires, which can affect your ability to maintain or open credit accounts. See [what happens to my credit card if my ITIN expires](/articles/expired-itin-credit-card-what-happens).

What credit score do I need for a Chase or Amex card with an ITIN?

With a caveat. Citi requires a valid SSN on file before it will graduate its Secured Mastercard to an unsecured account. An ITIN-only holder can open the card and build credit, but the upgrade path is blocked until they obtain an SSN.

Collect your ITIN letter (CP565/CP567), a passport or government ID, proof of U.S. address such as a utility bill or lease, and your initial deposit amount if one is required.

How long does it take to build enough credit to upgrade from a secured to an unsecured card with an ITIN?
Capital One reviews for an upgrade in as little as six months of on-time payments. Full graduation to an unsecured card at Capital One or OpenSky typically follows at the 6-12 month mark with consistent on-time payments.

Does Bank of America require a branch visit for ITIN applicants?
Generally yes. ITIN-only applicants typically cannot finish the standard Bank of America online application without visiting a branch to verify identity and complete the process.
