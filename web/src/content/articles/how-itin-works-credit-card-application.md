Your ITIN is a nine-digit IRS tax ID in the format 9XX-XX-XXXX, and on a credit card application it works as a direct substitute for a Social Security Number. The application process is essentially the same: you put the nine-digit ITIN where the form asks for a nine-digit SSN. What most guides skip is everything that happens after you type that number in: how the issuer verifies it, what the bureaus do with it, and where things can go sideways. This page covers all of that, step by step.

---

## What exactly is an ITIN, and why does a credit card issuer care about it?

An ITIN is a tax processing number issued by the IRS. Always nine digits, always beginning with 9, always formatted 9XX-XX-XXXX. An SSN follows the same nine-digit pattern but typically starts with 1 through 8, so a trained underwriter can tell the two apart immediately.

Issuers care about the number for two reasons. First, federal Know Your Customer rules require them to confirm your identity before opening a credit account. Banks and card issuers can legally accept an ITIN under the Patriot Act's KYC rules, though not every issuer chooses to. Second, they need a taxpayer ID to report your account to the credit bureaus. Without one, they have nowhere to send the data that eventually becomes your U.S. credit history.

As of October 2025, the IRS has issued approximately 31 million ITINs since the program started in 1996, with about 5 million active and nearly 470,000 new ones issued in 2025 alone. That is a large pool of potential cardholders, which is part of why more issuers have quietly expanded their ITIN acceptance over the past few years.

## Where exactly does my ITIN go on the credit card application form?

*A question we hear often:* applicants are not sure whether to leave the SSN field blank, invent something, or type the ITIN directly.

For issuers that accept an ITIN, type it directly into the SSN or "Taxpayer ID" field. Some issuers do not accept ITINs at all. For those that do, the mechanics differ slightly by platform:

- Online forms at ITIN-friendly issuers: type your ITIN directly into the SSN or "Taxpayer ID" field. Some forms display an ITIN option only after you select an alternate ID type, so look for a dropdown or checkbox before assuming the form rejects ITINs.
- Online forms at other issuers: if the form will not accept an ITIN, call the issuer's customer line and apply by phone. Branch visits work too, and agents are usually better equipped to handle non-standard applications.
- Capital One specifically: Capital One allows you to apply with an ITIN; enter it in the SSN field.

Never leave the field blank unless the form explicitly offers a "no SSN" checkbox. A blank field typically triggers an automatic rejection before a human ever reviews your file.

## What does the issuer actually do with my ITIN after I submit?

*This one comes up a lot:* people wonder whether their ITIN gets checked against an IRS database or whether issuers just take whatever number you type.

The issuer runs your ITIN through its standard identity-verification system, the same one it uses for SSN applicants. It checks your name, address, and date of birth against its data sources and, if you have any U.S. credit history, pulls your credit report. Non-residents may face additional verification requirements even when the process is otherwise similar to a standard application.

If you have no U.S. credit file yet, many issuers will ask for supplemental documents. Having these ready before you start speeds things up considerably:

- Your IRS ITIN assignment letter (CP-565)
- A government-issued photo ID (a passport is most universally accepted)
- Proof of a U.S. address (a lease, utility bill, or recent bank statement)
- Proof of income (recent pay stubs or a prior-year tax return)

Confirm that a specific card accepts an ITIN before you apply. A product advertised as "ITIN-friendly" by the parent bank may still require an SSN on a particular co-branded card.

## How does an ITIN application affect my credit file at the three bureaus?

*Readers frequently ask:* whether an ITIN creates a "lesser" credit file or one that scores differently.

It does not. When you open a credit account with your ITIN, the lender reports your payment activity to the credit bureaus under that number. The bureaus create a credit file tied to your ITIN, and your credit score is calculated from that file using the same FICO model applied to SSN-based accounts. There is no separate scoring system for ITIN holders.

The matching process is also more robust than most people assume. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau explains that bureaus match each reported account to the right person's file using details like name, address, and date of birth, not an SSN alone. That multi-factor matching is good news: a minor address change will not orphan your account history.

What this means practically: every on-time payment you make with an ITIN credit card is building a real, scoreable U.S. credit history. With consistent responsible use, most people reach a 650+ score within 12-18 months, according to data compiled by immigrant financial resources. Once you have a file, upgrading from a [secured card to an unsecured card](/articles/upgrade-secured-to-unsecured-credit-card-itin) becomes a realistic next step.

## What if the online form rejects my ITIN or says the number is invalid?

*A question we hear often:* the error usually has nothing to do with your ITIN being wrong.

Online application forms sometimes validate the SSN field against a format that excludes numbers starting with 9. That is a form-design issue, not an issuer policy issue. Three ways around it:

1. Call the issuer's application line. Agents can enter ITIN-specific notes and push the application through a non-standard workflow. Most major issuers have this capability.
2. Apply in a branch. Branch staff handle non-standard IDs routinely and can flag the application for manual review.
3. Try a different card from the same issuer. Some issuers have updated certain card applications to accept ITINs while older product pages still carry the legacy SSN-only field.

If you have already applied and been denied for a reason unrelated to your ITIN, our guide on [credit card reconsideration for ITIN holders](/articles/credit-card-reconsideration-line-itin) explains how to call for a review.

## What happens to my ITIN credit card if I later get an SSN?

Your credit card account stays open and in good standing. What changes is the identifier linked to your credit file. Once you have a Social Security number, you must rescind your ITIN, and the credit bureaus need to be notified to transfer your credit history from the ITIN to the SSN.

The date your credit history began does not change. Every month of on-time payments you made under your ITIN transfers with you. No reboot, no lost history. ## Frequently asked questions

**Do I type my ITIN in the SSN field on a credit card application?**
Yes. Most issuers that accept an ITIN ask you to enter it directly in the SSN or taxpayer ID field. Some online forms reveal an ITIN option only after you select an alternate ID type, and a few require a phone or branch application instead.

**Will a credit card application with an ITIN trigger a hard inquiry?**
Yes, if the issuer does a full credit check. The inquiry appears on your credit file just as it would for an SSN applicant. A handful of secured cards, like OpenSky, skip the credit pull entirely, which is helpful if you have no U.S. credit file yet.

**Does submitting a credit card application with an ITIN affect my immigration status?**
No. The IRS has stated repeatedly that an ITIN does not create an inference about an individual's immigration status. The card issuer uses it only for identity and credit-reporting purposes.

**How does my credit card activity get linked to my ITIN at the bureaus?**
The card issuer reports your account details, including your ITIN, name, address, and payment history, to Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion. According to the CFPB, the bureaus match data to your file using multiple identifiers, not your ITIN alone.

**What happens to my credit card account if my ITIN expires?**
An expired ITIN does not automatically close your account or stop bureau reporting. Renewing it promptly keeps your tax record clean and avoids complications if the issuer re-verifies your identity. See our guide on [expired ITINs and credit cards](/articles/expired-itin-credit-card-what-happens) for a full breakdown.
