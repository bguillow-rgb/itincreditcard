## Why can't I just apply for any credit card with my ITIN?

*A question we hear often:* An ITIN proves your U.S. tax identity, but it does not create a U.S. credit file. When a lender pulls your application, they see an empty report or no file at all. That is the core problem. Most rewards cards and unsecured cards require at least six to twelve months of U.S. credit history, and a thin or missing file triggers an automatic denial regardless of how strong your income is.

The workaround is a secured card. You post a refundable cash deposit, the issuer uses that as collateral instead of relying on credit history, then reports your payment behavior to the bureaus every month. After six to twelve months of on-time payments you typically have a scoreable file, and consistent on-time payments with low utilization can get you to 650-700 within 12-18 months.

One thing worth knowing: your ITIN does not slow the process. Once an account is opened using your ITIN, it reports to the bureaus the same way as any other account. Payment history, utilization, and account age all factor into your score identically, whether you used an ITIN or an SSN.

## How long will it realistically take to build credit from zero with my ITIN card?

*Readers frequently ask:* Building credit is a timeline question, and the honest answer has two distinct phases.

**Phase 1: Becoming visible (months 1-6)**

According to Experian, most scoring models require at least one account that has been open for six months and at least one account reported to the bureau within the past six months before a score can be generated. That is why the 3-6 month window is standard: it's the minimum activity threshold to produce a calculable score.

Your first credit score typically appears in the 600-650 range after 3-6 months, then reaches 650-700 with consistent good habits between months 6-12. VantageScore may generate a number sooner, sometimes within a month or two, but most lenders use FICO scores for approval decisions.

**Phase 2: Reaching a usable score (months 6-24)**

Starting with zero credit history, you can establish credit in as little as six months. A good credit score of 700 or better usually requires timely payments for at least 18 months to two years.

With clean execution, good credit (670+) commonly arrives within about 12 months and 700+ within 12-24 months. Authorized-user history on an old, clean account compresses the timeline. Getting to 800+ takes years because account age can only be earned by waiting.

The practical implication: open your starter ITIN card now, use it for small recurring purchases, and pay the full statement balance every month. Don't carry a balance. Don't miss a payment. After 6-12 months you will have a score, and after 12-18 months you will likely qualify for unsecured cards with real rewards. ## What documents do I need to apply for a secured ITIN credit card?

*This one comes up a lot:* The document list is shorter than most people expect.

You apply online with your ITIN, a foreign passport, a U.S. mailing address, and a U.S. bank account to fund any deposit. For OpenSky specifically, no bank account is required for the application itself, though you will need one to fund your deposit after approval.

Here is the standard document checklist across major ITIN-accepting issuers:

1. ITIN number (IRS Form W-7 letter or card)
2. Foreign passport (most widely accepted photo ID)
3. U.S. mailing address (P.O. boxes are generally not accepted)
4. U.S. bank or fintech account (to fund deposit, if secured)
5. Proof of income (pay stubs, offer letter, bank statements; not always required but helps)

One application tip: most major issuers let you enter your ITIN in the SSN field on their online form, or provide an option to indicate you have an ITIN rather than an SSN. Most Chase, Amex, and Capital One applications include an option to enter an ITIN or select "I don't have an SSN." You can also apply in person at a branch.

## What are the biggest mistakes ITIN holders make with their first credit card?

Understanding what breaks the credit-building process is as useful as knowing what speeds it up.

**Carrying a balance month to month.** Every card on this list has an APR above 20%. At 23-30% variable, a $200 balance that takes three months to pay off costs $15-20 in interest and does nothing positive for your credit history. Pay the full statement balance every cycle, not just the minimum.

**Applying for multiple cards at once.** Each application usually triggers a hard inquiry, which temporarily dents the thin credit file you are trying to build. Start with one card, let it season for 6-12 months, then consider adding a second. Our guide on [having multiple credit cards with an ITIN](/articles/multiple-credit-cards-itin-holders) covers the right timing.

**Maxing out the credit limit.** Your [credit utilization ratio](/articles/credit-utilization-itin-credit-card) is how much of your available limit you are using. Most credit experts recommend staying under 30%, and under 10% for the fastest score gains. On a $200 limit, that means keeping your balance below $60 at the time the statement closes.

**Closing the card too soon.** The length of your credit history matters. Keep your first card open even after you graduate to a better one, as long as there is no annual fee eating into your budget.
