If you landed on a credit card application that asks for a Social Security Number but you only have an ITIN, you are not alone. The term shows up constantly on application forms, yet most guides skip straight to card recommendations without explaining the number itself. This article closes that gap.

## "So what exactly is an ITIN, and why does a credit card ask for it?"

According to the IRS, "an Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN) is a tax processing number issued by the Internal Revenue Service." The IRS issues ITINs to individuals who are required to have a U.S. taxpayer identification number but who do not have, and are not eligible to obtain, a Social Security number from the Social Security Administration.

In plain terms: if you owe U.S. taxes but cannot get an SSN, the IRS gives you an ITIN so you can file a return. It is issued purely for tax administration, to people who have a U.S. tax obligation but are not eligible for an SSN. Both are nine digits, but an ITIN always begins with 9, and one person never validly holds both numbers at the same time.

Credit card applications ask for a taxpayer identification number because federal law requires lenders to verify your identity. Banks and credit card issuers can legally accept an ITIN under the Patriot Act's Know Your Customer rules, though not every issuer chooses to do so, and a growing number do.

## "What does ITIN stand for, and how is it different from an SSN?"

*A question we hear often:* people assume the two numbers are interchangeable. They are not, but for credit card purposes the distinction matters less than you might think.

| Feature | SSN | ITIN |
|---|---|---|
| Issued by | Social Security Administration | IRS |
| Who gets it | U.S. citizens, permanent residents, authorized noncitizens | People with U.S. tax obligations who cannot get an SSN |
| Number format | XXX-XX-XXXX (first digit 0-8) | 9XX-XX-XXXX (always starts with 9) |
| Application form | Form SS-5 | Form W-7 |
| Authorizes work | Yes | No |
| Expires | Never | After 3 consecutive years of non-use on a federal return |
| Accepted on credit card applications | Yes, universally | Yes, at many but not all issuers |

SSNs come from the SSA and authorize work; ITINs come from the IRS and exist only for tax filing. For credit purposes, though, once a credit account is opened using your ITIN, it reports to the credit bureaus the same way as any other account. Payment history, utilization, and account age all factor into your credit score identically whether you used an ITIN or an SSN to open the account.

## "Who actually has an ITIN in 2026?"

*This one comes up a lot:* readers want to know whether they qualify before they apply for anything.

As of October 2025, the IRS has issued approximately 31 million ITINs since the program's inception. Approximately 5 million ITINs are active, and 469,888 were issued in calendar year 2025.

ITIN holders include certain visa holders, spouses, dependents, and others who are lawfully present in the U.S. but not eligible for SSNs. Holding an ITIN, on its own, should not be used to infer a person's immigration status.

Common ITIN holders include F-1 students without work authorization, H-4 visa spouses, TN visa professionals whose employers have not yet sponsored them, nonresident investors earning U.S. income, and undocumented individuals who voluntarily file taxes. ITINs are issued regardless of immigration status because these individuals may have federal tax filing and payment requirements under the Internal Revenue Code.

If you are eligible for an SSN, the IRS will not issue you an ITIN. Do not complete Form W-7 if you have an SSN or if you are eligible to get one. You are eligible for an SSN if you are a U.S. citizen or if you have been admitted by the United States for permanent residence or U.S. employment.

## "Where do I actually put my ITIN on a credit card application? The form only says 'SSN.'"

*Readers frequently ask:* this is the single most practical question, and the answer depends on the issuer.

Many issuers and most credit unions accept an ITIN, and the application often has a field where you can enter it in place of an SSN. The experience varies by issuer:

- **Capital One:** Capital One will allow you to apply with an ITIN. Enter your ITIN number in the SSN field.
- **American Express:** The application's SSN field includes help text that explicitly says to enter your ITIN there. American Express identifies an ITIN as an accepted U.S. card-applicant tax identifier and states applicants should "have a Social Security number or Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN)."
- **Citi:** The online application has one identity field labeled "Social Security Number or ITIN."
- **Wells Fargo:** Also uses a combined "SSN/ITIN" field on its online application.
- **Chase:** The application checklist accepts both a Social Security card and an SSA Verification Letter or Department of Treasury letter verifying a TIN.

If the application asks for an SSN, look for an option to enter an ITIN. Some online forms display this option only after you select an alternate ID type. If the form will not accept an ITIN, call the issuer's customer line and apply by phone.

At least seven of the top ten U.S. credit card issuers accept an ITIN in place of an SSN, including Capital One, Chase, American Express, Bank of America, Wells Fargo, and Citi. ## "Does my ITIN number appear printed on my credit card?"

No, and this is a common misconception worth clearing up. Your ITIN is a tax identifier stored in the issuer's system. The physical card you receive shows only your name, the 15 or 16 digit card number, the expiration date, and the security code. Your ITIN is never printed on the card face, nor is it embedded in the card number.

Your ITIN connects to your account in the issuer's database, the same way an SSN connects to a citizen's account. That behind-the-scenes link is what lets the issuer report your payment history to Experian, Equifax, and TransUnion under your ITIN. If you are using an ITIN instead of an SSN, you can still access your credit information for free. Major credit bureaus Experian, Equifax, and TransUnion allow individuals with an ITIN to request their credit report.

## "Is it safe to give my ITIN to a credit card company? Will it be shared with immigration authorities?"

*A question we hear often:* especially from readers who are uncertain about privacy in the current regulatory environment.

The government does not use the ITIN to track undocumented individuals. The IRS does not share this information with immigration enforcement because of Section 6103 of the Internal Revenue Code, which prohibits the release of tax information. When you give your ITIN to a credit card issuer, the issuer uses it for identity verification and credit bureau reporting, not to check or report your immigration status.

The issuer itself is subject to federal privacy laws that govern how financial institutions handle customer data. What they cannot do is use your ITIN to make decisions based on national origin alone, which would violate the Equal Credit Opportunity Act.

That said, the political environment around ITIN data has shifted. In February 2025, DHS formally requested that the IRS provide the names, home addresses, phone numbers, and email addresses of 700,000 ITIN holders. The IRS initially denied the request, citing long-standing taxpayer privacy protections. ## "What documents do I need when I apply for a credit card using my ITIN?"

Beyond the ITIN itself, issuers typically require supporting documents because they cannot verify your identity through an SSN lookup the way they do for U.S. citizens. Plan to have:

1. Your IRS ITIN assignment letter (CP-565 or CP-566) or your most recent tax transcript showing the number.
2. A valid government-issued photo ID, typically a foreign passport or consular ID (matrícula consular).
3. Proof of a U.S. address such as a lease agreement, utility bill, or bank statement dated within 60-90 days.
4. Proof of income: recent pay stubs, a tax return, or bank statements showing regular deposits.

Once you have the number, confirm that a card's application takes an ITIN before you apply, since some issuers accept one only on certain cards. Applying without checking first wastes a hard inquiry that will temporarily affect your credit profile.

## "Will my ITIN expire and cause problems with my cards?"

SSNs do not expire or need to be renewed, but ITINs can expire if they are not used on a federal tax return at least once every three years. Just like the SSN, the number will usually stay the same even if it expires and is later renewed.

An expired ITIN does not automatically close existing credit card accounts you already hold. The issuer already has your number on file. Where expiration causes real friction is on new applications: an issuer running a verification check may flag the number as inactive and decline the application. Renewing your ITIN before it lapses keeps your financial life uninterrupted. ## Frequently asked questions

**What does ITIN stand for on a credit card application?**
ITIN stands for Individual Taxpayer Identification Number. On a credit card application it is your taxpayer identifier in place of a Social Security Number, letting the issuer verify your identity and report your account activity to the credit bureaus.

**Where do I enter my ITIN on a credit card application?**
Most issuers that accept an ITIN let you type it directly into the SSN field. Some applications show help text that reads "Social Security Number or ITIN." If the online form rejects it, call the issuer or apply at a branch in person.

**Does using an ITIN instead of an SSN affect my credit score?**
No. Once an account is opened with your ITIN, it reports to the credit bureaus the same way as any other account. Payment history, utilization, and account age all factor into your score identically whether you used an ITIN or an SSN.

**Will my ITIN expire and cause problems with my credit card?**
An ITIN expires if it is not used on a federal tax return at least once every three years. An expired ITIN can complicate new card applications, but cards you already hold are generally not closed immediately. Renewing your ITIN early avoids any disruption.

**Can any credit card issuer legally refuse to accept an ITIN?**
Yes. While issuers cannot require an SSN if you do not have one, they can decline to accept an ITIN as a matter of internal policy. That is why you should confirm a specific issuer accepts ITINs before submitting a hard-inquiry application.
