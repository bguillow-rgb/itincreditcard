If your green card application is pending, the wait can feel like everything is on hold. Your credit life does not have to be. An ITIN lets you apply for a credit card right now, before USCIS approves your Form I-485, before you receive a Social Security number, before any of that uncertainty resolves. The cards that accept ITINs report to the same three bureaus that every other card does, so every on-time payment you make today is building a real U.S. credit file for the future you are working toward.

## "Can I really get a credit card while my green card is still pending?"

*A question we hear often:* yes, and the timing actually works in your favor.

The IRS issues ITINs to foreign nationals who need a taxpayer identification number but are not eligible for a Social Security number. That nine-digit number beginning with 9 is the only tax ID you need to open a credit card account at multiple major issuers. A growing number of lenders accept ITINs on applications, which opens up regular cards for non-citizen residents.

The practical implication for adjustment-of-status applicants is real. Someone who files an I-485 today and waits 18-24 months for approval can walk out of that process with nearly two years of credit history already established, rather than starting from zero the day the green card arrives.

## "Does having a pending I-485 hurt my chances of approval?"

No issuer advertises a policy that penalizes pending adjustment of status. What changed in 2026 is different and narrower. On June 8, 2026, the CFPB's Statement on Ability to Repay and Immigration Status took effect. Under the Truth in Lending Act, it says lenders may be obligated to consider immigration status when deciding whether you can repay a credit card. In practice, some issuers may ask for additional documentation about your status, but a pending I-485 demonstrates you are actively pursuing lawful permanent residence, not that you are a flight risk.

An ITIN remains a valid identifying number under Customer Identification Program rules. The June 2026 advisory did not prohibit ITIN accounts. It encourages banks to treat an ITIN used instead of a Social Security number as one risk factor among many.

## "What documents do I need to bring to the application?"

*Readers frequently ask:* what exactly goes into the folder. Most of what you already have for your I-485 package overlaps with what card issuers want.

You will need a valid ITIN (not expired from three years of non-use on a tax return), your IRS CP565 ITIN assignment notice or a prior-year tax return showing your ITIN, a valid passport or government-issued photo ID, a U.S. or international mailing address, and proof of income such as pay stubs, bank statements, or a tax return.

One detail that trips people up: even small differences like a missing middle name or accent mark can cause an application to be rejected. Check your CP565 notice for the exact name format the IRS has on file and enter it identically on the credit card application. Your Employment Authorization Document (EAD), which many I-485 applicants receive while waiting, is also worth bringing as a secondary ID. Issuers who want to see immigration documentation will find it reassuring.

## "Should I apply online or visit a branch?"

It depends on the issuer. Capital One's online application accepts ITINs and processes approvals in minutes. For Bank of America and Citi, the in-branch route tends to be more reliable, as noted in our guide on [applying for a credit card online vs. in branch with an ITIN](/articles/credit-card-itin-apply-online-vs-in-branch).

If you go in-branch, bring originals of everything listed above plus a second form of ID. A branch manager who can see your full document package is more likely to push an ITIN application through than an automated online system that flags unfamiliar fields.

## "How much credit history can I realistically build before my green card is approved?"

*A question we hear often:* quite a bit, and the math is encouraging. USCIS processing times for adjustment of status (Form I-485) have ranged from 18 to 36 months for many employment-based categories in 2026. If you open a secured card the month you file, you can have 18-36 months of payment history established by the time your green card arrives.

Three numbers shape almost every approval decision: payment history, credit utilization, and length of credit history. Payment history is roughly 35% of a FICO score, and a single missed payment can drop a young file by 50 points or more. This is why opening the card and paying it on time, every month, matters far more than which specific card you chose. By the time your SSN is active, you can be in a position to qualify for premium travel or cash-back cards rather than starting from scratch. See our full guide on [building credit with an ITIN credit card](/articles/build-credit-with-itin-credit-card) for a month-by-month breakdown.

According to the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, most lenders use all three major bureaus when evaluating new credit applications. Before applying, confirm that the card reports to Experian, Equifax, and TransUnion. Cards like the OpenSky Secured Visa and the Discover it Secured Card report to all three, which matters because different lenders pull from different bureaus when evaluating your creditworthiness for future products.

## "What happens to my credit card and credit history when my SSN arrives?"

*Readers frequently ask:* nothing negative, and potentially something very positive. When USCIS approves your green card and you receive your Social Security number, your existing card accounts remain open and in good standing. Contact each issuer and ask them to add the SSN to your account on file. The credit bureaus then link both your ITIN and your new SSN to a single credit profile, so your full history is preserved.

For a detailed look at this transition, see our article on [what happens to your credit card when you get an SSN after using an ITIN](/articles/itin-to-ssn-credit-card-history-transfer). Once that history is attached to your SSN, the full range of issuers that previously required an SSN becomes available to you, and you are applying with an established credit file rather than starting at zero.

## "Can I apply for more than one card while my green card is pending?"

Yes, and it can be a smart move if done carefully. Two cards with low balances and consistent on-time payments builds your credit profile faster than one card alone. It increases your total available credit and shows you can manage multiple accounts. For more on this, our guide on [having multiple credit cards with an ITIN](/articles/multiple-credit-cards-itin-holders) covers timing and spacing of applications to minimize hard-inquiry impact.

The practical rule of thumb: wait at least six months between applications so each account has time to age before you add another inquiry to your file.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I get a credit card with an ITIN while my green card application is pending?**
Yes. Your ITIN is accepted by multiple major issuers including Capital One and OpenSky. You do not need to wait for your green card or SSN to apply.

**Will a pending I-485 or adjustment of status hurt my credit card application?**
No issuer has a policy that penalizes applicants for having a pending I-485. Under a June 2026 CFPB advisory, lenders may consider immigration status as a repayment factor, but pending adjustment of status alone is not an automatic denial reason.

**What documents do I need to apply for a credit card as a green card applicant?**
Bring your ITIN letter (IRS CP565 notice), an unexpired foreign passport or consular ID, proof of a U.S. address such as a lease or utility bill, and proof of income. Some issuers also welcome your EAD card as a secondary ID.

**What happens to my ITIN credit card when my green card and SSN are approved?**
Your existing credit card account stays open. Contact the issuer to add your new SSN to the account. Your full credit history built under the ITIN transfers automatically because bureaus link both numbers to the same profile.

**Should I get a secured or unsecured card while waiting for my green card?**
If you have little or no U.S. credit history, start with a secured card. It requires a refundable deposit and has much higher approval odds. Once you have 12-18 months of on-time payments, you can apply for an unsecured card with better rewards. See our [secured vs. unsecured ITIN credit card comparison](/articles/secured-vs-unsecured-credit-card-itin-comparison) for a full breakdown.
