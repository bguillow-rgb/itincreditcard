American Express accepts ITINs on every U.S. consumer credit card application, which makes it one of the more accessible major issuers for immigrants and non-citizens. That said, acceptance is structural, not automatic. The application lets you submit your ITIN, but approval still depends on your U.S. credit file and income. This guide covers what to expect, which cards are realistic, and where the Amex Global Card Relationship program fits in.

## Does American Express actually accept an ITIN on its applications?

*A question we hear often:* whether the Amex ITIN option is real or just marketing language for immigrants who end up rejected anyway.

It's real. The SSN field in Amex's consumer card application explicitly states that you may enter your ITIN if you don't have an SSN. The system is built to handle it.

You can get an American Express credit card without an SSN, because Amex will accept an ITIN in place of a Social Security number.

The nuance worth knowing: acceptance and approval are two different things. Amex accepts an ITIN as a tax ID alternative to an SSN, but approval rates for direct ITIN applications run lower than at Discover or Capital One, because Amex underwriting leans harder on your U.S. credit file. If you have little or no U.S. credit history, approval is harder, but the path is open. For context on building that credit file first, see our guide on [how to build credit fast with an ITIN credit card](/articles/build-credit-with-itin-credit-card).

## Which Amex cards can I realistically get with an ITIN?

*This one comes up a lot.* Not every Amex card is equally accessible to ITIN holders, and the gap between starter cards and premium products is wide.

The Blue Cash Everyday Card from American Express is the most realistic direct target for ITIN holders. No annual fee, more modest credit requirements than Amex's premium lineup, and after 12 or more months of established U.S. credit, it becomes genuinely attainable.

Here is how the main Amex card tiers stack up for ITIN applicants:

| Amex Card | Annual Fee | Realistic ITIN Path | Approx. U.S. Credit History Needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blue Cash Everyday | $0 | Direct ITIN application | 12+ months |
| Blue Cash Preferred | $95 | Direct ITIN application | 12-18 months |
| Gold Card | $325 | Direct ITIN or Global Transfer | 18-24 months |
| Platinum Card | $695 | Global Transfer preferred | 24+ months, high income |
| Green Card | $150 | Direct ITIN application | 12-18 months |

Direct ITIN approval for the Amex Platinum is rare, under 10%, without at least 24 months of established U.S. credit history and high income. Through Global Transfer, if your non-U.S. card is a comparable Platinum-tier product, the transfer can go to the U.S. Platinum.

## What is the Amex Global Card Relationship and do I need an ITIN for it?

*Readers frequently ask* about this program because it sounds like a shortcut around the U.S. credit file requirement entirely.

The Amex Global Card Relationship program (formerly called Global Transfer) lets an existing Amex cardholder in an eligible country get a U.S. Amex card with no Social Security Number, no ITIN, and no U.S. credit file. The card must have been active for at least six months and in good standing.

The catch matters for credit building. Global Transfer uses your internal Amex history and needs no tax ID, but the card it produces may not report to U.S. credit bureaus. You gain a U.S. Amex card but don't automatically start accumulating a U.S. credit score. A secured or starter card opened with an ITIN, by contrast, reports to all three bureaus from day one.

The practical move: treat the Global Transfer card as your foot in the door, then get an ITIN and apply for a second Amex card with it so your payments start landing on an actual U.S. credit report.

## What documents do I need when I apply for an Amex card with an ITIN?

The most common reasons American Express rejects ITIN applications are a thin U.S. credit history, an ITIN that can't be verified (often because it was recently issued), a U.S. address that triggers a commercial mail-receiving agent or registered-agent flag, and insufficient income documentation.

To give yourself the best shot, gather these before you apply:

- **ITIN assignment letter (IRS CP-565):** Proof that your number is valid and not expired. If your ITIN was issued in 2021 or earlier and you haven't used it recently on a tax return, confirm with the IRS that it hasn't lapsed.
- **Government-issued photo ID:** A passport is the most universally accepted document. Some applicants are also asked to provide it for identity verification after submission.
- **Verifiable U.S. address:** A real residential address, not a P.O. box or mail-forwarding service. Amex's fraud filters flag CMRA addresses.
- **Income documentation:** Pay stubs, bank statements, or tax returns. Amex is more income-sensitive than some other issuers.

According to data compiled by the CFPB, income verification is cited in a significant share of credit card denials for applicants without established U.S. files, so documenting your income clearly matters more here than it would for a long-tenured U.S. cardholder. If you want to understand the income side in more detail, see [what income do you need to get a credit card with an ITIN](/articles/income-requirements-credit-card-itin).

## How does applying for an Amex card with an ITIN compare to other major issuers?

*A question we hear often:* whether Amex is actually a better or worse choice than Capital One, Citi, or Bank of America for an ITIN holder.

Some major credit card issuers, including American Express and Capital One, will accept an ITIN in place of an SSN to help satisfy federal identity verification requirements. The difference is in how they weight your application after they accept it.

| Issuer | Accepts ITIN | No U.S. History Option | Approval Difficulty (ITIN, thin file) |
|---|---|---|---|
| American Express | Yes (all consumer cards) | Via Global Transfer only | Medium-High |
| Capital One | Yes | Secured card available | Medium |
| Bank of America | Yes | In-branch path exists | Medium |
| Citi | Yes | In-branch path exists | Medium |
| Chase | Yes (select cards) | No | High |
| OpenSky | Yes | No credit check | Low |

Some U.S. card issuers accept an ITIN in place of an SSN, and a smaller subset accept a passport plus other identity documents. Acceptance is issuer-by-issuer and not guaranteed by law. It's worth calling the issuer's customer service line directly to confirm their current ITIN policy before applying, because published policies and branch-level execution aren't always the same thing.

## What happens after I get approved for an Amex card with an ITIN?

Approval is the beginning, not the end. Amex is known for its internal relationship model, meaning responsible use of one Amex card can open doors to other Amex products through what cardholders call the "backdoor" or "pop-up" process. A few things worth keeping in mind:

Your ITIN is tied to your Amex profile. After getting your ITIN, you can apply for a second U.S. Amex card and simply enter your ITIN in the SSN box on the new application. Each subsequent card then reports to U.S. bureaus and compounds your credit-building progress.

Keep your utilization low. Amex reports your statement balance to the bureaus each month, so paying your balance in full and keeping spending below 30% of your limit protects the score you're working to build. Our guide on [credit utilization with an ITIN card](/articles/credit-utilization-itin-credit-card) covers the mechanics in detail.

Think about graduation over time. Unlike some secured cards, Amex unsecured cards don't have a formal graduation path from secured to unsecured. You start unsecured and work toward better products within the Amex lineup. For the secured-to-unsecured journey with other issuers, see [how to upgrade from secured to unsecured credit card with an ITIN](/articles/upgrade-secured-to-unsecured-credit-card-itin).
