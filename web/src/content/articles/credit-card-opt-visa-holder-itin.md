Optional Practical Training sits in a financial gap that trips up a lot of graduates: you have real U.S. income, you have (or can get) an ITIN, but your SSN may still be weeks away and your U.S. credit history is essentially blank. The good news is that a growing list of card issuers specifically accommodate this situation, and the right card opened now can give you 6-12 months of on-time payment history before you ever set foot in a new job's HR office.

## Can I actually get a credit card on OPT if I have an ITIN but no SSN?

*A question we hear often:*

Yes, and the path is clearer than most OPT holders realize.

OPT holders authorized to work in the U.S. can obtain an SSN, but that process requires visiting the Social Security Administration with your passport, visa, I-20, and EAD card, which takes additional weeks you may not want to wait. In the meantime, you might be able to use an ITIN in place of an SSN for some banking and credit applications.

Banks and credit card issuers can legally accept an ITIN under the Patriot Act's Know Your Customer rules. Not every issuer chooses to do so, but a growing number do. The practical result: you do not have to pause your credit-building strategy while waiting on the Social Security Administration. Apply with an ITIN now and you could have 6 months of positive payment history already on your credit file by the time your SSN arrives.

## What documents do I need to apply as an OPT holder?

Gather these before you start any application to avoid mid-process delays:

- ITIN letter from the IRS (your official assignment notice, not just the number written down)
- Valid foreign passport as primary photo ID
- Employment Authorization Document (EAD) showing active OPT status
- U.S. mailing address (your campus address or apartment works)
- Proof of income such as an offer letter, pay stubs, or a stipend letter from your employer
- U.S. bank account to fund a security deposit if you apply for a secured card

If you are under 21, the Credit CARD Act of 2009 requires you to show independent income or have a co-signer. Part-time jobs, stipends, and work-study all count toward that income requirement, according to the CFPB. For most OPT holders who are 22 or older and actively employed, demonstrating income is straightforward.

One practical note: use a real U.S. mobile number during the application. Issuers send a one-time verification code, and a VoIP number like Google Voice will fail this check.

## Does my OPT expiration date scare off lenders?

*Readers frequently ask:*

It can be a factor, but it is not the dealbreaker many OPT holders fear. What matters more to most issuers is your income and existing U.S. credit history. Some lenders are cautious about near-term visa expirations, but immigrant-friendly fintechs tend to focus on income potential rather than visa type.

Secured cards sidestep this concern almost entirely. Because your deposit fully backs the credit line, the issuer carries very little risk regardless of when your EAD expires. If your OPT period is ending and you are transitioning to an H-1B, your credit file carries over without any disruption. Your U.S. credit history stays on file with the bureaus even if you leave the country. Return on an H-1B or another visa and that history is still there, giving you a head start. If you do not return, the history eventually becomes inactive but does not hurt you.

## How do I actually apply? Step-by-step for OPT holders

1. Get your ITIN first if you do not already have one. The IRS processes Form W-7 in roughly 7-11 weeks. Visit our [how to get an ITIN guide](/how-to-get-an-itin) for the full W-7 walkthrough.
2. Open a U.S. bank account using your ITIN and passport. National banks like Chase, Bank of America, and Wells Fargo accept ITIN applications for checking accounts, though policies vary by branch.
3. Pre-qualify before applying. Capital One's soft-check pre-qualification tool lets you gauge approval odds without a hard inquiry on your credit report.
4. Choose your card from the table above. Start with OpenSky if you want zero credit-check risk, or Capital One Platinum Secured if you want the strongest upgrade path later.
5. Submit your application with your ITIN, EAD, passport, U.S. address, and income information. If applying online hits a wall, many issuers complete ITIN applications by phone or in-branch.
6. Activate and use the card for small recurring purchases, then pay the full balance monthly.

For a deeper look at in-person versus online applications, see our guide on [applying online vs. in-branch with an ITIN](/articles/credit-card-itin-apply-online-vs-in-branch).

## How fast can I build a real credit score on OPT?

Starting from zero, the right first card can put you on a twelve-month path to a score in the mid-600s. The keys are straightforward: pay on time every month, keep your balance below 30% of your limit, and avoid applying for multiple cards at once.

Credit bureaus track activity by ITIN, so your file builds the same way it would with an SSN. Every on-time payment, every month of low utilization, every additional month of account age works in your favor exactly as it would for a U.S. citizen.

Once you receive your SSN (whether at the end of OPT or after transitioning to an H-1B), you can add it to your existing card accounts. If you got a credit card with an ITIN and later receive a Social Security number, you will need to request a transfer of your credit history from each credit bureau so that the history you built under your ITIN follows you to your new SSN-linked profile. Our article on [what happens to your credit cards when you get an SSN after ITIN](/articles/itin-to-ssn-credit-card-history-transfer) explains exactly how to make that request.

One more reason to pay your balance in full each month: the average APR on U.S. credit cards was 21.00% across all accounts in Q1 2026, according to the Federal Reserve G.19 report. Carrying a balance while you build your profile is an expensive way to do it.

## Should I wait for my SSN before applying, or apply with my ITIN now?

This is the central strategic question for OPT holders. The honest answer: apply now with your ITIN if you are at least 60 days away from receiving your SSN.

Every month you delay is a month of payment history you cannot get back. If you expect to receive an SSN soon, it could save some hassle to wait until you have it before applying for your first U.S. credit card, but "soon" in practice means within two to three weeks. If the SSA timeline stretches longer than that, the opportunity cost of waiting outweighs the administrative step of later updating your account number.

Starting with a secured card also gives you a concrete upgrade milestone to work toward. Most issuers review secured card accounts for an unsecured upgrade around the 12-month mark. By the time that review comes, your SSN will almost certainly be in hand and you will have a year of history behind you. See our [upgrade from secured to unsecured guide](/articles/upgrade-secured-to-unsecured-credit-card-itin) to understand what issuers look for at that review point.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I apply for a credit card while on OPT if I only have an ITIN, not an SSN?**
Yes. Several issuers, including Capital One and Discover, accept an ITIN in place of an SSN. Your EAD and ITIN together are enough to start a U.S. credit file. Once you receive an SSN, you can update your account and unlock more card options.

**Do I need an SSN to get a credit card on OPT, or will my ITIN work?**
An ITIN works at a growing number of issuers. OPT holders with an active EAD are eligible for an SSN from the Social Security Administration, but applying with an ITIN now means you can start building credit history immediately rather than waiting weeks.

**Which credit cards are easiest to get on OPT with an ITIN and no U.S. credit history?**
OpenSky Secured Visa requires no credit check and accepts an ITIN, making it the most accessible starting point. Capital One Platinum Secured and Discover it Secured are also strong options that report to all three bureaus and have upgrade paths to unsecured cards.

**Will my OPT expiration date hurt my credit card application?**
It can be a factor at some issuers who assess near-term visa expiration risk. Secured card products, however, focus on your income and deposit rather than visa end date, which largely removes this as a barrier.

**What documents do I need to apply for a credit card on OPT with an ITIN?**
Typically: your ITIN letter from the IRS, a valid foreign passport, your EAD, a U.S. mailing address, and proof of income such as an offer letter or pay stubs. Some issuers also require a U.S. bank account to fund a security deposit.
