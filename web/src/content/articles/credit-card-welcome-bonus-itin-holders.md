ITIN holders who have been approved for a credit card often wonder whether the welcome bonus printed on the card's offer page actually applies to them. It does, and this guide explains how those bonuses work, which cards offer the most valuable ones, and what to do if you don't receive a bonus you earned.

## Does my ITIN affect whether I can earn the welcome bonus?

No. The welcome bonus on a credit card is linked to your spending behavior after approval, not to the tax ID you used to apply.
An ITIN can be used as a substitute for a Social Security number with many credit card issuers,
and once your application is approved, you are treated as a full cardholder in every respect, including bonus eligibility.

The spending threshold and the time window (almost always 90 days from account opening) are the same for every new cardholder. Meet those conditions and the bonus posts to your account automatically. No extra steps, no separate form, nothing different because of your ITIN.

## Which ITIN-friendly cards actually offer a welcome bonus worth chasing?

*A question we hear often:* many readers get approved for a card and then discover the bonus is barely worth the effort. Here is a realistic breakdown by card tier.

Entry-level cards with no annual fee: The Chase Freedom Rise earns 1.5% cash back on all eligible purchases, and new cardholders can earn a $25 statement credit after signing up for automatic payments within the first three months. It's a modest offer, but it's the easiest ITIN-friendly bonus to reach.
You can use an ITIN to complete an online Chase credit card application.

Mid-tier cards:
Many of the top credit card issuers have rewards credit cards you can apply for using an ITIN, including Chase, American Express, and Capital One.
The Chase Freedom Unlimited, for example,
is a no-annual-fee card that earns cash back on everyday purchases and a welcome bonus.

Premium cards:
If you do not live in one of the countries supported by American Express, you can still apply for Amex credit cards with an SSN or ITIN.
Cards like the Amex Gold and Platinum carry large welcome bonuses in points, but they also require significantly higher minimum spend, usually $3,000-$6,000 in the first three months.

## What about secured cards? Do those come with a welcome bonus?

*This one comes up a lot:* ITIN holders who start with a secured card are often disappointed to learn that most secured products don't carry a traditional welcome bonus. The tradeoff is straightforward. A secured card is easier to get approved for because you back it with a deposit, and issuers offset the lower risk with fewer perks.

The notable exception is the Discover it Secured. Rather than a lump-sum bonus,
the Discover it Secured Credit Card establishes U.S. credit history under your ITIN,
and Discover automatically matches all the cash back you earn in your first 12 months. That match works as a delayed welcome bonus and can be worth $50-$150 depending on how much you spend.
For ITIN holders with established credit, the Discover it Cash Back is the typical next target.

The Capital One Quicksilver Secured earns 1.5% cash back on all eligible purchases and 5% cash back on hotels, vacation rentals, and car rentals booked through Capital One Travel, has no annual fee, but requires a minimum security deposit of $200
and carries no traditional welcome bonus.

## How do I actually meet the spending requirement?

*Readers frequently ask:* what counts toward the bonus threshold, and what doesn't?

Normal everyday purchases count: groceries, gas, subscriptions, utility bills if paid by card, and online shopping. Categories that typically don't count include balance transfers, cash advances, fees charged by the issuer, and purchases that are later returned or refunded.

The 90-day window starts on the account opening date, not the date you receive the card. If your card arrives a week after approval, you effectively have about 11 weeks of usable time. Plan your budget so that spending falls naturally within that window rather than forcing purchases. According to the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB), carrying a high balance to hit a minimum spend can cost more in interest than the bonus is worth, so aim to pay the statement in full each cycle.

Also worth knowing:
except in rare cases, you do not get a bonus just for opening a credit card account. It is a bonus you earn after you sign up by using the card for purchases.

## Can I earn a bonus on a business credit card with my ITIN?

Business cards are more complicated for ITIN holders.
Most flagship U.S. business cards with strong welcome bonuses are underwritten with the assumption that the applicant has a U.S. credit history tied to a Social Security Number. Non-residents generally do not have an SSN, and even with an ITIN, you may have no U.S. credit file at all.

That said, some business cards do work.
American Express Business charge cards such as the Gold and Platinum often have points-based welcome bonuses and require an EIN, U.S. bank activity, and sometimes an ITIN or SSN.
If you have an existing business with a U.S. checking account and EIN, you are in the best position to qualify. See our full guide at [business credit card with ITIN](/articles/business-credit-card-with-itin) for card-by-card details.

## What do I do if the bonus does not post?

Most welcome bonuses post automatically within one to two billing cycles after you meet the spend requirement. If yours doesn't appear, here is the sequence to follow:

1. Check your account portal or mobile app for a "rewards summary" or "offer tracker."
2. Confirm that your total net purchases (after any returns) met the minimum threshold.
3. Call the number on the back of your card and ask a representative to manually review your bonus eligibility.
4. If the representative confirms you met the requirement but the bonus still doesn't post,
federal law requires credit card providers to resolve billing errors within 90 days after receiving written notice, and providers must acknowledge the dispute within 30 days of receiving it.
A missing bonus can be escalated through this process.

Your ITIN status is not a valid reason for a card issuer to withhold a bonus you earned. If you receive that explanation, ask for the specific policy in writing and escalate to the issuer's customer relations team.

## Does earning a welcome bonus help build my credit history?

The bonus itself does not affect your credit score or your credit file. What does help your file is using the card to earn that bonus: regular purchases reported as on-time payments and low utilization each month.
Within 3 to 6 months of regular use, you may see a thin FICO file turn into a usable score in the low 600s or higher, though results vary.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I earn a credit card welcome bonus if I applied with an ITIN instead of an SSN?**
Yes. Once approved, your welcome bonus eligibility is identical to any other cardholder. The bonus is based on how much you spend in the required window, not on whether you used an ITIN or SSN to apply.

**Which credit cards with welcome bonuses accept an ITIN in 2026?**

Many of the top credit card issuers have rewards credit cards you can apply for using an ITIN, including Chase, American Express, and Capital One.
All three offer some form of welcome bonus or introductory offer on eligible cards.

**Do secured credit cards for ITIN holders come with welcome bonuses?**
Rarely. Most secured cards skip a traditional welcome bonus. The main exception is the Discover it Secured, which matches your first-year cash back dollar for dollar, functioning as a delayed first-year bonus.

**Does earning a welcome bonus affect my credit score?**
No. Meeting a spending requirement and receiving a bonus does not appear on your credit report and does not change your score. On-time payments and low utilization are what build your credit history.

**What is the minimum spending requirement for most welcome bonuses on ITIN-friendly cards?**
Entry-level ITIN-friendly cards typically require $500-$1,500 in purchases within the first three months. Premium cards like the Amex Gold or Capital One Venture X require $3,000-$6,000 within the same window.
